I. Open Source Community and Software Ecosystem Updates

1.1 RISC-V High-Assurance Cryptography (HAC) Specification Sees Intensive Updates: ML-KEM/ML-DSA Post-Quantum Cryptography Descriptions Refined (Top Story of the Day)

Date: 2026-08-15 (approx. 20 commits between 11:34 and 23:25 UTC)　Source: riscv/riscv-high-assurance-cryptography GitHub

The RISC-V International High-Assurance Cryptography (HAC) specification repository saw roughly 20 commits within the window, primarily focused on post-quantum cryptography and high-assurance implementations: revisions to instruction set descriptions for ML-KEM (Kyber post-quantum key encapsulation) and ML-DSA (Dilithium post-quantum signatures), wording unification in the ACE-ISA-unpriv specification (“must” changed to “shall”), streamlining of the OCB3 structure, clarification of CR representation and capacity limits, as well as handling review comments (review1.md) and AsciiDoc formatting fixes. HAC is RISC-V’s extension specification targeting FIPS 140-3-level high-assurance cryptographic implementations (constant-time, side-channel protection). Its ML-KEM/ML-DSA and other post-quantum instruction set descriptions entering an intensive revision phase signals that RISC-V cryptographic extensions are converging toward practical deployment and standardization—directly relevant to RISC-V entering the trust boundaries of finance/government/cloud, and echoing this week’s security trust discussions sparked by the Spectre paper.

II. Product and Ecosystem Developments

2.1 EE Times China Feature: How Dual-Core RISC-V RT-MCUs Underpin the Motion Control Foundation of Embodied Robots

Date: 2026-08-15 (09:00 CST)　Source: gnews link (EE Times China)

EE Times China published a feature examining the role of dual-core RISC-V RT-MCUs (real-time control MCUs) in the motion control foundation of embodied robots: the real-time core handles motor/joint control loops (current loop, position loop), while the application core handles communication/sensing/edge AI. This dual-core division of labor matches robots’ heterogeneous requirements of “real-time control + intelligent decision-making.” The representative product is the Fortior Technology FU75XX series (2nd-generation ME + 32-bit RISC-V dual-core MCU, positioned as “born for future robots” at its launch in 2025-11; the dual-motor drive model FU7512L was nominated for the 2026 China IC Design Achievement Award). This feature, together with the motion control chip signals from the Dishui Lake Forum earlier in this window (Gejian’s 500-nanosecond current loop, HPMicro HPM53M1 joint control, and SpacemiT K3 motion control positioning), forms part of the same narrative: RISC-V motion control MCUs are moving from “single-core real-time” to “dual-core heterogeneous.” See Chapter IV for a dedicated section.

IV. Special Chapter on Key Directions: Embodied Intelligence and Robotics

4.1 Dual-core RISC-V RT-MCU: the “heterogeneous division of labor” narrative for the motion control foundation takes shape

The most directional signal in this window is the EE Times China feature “How Do Dual-Core RISC-V RT-MCUs Support the Motion Control Foundation of Embodied Robots?” (08-15): the dual-core architecture (a real-time core running motor/joint control loops + an application core running communication/perception/edge AI) has become the mainstream design approach for the motion control foundation of embodied robots, with the representative product Fortior FU75XX series (32-bit RISC-V dual-core, dual-motor drive) positioned since 2025-11 as “born for future robots.” This is completely in sync with the motion control signals from the Dishui Lake Forum (08-13): Gejian Semiconductor’s 500-nanosecond current loop / single-chip 25 degrees of freedom (yesterday’s report 3.3), HPMicro HPM53M1 humanoid robot joint control, and SpacemiT K3 robot motion control positioning—domestic RISC-V motion control solutions are evolving from “single-core real-time DSP/MCU” to “dual-core heterogeneous (real-time + intelligent),” further concretizing the domestic substitution path at the motion control layer.

4.2 Edge-side AI inference NPU-ification: SPERT whole-graph kernel path

The SPERT whole-graph kernel path in the SpacemiT llama.cpp branch (3.1) and Sophgo’s production-grade YOLOv8-pose fixes (3.2) jointly point to the maturation of the embodied intelligence software stack: the former offloads LLM/VLA inference to the edge-side NPU engine (low-power real-time capability), while the latter continues to patch engineering defects in visual perception examples. Combined with yesterday’s LeRobot (SmolVLA/ACT) edge-side deployment and humanoid robot adaptation, the three-layer domestic path for RISC-V embodied intelligence—”perception (vision) — decision (edge-side LLM/VLA) — motion (dual-core RT-MCU motion control)”—is gradually becoming clear.

V. Summary and Trend Observations

  1. Post-quantum cryptography becomes a RISC-V specification hotspot: The official RISC-V HAC specification saw approximately 20 commits within the window refining descriptions of post-quantum cryptographic instructions such as ML-KEM/ML-DSA, with the cryptographic extension converging toward FIPS-level high-assurance implementations—aligned with the security trust discussion triggered by this week’s Spectre-on-RISC-V paper, security/cryptography is becoming an official priority topic in the RISC-V ecosystem.
  2. The “dual-core heterogeneous” narrative at the motion control layer takes shape: The EE Times China feature (dual-core RISC-V RT-MCU) + Fortior FU75XX + Dishui Lake Forum motion control new products (Gejian/HPMicro/K3) are in sync, with domestic RISC-V solutions for the embodied robot motion control foundation moving from “single-core real-time” to “real-time core + application core” dual-core division of labor.
  3. Edge-side LLM inference acceleration sinks to the NPU: SpacemiT’s llama.cpp merges the SPERT whole-graph kernel path, with edge-side large model inference moving from “running on CPU” toward “NPU whole-graph acceleration,” forming a complete chain with the operator library (FlagGems-RACE) and inference documentation (docs-ai).
  4. ESWIN’s IPO enters the “eve of listing”: The Hong Kong stock “first RISC-V architecture stock” narrative advances to the verge of listing; despite cumulative losses of 4.9 billion over three years, its automotive-grade MCU/CMS product line and the mainstream domestic RISC-V narrative endorse it.
  5. Verification infrastructure continues to ramp up: XiangShan difftest adds verifier backpressure control and batch verification (improving throughput) and adds macOS arm64 host support, making verification efficiency of open-source high-performance cores a focal point of competitiveness.
