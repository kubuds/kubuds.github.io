I. Open Source Community and Software Ecosystem

1.1 PoCL 7.2 Released, OpenCL 3.0 Official Conformance Lands on RISC-V and x86_64

Date: 2026-09-04　Source: Phoronix

The Portable Computing Language project PoCL has released version 7.2, becoming a Khronos Group-certified official OpenCL 3.0 conformant implementation, covering both RISC-V (RV64GC and RVA22-class hardware) and x86_64 (AVX-512 path, validated on AMD Ryzen 9 9900X) CPU platforms. The new version also supports LLVM 22 (including CUDA and Level Zero device backends) and CPU device targets up to LLVM 23, and follows up on a batch of new OpenCL extensions including cl_khr_extended_bit_ops, cl_khr_integer_dot_product, cl_khr_kernel_clock, and cl_khr_spirv_linkonce_odr. For the RISC-V ecosystem, this is the first time an open parallel computing standard has received official conformance endorsement on RISC-V general-purpose processors, marking a further step in the maturity of RISC-V’s heterogeneous computing software stack on the desktop and server side.

1.2 Echo Item: ServeTheHome Reposts Hot Chips 2026 SiFive Standards and Adoption Talk Transcript

Date: 2026-09-04　Source: ServeTheHome

ServeTheHome reposted its transcript of the SiFive “RISC-V Standards and Adoption” talk recorded during Hot Chips 2026. Yesterday’s (09-03) report already covered the same-topic repost from SiFive’s official blog; this piece is a same-source media echo with no substantive new content, and per deduplication policy is not detailed separately here; it only reinforces one signal—the talk’s closing remarks stated that the first batch of RVA23 server chips will land this year, with standards work translating into real hardware, corroborating the advancement of SiFive’s server platform specification (RVA23 + AIA interrupts + UEFI/ACPI + MCTP/Redfish management).

II. Product and Ecosystem Developments

2.1 XenoSpectrum Analysis: China’s Server Market Still 74% x86, Why Beijing Bets on RISC-V

Date: 2026-09-04　Source: XenoSpectrum (translated from Japanese original)

Japanese media outlet XenoSpectrum published a long-form article analyzing China’s RISC-V strategy from both market data and policy logic: on one hand, about 74% of the server market is still held by x86, with a gap between reality and the perception that “RISC-V has already spread”; on the other hand, Beijing continues to bet through a combination of Xinchuang catalogs, state-owned cloud, and procurement subsidies. The article criticizes how “RISC-V shipment share” figures circulating in the industry are often cited repeatedly stripped of their time point and product category context, causing diffusion-driven exaggeration; and using research showing RISC-V E203 (0.145mW) is about 15% more power-efficient than Arm Cortex-M0 (0.17mW) as an example, it points out that such isolated-case conclusions should not be generalized. The article has reference value for assessing the “expectation vs. reality gap” in domestic RISC-V server narratives, and can be seen as a demystifying supplement to recent “China RISC-V policy fever” coverage.

III. Domestic Team Updates and Deep Dive

3.1 Zhongke Haoxin Completes Series B Funding, Betting on AI Edge RISC-V DSP

Date: 2026-09-03/04　Source: STAR Market Daily (republished by East Money), PEdaily

Beijing Zhongke Haoxin (a technology transfer company of the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and a specialized supplier of RISC-V-based digital signal processors) has recently completed a Series B funding round from Yuehu Capital, with proceeds focused on R&D for AI edge chip products and industry-customized solutions. The company’s self-developed Haawking HX2000 series DSP has achieved scaled deployment, covering AI smart home appliances, industrial control, new energy vehicles, photovoltaic energy storage, and embodied intelligence, targeting domestic substitution of high-end DSPs. Its team has been engaged in RISC-V processor research since 2016, building a full-chain self-developed system spanning cores, architecture, and its own toolchain (Haawking IDE). Notably, the sector is heating up: fellow player Liuyue Microelectronics just completed a RMB 500 million Series A round in July 2026 (with participation from over ten institutions including Yueke Financial and Guangzhou Industrial Control), as capital accelerates into the RISC-V DSP crossover sector of “domestic substitution + embodied intelligence.”

3.2 Espressif ESP32-S31 Dev Board Hands-On: Dual-Core RISC-V 320MHz + Wi-Fi 6

Date: 2026-09-04　Source: Geeky Gadgets (Google News aggregation), Espressif official product page

Geeky Gadgets published a hands-on review of the Espressif ESP32-S31 dev board (40-pin header + dual USB-C). The ESP32-S31 is a dual-core 32-bit RISC-V SoC in Espressif’s S series: 320MHz clock, one core with a 128-bit-wide data path and SIMD instructions, plus an MMU, 512KB SRAM, and 250MHz 8-bit DDR PSRAM (supporting concurrent access with flash), with up to 60 GPIOs. On the wireless side it integrates 2.4GHz Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.4 (LE Audio/direction finding/Mesh 1.1), and IEEE 802.15.4 (Thread/Zigbee), offering dual Wi-Fi and Thread paths for Matter protocol support. The company says it has entered mass production and is available for purchase, backed by the full ESP-IDF, ESP-Matter, and ESP-GMF stack, and can also serve as a connectivity co-processor via ESP-Hosted/ESP-AT. Target scenarios are smart home hubs, smart speakers, edge AI, and industrial automation. Following the C series (C2/C3/C5/C6/C61, RISC-V) and the P4, Espressif is bringing RISC-V main control further into the S series product line; note that the S31 differs architecturally from the earlier Xtensa-core S2/S3, and coverage should not conflate the two.

IV. Special Chapter on Embodied Intelligence and Robotics

4.1 Window Observation: No Major New Robot Products; RISC-V Embodied Narrative Carried by Funding and RL Toolchain

Date: 2026-09-04/05

No new RISC-V robot systems or embodied-specific chips were released during the window. Two related peripheral developments: first, Zhongke Haoxin’s Series B funding explicitly lists “embodied intelligence” as one of the scaled application scenarios for its HX2000 DSP (see 3.1), with proceeds directed at AI edge chips, indicating that industrial-control-class RISC-V processors are expanding their narrative radius via embodied intelligence; second, SpacemiT’s model_zoo_rl added a raw policy action export interface (see 3.3), continuing its investment in robotics reinforcement learning model toolchains. Also note: the Petoi Quaddle mini robot dog (crowdfunded) covered by CNX Software uses an ESP32-S3 (Xtensa core) as its main controller, not a RISC-V platform, and is excluded per reporting criteria. The industry mainline continues yesterday’s cited discussion of “humanoid robot compute moving toward hybrid centralized and distributed architectures” and the industrial narrative of “the inaugural year of embodied intelligence mass production.”

V. Summary and Trend Observations

  1. Software ecosystem: PoCL 7.2 gives RISC-V CPUs their first official OpenCL 3.0 conformance certification, providing another substantive endorsement of the combination of open parallel computing standards and RISC-V, benefiting desktop/server-side heterogeneous computing stacks.
  2. Capital: Zhongke Haoxin’s Series B has landed (Yuehu Capital), and together with Liuyue Microelectronics’ RMB 500 million Series A in July, RISC-V DSP has become a capital-intensive sector under the narrative of “high-end domestic substitution + AI edge + embodied intelligence.”
  3. Products: Hands-on reviews of the Espressif ESP32-S31 (dual-core RISC-V 320MHz + Wi-Fi 6 + 802.15.4) dev board are beginning to roll out, with mass production availability and the ESP-IDF/Matter/voice ecosystem ready in tandem, deepening RISC-V penetration in consumer AIoT main control.
  4. Narrative: A XenoSpectrum long-form piece cautions that figures like “RISC-V server share” are often cited out of context regarding time and category; the signal that “the first RVA23 servers will land within the year” coexists with the reality that “the server market remains 74% x86,” and volume ramp-up in the high-end market still needs time to verify.
  5. Note on the quiet day: Domestic teams’ GitHub activity was limited to routine maintenance, with no new products in the robotics direction during the window; this issue is dominated by software ecosystem milestones and funding events.
