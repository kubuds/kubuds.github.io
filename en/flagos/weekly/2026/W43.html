FlagOS Weekly Report (2026-10-19 ~ 2026-10-23)

2026-10-23 Kubuds

Reporting period: October 19, 2026 (Monday) to October 23 (Friday), 5 working days in total.

Sources: 5 issues of this publication’s daily FlagOS update reports (10-19 / 10-20 / 10-21 / 10-22 / 10-23, with no missing issues). Among them, the 10-19 issue is the Monday morning report, whose research window starts from the previous issue’s cutoff at 10-16 06:00 (approximately 72 hours, covering the entire “2026 AI Open Computing Conference & Zhongzhi FlagOS Technology Conference” held from 10-17 to 10-18 and the Liangzhiyun launch); the four issues from 10-20 to 10-23 each cover a 24-hour window, running back-to-back, with the final issue’s last shift covering 10-22 06:00 to 10-23 06:00. Therefore, the factual range of this report is 10-16 to 10-22, with the 10-16 to 10-18 portion labeled according to the original dates.

Compilation notes: Covers five dimensions: versions and releases, component and code co-building, member units and vendor adaptation, ecosystem and community, and trend observations. The five daily reports, accumulated by their respective windows, total 654 commits and 661 merges; if only the four complete 24-hour windows are counted (the merged days from 10-19 to 10-22), the totals are 507 commits and 522 merges. The number of repositories with pushes in each issue is 16, 16, 19, 22, and 16 respectively, out of a total of 61 organization repositories. Versions and tags were independently verified against each component repository’s atomic source (releases.atom), consistent with the daily reports’ methodology.

I. Weekly Highlights

  • The conference wrapped up over two days, and the scale of 2.2 was for the first time broken into four verifiable layers (10-17 ~ 10-18): In the main forum, Lin Yonghua, Vice President and Chief Engineer of the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI), presented under the title “Zhongzhi FlagOS 2.2: From the Token Economy to Physical AI” — 21 chip vendors and 35 chips, 9 major operator libraries totaling 1,454 operators (an increase of 890 over the previous version), cross-chip operator performance up 27.6% (covering 10 chips), and 264 code contributors (up 55% quarter-over-quarter). On the community side, there are also more than 730 active contributors, more than 100 member organizations, coverage of more than 30 countries, and more than 55,800 developers; merged PRs from 2.1 to 2.2 rose from 1,890 to 4,890, new issues from 420 to 1,423, and commits from 5,100 to 9,730. On the same day, the cross-chip debugging and performance analysis tool FlagPrism and the unified quantization tool FlagOS-Compressor were released. Reports state that 2.2 was released on September 30, whereas the repository manifest was actually committed between 09-28 and 09-29; the two-day discrepancy is handled in the body text according to the repository timeline.

  • Quantum-AI Cloud was publicly launched, extending the FlagOS execution stack to hybrid quantum-classical scheduling (10-18): The Quantum-AI Fusion sub-forum included a “Quantum-AI Cloud Platform Launch” session. Quantum-AI Cloud was jointly launched by BAAI and the Beijing Academy of Quantum Information Sciences, supporting FlagQuantum through the FlagOS Execution Stack and interfacing with the “Jiuding” AI computing system and the “QUAFU” quantum computer to enable hybrid quantum-classical job scheduling. The code side corroborates this: the release notes for FlagQuantum v0.3.0 already state that Jiuding and Quafu tasks are resumable and that Quafu multi-circuit results preserve order, indicating that at release the task submission, receipt, and result-ordering pipeline was already inside the package rather than merely a framework interface. The boundaries are equally clear — it does not run on quantum hardware, does not include an MCP server or agent runtime, and hardware execution is handled by the Jiuding and QUAFU sides.

  • The 2.3 cycle saw its first same-day loop from “requirement to evidence” (10-21 ~ 10-22): On 10-21, two FEPs were merged — FEP-0140 defines three version lines for vllm-plugin-FL (0.24 completing five vendors, 0.28 as the November main release supporting ten vendors, and 0.32 introducing five vendors), while FEP-0141 locks in the ten-vendor matrix for SGLang 0.5.18 and the DeepSeek-V4 W8A8 target; on 10-22, Hygon completed alignment with vLLM 0.28.0 and SGLang 0.5.18 on the same day in both the vllm-plugin-FL and sglang-plugin-FL repositories (84 files in total, a net increase of about 5,700 lines). From “writing the matrix into the document” to “the first measured pass” took only one day — the fastest requirement-to-evidence loop this quarter.

  • The delivery side closed out six backends to seven within the same week and turned the communication library from a special case into the general rule (10-16 ~ 10-22): build-infra first landed one self-contained 22.04 builder each for FlagCX’s six backends — nvidia, hygon, cambricon, metax, enflame, and iluvatar — lowering the glibc floor from 2.38 to 2.35; on 10-19 it migrated the four Ascend wheels from the 24.04 runtime into the same form and added Tsingmicro and Xiwang; on 10-21 it onboarded Tsingmicro and Moore Threads as FlagTree’s fifth and sixth backends; on 10-22 Kunlunxin became the seventh backend. On the same day, flagcx==0.14.0 was extended from being pinned only on the two nvidia lines to all twenty backends, and a one-click install script was added to condense multi-repository dependencies into a single command.

  • Four infrastructure items were completed on the same day, shifting organizational capacity toward “how to test, how to install, how to share” (10-20): optest received its first substantive commit since the repository was created (PR #1, 38 files), containing 12 backend configurations and hardware probes for 13 vendors; FlagOps elevated FlagGems’ five CI actions into organization-level shared resources; build-infra added a one-click install script (1 file, +308); Torch-FL and TransformerEngine-FL introduced the unified FlagCICD entry point on the same day with support for L0 to L2 tiers, and FlagScale added selective platform CI. Testing, actions, installation, and the CI entry point — all four lines each completed a structural merge within the same window.

  • The quantum stack advanced from design contract to runnable path (10-21 ~ 10-22): On 10-21, FlagQuantum saw 84 commits and 34 merges, downgrading dynamic circuits to “successor qubit plus Boolean SSA results” and landing this in the Program IR, while making exact downgrade and restoration a mandatory prerequisite for deployment; on 10-22, a further 53 commits and 20 merges turned the M3 neutral atom from two design documents into an executable path — a native Rydberg frontend on the Core side (PR #700), plan 1.7 dynamics on the Simulation side (#707), and moving-atom pulse training on the Examples side (#714, with unconditional RR rising from 0.0431 to 0.1228 in a 40-step, 128-interval measured run).

  • Merge volume hit a new high during the week, but the bulk of the increment was test contracts rather than operators (10-22): The day’s 163 merges were the highest in the past week, of which FlagGems alone accounted for 91, or 56% of the whole organization; at the same time, commit volume fell 18% and pushing repositories decreased 27%, indicating digestion of a backlog rather than expansion. The testing mainline was carried by lukatao alone with 61 commits (67% of that repository), opening 10 batch PRs within seven minutes from 23:40 to 23:47, changing test cases from wrapper functions to directly calling operators or injecting candidates. For the full week, KernelGen’s new operators were concentrated in three batches of merges plus the tail end of the sparse family, with genuinely new capability accounting for less than 20%.

  • Three information increments from outside the repositories appeared on the ecosystem side (10-19 ~ 10-22): China.com published the first full-stack technical breakdown after the conference (adding seven quantization cases and grouped evidence); The Beijing News and the Beijing Daily app carried out a second wave of industry-version dissemination, adding a citable figure of “a new model completing adaptation for 12 chips within one day”; Pandaily systematically relayed 2.2 in English, the first complete article aimed at overseas readers. In addition, the Beijing Municipal Science & Technology Commission and the Zhongguancun Administrative Committee published a pre-approval notice for the 2026 Zhongzhi FlagOS Acceleration Program, in which Kuangwei Zhixin’s “On-device Deployment and Application Demonstration of the AlohaMini Embodied Intelligent Robot Based on FlagOS-Robo” was selected — the first external project with deliverable constraints to appear in the embodied intelligence direction.

FlagTree: Release Cadence Sliced by Hardware Version

  • Four build lines coexist: the 0.7.1 line now has four hardware build branches—ascend3.5, iluvatar3.6, xpu3.6, ppu3.6—with the base line moving from a1 to dev1. The release notes only say “add version number to pypi,” indicating automated nightly builds rather than feature releases; whether a release has occurred must be judged from the build line, not from version-number semantics.
  • The backend matrix expanded to seven rows this week: SpacemiT TX8 (10-21, fifth), Moore Threads MUSA (10-21, sixth), and Kunlunxin XPU (10-22, seventh) were successively integrated into the FlagTree build line and daily build rows. The Kunlunxin row has five unique constraints: ten precompiled inputs; caches matched by directory name and LLVM 19 packages sharing two tarball roots; frontend selected by environment variable; pybind11 pinned exactly to 3.0.1 (3.0.4 causes the dispatcher to SIGSEGV); and no Release build flags (the vendor wheel itself is rel-with-asserts).
  • Compiler-side feature increments are few and concentrated: AMD RDNA advanced on 10-21 from single-cooperative large-core decode (M=1) to prefill (M>1, PR #1365, 66 files); the RDNA prefill WMMA GEMM microkernel is 10 to 30 times faster than the GEMV loop when M>1, and on the decode side split-K cut lm_head from 17.0 to 14.9 ms/token and per-layer wide projections from 14.9 to 10.5 ms/token; MUSA added per-copy TME cache persistence (#1385); the SpaceMiT RISC-V backend added the smt_rvisa bare-metal eDSL and retired the private TLE dialect (#1381, 10-16, within the Monday window).

FlagGems: 5.4 Is the Formal Line, No New Additions on the 5.5 Development Line

  • Formal-line status unchanged: 5.4 remains the current formal line (released 09-28); 5.5.0.dev0 opened on 10-12 and had no new tags this week, with package version metadata still showing 0.1.0—the Hygon vLLM 0.28 acceptance record gathered evidence by “comparing all 1,944 installed source files one by one against commit e12a220, metadata version untrustworthy,” and this practice of substituting content for VCS metadata appeared again this week.
  • Operator coverage enters the wrap-up phase: this week KernelGen merged 14 Nvidia operators in three batches, all boundary operators for aliasing, views, and sparse semantics. The 10:43 batch covered copy, sparse construction, and nested-tensor table construction (ger, sparse_bsc_tensor); the 15:23 batch covered value and shape views, plus nested-tensor storage offsets and csr-to-coo sparse index conversion (_values_copy, _shape_as_tensor); the 22:23 batch covered dimension reordering and complex views (swapdims, moveaxis, view_as_complex_copy). On the review side, roughly 20 sparse-family PRs (_sparse_mm series, copy_sparse_to_sparse_, coo and csr index conversion, etc.) were opened in one go on the night of 10-19.
  • The leading signal for a version release is in build-infra: the vllm-plugin-native wheel added nvidia, metax, kunlunxin, and hygon backends on 10-21 and registered a release; on 10-22 it uniformly pinned flagcx==0.14.0 for all backends—all artifact preparation ahead of the 2.3 formal release.

FlagGems (290 Merges): Test Contracts Become the Primary Theme

  • Three Rounds of Revert and Rebuild: On 10-20, PR #7361 first reverted the earlier shared test-infrastructure changes (17 files +599/−1040), pushing compatibility handling down into each operator’s own tests; 22 subsequent fixes all followed this direction—input snapshots in CPU reference mode, tensor view geometry, complex and half-precision value comparison, sparse construction and conversion, generated-case executability, assertion boundaries, pytest marker normalization. This was an engineering correction that “first acknowledged the shared approach was unworkable, then filled in operator by operator.”
  • lukatao’s Three-Part Test Case Overhaul: On 10-21, 35 commits converted generated cases to run cross-backend (split and squeeze, adjoint and mH and mT, as_strided, moveaxis and reshape_as and swapaxes, permutation views, integer and half-precision conversion), with #7425 through #7440 being sixteen consecutive commits; on 10-22, another 61 commits converted foreach overrides to “direct routing” batches 01 through 09 and moved the last nine operator suites to the common candidate API—making “which implementation is tested” explicit rather than implicit.
  • Backend Skip Governance Begins Documenting Reasons: Ascend and Moore Threads skips were individually scoped by input domain (linalg_vander complex input, logdet complex, row_stack_out memory format, trapz complex32, orgqr MUSA zero columns, the qint32 series, Hygon cuDNN validation), and four sparse suite ports were merged consecutively in the early hours of 10-23 (sparse log-softmax cross-backend portability, sparse validator firmware and direct benchmark candidate invocation, compressed sparse validator, common sparse block constructor).
  • Two Large Vendor-Side Commits: Enflame’s bulk update #7077 (26 files +3520/−511, titled only “update from enflame 20261013,” with no itemized details); Kunlunxin’s fused MoE #7107 (13 files), adding three fused_experts variants and dispatch_fused_moe_kernel, with aggregate score 1.324 to 1.329, and proactively declaring the baseline as an alternative implementation and dispatch_fused_moe_kernel’s 0.626 as substandard.
  • Kunlunxin Optimization Line: 9 commits on 10-20, 17 on 10-21, 13 on 10-22, shifting from adding implementations to gating by precision and shape—w8a8_block_fp8_matmul gains FP8 capability gating, fp64 and grouped-MM switch to capability-based skips with documented reasons, single-layer GRU cells switch to a flat 1D kernel raising the aggregate score from 0.4217 to 0.8253, and sgn’s large-numel path switches to a cheap main body plus configurable 1D tiling.

FlagQuantum (82 Merges): From Contract to Code, Then From Code to Rejection

  • Dynamic Circuits Enter the IR (10-21): PR #681 defines a typed dynamic Program IR contract; #683 lowers mid-circuit measurement to “successor qubit plus Boolean SSA result,” reset to an explicit successor qubit, and conjunction/disjunction to canonical DNF over active Boolean values, restoring only when the lowered result matches the full function graph verbatim; #694 makes exact lowering and restoration a mandatory prerequisite for dynamic deployment, halting unverified programs before calling any provider.
  • M3 Neutral Atoms From Contract to Runnable (10-22): On the Core side, #700 exposes a frontend for native Rydberg control directly on ProgramRecord (RydbergProgram provides three actions—move, pulse, to_ir—and explicitly states “a controlled position does not represent physical presence”); on the Simulation side, #707 routes native Rydberg programs through the existing Lindblad plan, with plan 1.7 sealed to exactly seven outer fields; on the Examples side, #714 trains two pulse amplitudes and moves atom pairs, with 40 steps over 128 intervals measuring unconditional RR rising from 0.0431 to 0.1228, survival-conditioned RR 0.1500, and no-flag selection RR 0.1439.
  • Half of Merges Spent on Rejection and Deletion: On 10-22, #701 makes native physics programs uniformly throw CapabilityError instead of AttributeError at packaging time; #705, per Proposal 082, directly deletes NeutralAtomProvider and two Remote exports that never had a corresponding external service, leaving no aliases and no empty shells; #716 proposes Proposal 083 defining the Runtime’s sample_terminal primitive and prints “proposed, not implemented” at the end of the example. Such actions add no capability but determine whether M3’s boundaries are credible.
  • Plotting and Citability Chain Completed: On 10-21, #668 adds a lazy LaTeX backend and an “encoder—circuit—measurement head” hybrid model view (43 files +14311), with Notebooks preferring SVG display; #684 adds CITATION.cff (version 0.3.1, release date 2026-10-16, Apache-2.0); #687 inventories reusable neutral-atom capabilities and missing contracts, providing independent numerical references of physical survival 0.8187, unconditional RR 0.4094, and survival-conditioned RR 0.5.
  • Compute Expansion: On 10-20, #653 and #661 migrate the CPU suite to eight A800 runners with automatic GPU test triggering, and long tasks switch to parallel—the advancement method from here on is large-scale consistency validation rather than feature rollout.

  • FlagTree Build Line: The builder merges from “two workflows doing one thing” into a single matrix, with one line in backends.yaml carrying the Containerfile, image repo, runner label, disk budget, and acceptance assertions. Tsingmicro, Moore Threads, and Kunlunxin rows land on the same template, differing only in prebuilt tree and toolchain resolution paths.
  • Installation Path Convergence: On 10-20, #1425 adds a one-click install script (1 file +308), converging for the first time the installation of “multiple repos, multiple dependency sets” into a single command; #1435 restores the vllm native extension wheel for the CUDA distribution backend and points out the pitfall that PEP 440 ignores build tags and short pins install the wrong backend.
  • Build Docs Become a Site: On 10-22, twelve commits (#1462 through #1470) merge design records and site pages for eight components—vllm-plugin-FL, FlagTree, FlagCX, FlagGems, FlagLibs, Megatron, SGLang, verl—consecutively within five hours, the first time this season build design has been written into searchable content.

Torch-FL (53 Merges): Platform Expansion Followed by Item-by-Item Completion

  • MPS Line Completes Its Main Body: Eight MPS-related merges across 10-19 to 10-21—native mm and bmm into the MPSGraph gemm class, softmax and elementwise neg and pow nativized, compiled MPSGraph plan caching, queued-work keepalive and native masked_fill, native MPSGraph kernels for Qwen3.5, advanced indexing collection on Metal; on 10-20, #566 lands with 69 files +22929/−236, CI uses GitHub-hosted Apple Silicon arm64 runners, and explicitly states “x86_64 macOS runners have no Metal device, so environment check failure is expected.”
  • Tsingmicro Triple: torch.compile lands on TX8 (#541, 14 files, author explicitly notes device property queries can only return constants rather than measured hardware values), host-side random number kernels (#604), device timeline into torch.profiler (#630, DeviceTracer based on rocprofiler-sdk, measured on TX8110).
  • Two Hard Fixes for Multi-Card Correctness: Kunlunxin’s CrossDeviceMemcpy previously called the same-card-semantics xpu_memcpy, passing pointers from two cards returned −801 with the destination unwritten, silently invalidating any inter-card copy; on 10-22 it switches to xpu_memcpy_peer (#634); liblaunch_shared.so’s undefined symbol: cudaMemcpy can only be resolved after the Triton launcher dlopen, so Kunlunxin’s CUDA compatibility runtime is preloaded (#632). These two commits produce no performance numbers but require recalculating conclusions from all prior multi-card experiments.
  • CI and Platform Line: BI-V150 shared CI line (#569, 24 files, allowing initial runs to retain known failures to complete bring-up), FlagCICD unified entry with L0 to L2 tiering (#599), AGENTS.md designated as the sole agent guide (#601), Kunlunxin P800’s 2.9 stable branch line (#273), platform pytest results reported to FlagCICD (#542 and #577).

  • This Week’s FlagTree Merges Are Almost Entirely on the “Backend Times Version Times Workflow” Three-Dimensional Matrix: Enflame enflame3.6-gcu300’s FlagGems baseline and test workflows, Enflame vLLM benchmarks into workflows, AMD amd3.6 baseline and tests, Ascend 3.5 py312 delivery and fixes, triton_v3.5.x branch codeowner updates, XPU3.6’s vllm benchmark workflow.

  • Interface With RISC-V: smt_rvisa lets authors explicitly specify SEW, VL, tiling, and instruction selection, with kernels lowered through the C++ builder API, plus an LLVM direct-emit path that skips spine-opt entirely; the backend no longer keeps a private TLE dialect copy, instead consuming the common third_party/tle. Such changes appear in no external messaging yet determine whether the RVV backend can go from “runs” to “squeezes out performance.”

IV. Member Units and Vendor Adaptation

  • Kunlunxin: The backend with the highest engineering density this week, and it is not on the public member unit list. Approximately 40 attributable merges (nine on the 10-20 merge date, eighteen on 10-21, thirteen on 10-22; the 10-19 window was not counted separately), spanning three layers: operators, build, and runtime. On the operator side, work shifted from filling in implementations to gating by precision and shape (FP8 capability gating, skipping fp64 and grouped-MM by capability, GRU flattened 1D kernel aggregation score from 0.4217 to 0.8253, fused MoE three-operator from 1.324 to 1.329); on the build side, it became the seventh FlagTree backend; on the runtime side, two hard defects were fixed — silent failure of cross-card copies and preload ordering. The risk is on the operator side — most changes are another tile or fusion of the same operator, there is no unified performance gate, and aggregation scores are not comparable across vendors because of baseline substitution.
  • Hygon: The only vendor this week to complete upstream version alignment in two inference plugin repos simultaneously. vLLM 0.28.0 and SGLang 0.5.18 were merged on the same day (84 files total, net increase of about 5,700 lines), validated on a single-card 64 GiB BW1000 cluster; the vLLM side ships release package 0.28.0+empty, while the SGLang side fixes pipeline-parallel coordinated exit for both physical two-node TP4/PP1 and TP2/PP2. Supporting work: FlagBLAS six-precision GEMM and L1, FlagGems FP8 sparse sum backward firmware, and one hygon fix in FlagDNN. Its reusable value lies in writing the empty-backend upgrade process as a template with a version table.
  • Qingwei Intelligent: Everything added this week addresses infrastructure gaps rather than operator coverage — torch.compile landed on TX8 (#541, device properties can only return constants), host-side random number kernels (#604), device timeline into torch.profiler (#630, measured on TX8110, also pinning the vendor build switch), and FlagTree build lines integrated and hooked to the daily line (#1436, #1440). The order is worth noting: first buildable in CI, then reproducible randomness, then observable performance, and only then optimization.
  • Ascend: The main line this week is versions and graph execution in the two plugin repos — vLLM 0.24 full decode graph execution plus Qwen3.6-27B and 35B-A3B text and image inference (#606), SGLang 0.5.18 standalone adaptation for 910C (#131), W8A16 FP8 TopK (#6030), fp32 capability gating for matmul by SoC (#616), and four wheels migrated to the 22.04 builder (build-infra #1414, glibc floor lowered from 2.38 to 2.35). On the test side, a dozen-plus changes cover FP8 input construction, tile block errors, and reference docs, still mostly fixes.

Five test skips are concentrated on this backend’s input-domain limits, indicating that failures are still mainly “input unsupported” rather than implementation errors.

On the FlagTree side, the 02-sparse-mla case was disabled due to an intermittent bus error and the vllm benchmark parameters were updated; stability issues remain at the test-case layer.

Process standardization lags behind Torch-FL and FlagQuantum.

VI. Trend Observations

  • Unified delivery surface precedes feature expansion: The heaviest batch of work this week was not new operators, but the glibc baseline, builder form factor, communication dependencies, and installation commands—the 22.04 self-contained builder consolidated from six backends to seven, flagcx==0.14.0 expanded from two nvidia lines to twenty backends, the one-click install script reduced multi-repo dependencies to a single command, and optest turned test infrastructure into an installable wheel. The common effect of these moves is “no more editing install scripts when switching backends,” which more directly determines 2.3’s delivery speed than any single-point performance gain.
  • The loop from requirement to evidence was compressed to one day this week: The two FEPs merged on 10-21 wrote the version-by-vendor matrix, and on 10-22 Hygon lit up the first cell; likewise, after optest and FlagCICD landed on 10-20, the FlagGems test-case rework on 10-22 immediately pivoted to “direct-connect candidates.” The remaining nine vendors and two version lines for 2.3 are the main workload for the next six weeks; the thing to watch is whether the same 0.28.0 reuses Hygon’s empty backend template on SpacemiT, MetaX, Enflame, and Kunlunxin.
  • The quality-over-quantity orientation has stabilized: Of the 661 merges for the week, fewer than 20% actually added operator capability; the bulk was test contracts (at least 119 from a single person), automated output (three KernelGen batches of 14 operators plus the sparse family under review), and build infrastructure. This is consistent with the post-2.2 “measure existing capability accurately first” orientation; the cost is slower operator-count growth and slower updates to external performance numbers.
  • The quantum stack has formed a replicable mode of advance: write the contract as a rejectable implementation first, then open it up block by block: expand the subpackage while locking the core (159 new public names into the subpackage, Stable Core’s 41 exports unchanged), make deployment pre-checks mandatory gates, remove vendors with no external service, and freeze unapproved sampling interfaces. Half of this week’s merges went into rejection and deletion, which determines whether M3’s subsequent boundaries are credible; the risk is that all 20 to 34 merges were done by a single account, and verification of physical semantics depends on the same people writing tests and implementations, leaving no independent external review.
  • Observability and multi-card correctness have become the new weak spots for multi-backend: SpacemiT got a device timeline for the first time, and Kunlunxin’s cross-card copy had been wrong all along (bytes were never written; synchronous calls would not mask it)—both surfaced only after “it runs.” The past two weeks’ test porting and test-case contract rework, and this week’s optest centralization, are essentially different expressions of the same judgment: after “it runs,” the first hurdle for multi-backend is reproducibility, not speed.
  • Three risk items need confirmation in the next issue: First, “whole-batch update” commits lack verifiable detail (Enflame’s 26 files, +3520/−511, cannot be mapped to specific operators and gains), and Kunlunxin’s fused MoE was measured against a substituted baseline—both will distort subsequent cross-vendor comparisons. Second, Horizon and Cambricon have been silent for two consecutive days; if this continues next week, it should be called out separately in the weekly report. Third, on the training side (Megatron-LM-FL, FlagScale), pushes were sparse this week, and the three NPU PRs for TransformerEngine-FL have not yet merged; the training stack is lagging the inference stack in the 2.3 cycle.
  • Next things to watch: whether the FlagTree 0.7.1 development build goes GA before 2.3; whether the second vendor for vLLM 0.28 (SpacemiT or MetaX) reuses the Hygon template; when FlagCV lands its first runnable operator; whether per-session notes from the conference sub-forums and workshops are published; and when Liangzhi Cloud opens to the public.
