FlagOS Weekly Report (2026-11-02 ~ 2026-11-06)

2026-11-06 Kubuds

Reporting period: Monday, November 2, 2026 through Friday, November 6, 2026 — 5 working days in total.

Sources: 5 issues of this publication’s daily FlagOS update reports (11-02 / 11-03 / 11-04 / 11-05 / 11-06; no issues missing within the period). The 11-02 issue is the Monday morning report, whose research window starts from the previous issue’s cutoff at 10-30 06:00 — a 72-hour window covering the weekend of 10-31 and 11-01; the four issues from 11-03 to 11-06 use 24-hour windows that run back-to-back, with the final window spanning 11-05 06:00 to 11-06 06:00. Accordingly, the factual interval of this report is 10-30 to 11-05, with the 10-30 to 11-01 portion labeled by its original dates.

Editorial note: Organized across six dimensions: top stories of the week, versions and releases, component and code co-building, member organizations and vendor enablement, ecosystem and community, and trend observations. Summed across their respective windows, the five daily reports total 1,117 commits and 715 merges; counting only the four complete 24-hour windows (11-03 to 11-06), the totals are 861 commits and 600 merges. The number of repositories with pushes per issue was 17, 16, 24, 26, and 21 respectively, out of 61 organization repositories in total. Versions and tags were independently re-verified against the tags and Release atoms of each component repository; no new Release objects were created during the period.

I. Weekly Highlights

  • Within a week, the 2.3.0 image layer expanded from four tiers to six; all 23 base and 23 runtime images are now tagged 2.3.0, yet the release manifest still contains only the schedule table (10-31 to 11-05): New coverage includes Hygon DTK 26.10 base and runtime, AMD ROCm 7.2.4 runtime and the FlagTree amd build line, Ascend CANN 9.2.1 base (only 910B, python 3.12, torch 2.13, triton_ascend 3.6.0, with FlagTree and FlagCX deliberately left empty), and NVIDIA cuda13.3 (torch 2.13.0+cu130, triton 3.7.1, while cuda12.8 is frozen with enabled set to false). The release/2.3 directory in the community repo contains only the Chinese and English schedule tables, and release-2.3.yaml is still 404; the atomic check of Releases across 31 repos and Tags across 8 repos showed zero additions all week. Per the schedule, 11-13 is the FEP merge deadline, 11-30 is feature freeze with rc0 cut, 12-07 is rc1, 12-14 is rc2, 12-25 is final freeze, and 12-30 is GA.

  • vLLM 0.28.0 was closed out by four backends within a single day, and the next day a repack rolled it out to 11 vendor indexes (11-03 to 11-04): Tsingmicro TXDA implements eager only and actively rejects graph mode (#642, +3067/−146, 29 files); MetaX C550 does a “0.28-empty” stub adaptation despite no corresponding upstream branch (#554, +2838/−70, 36 files); Moore Threads MUSA brings in native MCCL and segmented native graphs in one go (#575, +7804/−233, 46 files); Ascend builds a FULL_DECODE_ONLY decode-specific graph (#646, +3342/−340, 39 files).   • Cambricon MLU enters Torch-FL as a native platform; the next day fused attention forward/backward is filled in and CPU fallbacks in a Qwen3 training step drop from 190 to zero (11-04 to 11-05): #720, +4870/−64 across 40 files, writes cambricon into flagos_platforms.json and fills in the libcnrt runtime layer, the torch.mlu shim, backends_cambricon.conf, CI configuration, and the platform contract; the whole stack runs on stock torch 2.10.0+cpu, without torch_mlu, without FlagTree, and without a CUDA runtime, and was tested end to end on eight MLU590-M9 cards. The next day, #748 and #749 wire CNNL fused attention forward and backward back into SDPA coverage and wrap them as a C++ autograd Function; #757 runs a fallback census on a full Qwen3 causal language model training step, finding that of 190 cpu_fallback calls, 188 come from aten::mul.out and 2 from aten::embedding_dense_backward; after routing them through CNNL on MLU, the step reports zero fallbacks. On the same day, #724 removes the cambricon CI lane on the grounds that no runner can host it.

  • DAMO XuanTie merges 13 specialized operators in batch form within a single day while bumping the ZW810E cost model to 1.1.0 (11-02): FlagGems merges 13 PRs in the 43 minutes from 16:03 to 16:46, all tagged as KernelGen XuanTie output, covering long-tail items such as Chebyshev polynomials, igamma, 3D convolution and transposed convolution, quantized LSTM, fused RMSNorm backward, matrix norms, 3D adaptive pooling backward, and Lanczos upsampling—23.6% of FlagGems’ 55 merges that day. FlagTree #1236 bumps the ZW810E MM/MV cost model package to 1.1.0, with 8 BF16 mm variants and 2 mul models, and makes the tuning contract accept an ordered configs list. On 11-05, FlagAttention #127 opens another Wall Attention optimization ticket for PPU-ZW810E.

  • The quantum stack turns “hardware acceptance + resource accounting” into regressible evidence: the Ascend 910C native provider passes 167 items, and the synthesis line advances to UP-06 within the week (10-31 to 11-05): FlagQuantum #958 moves the 910C from experimental split-FP32 to the native provider operator path; the PR body records 167 passed in 115.41s (142 circuit, noise, TEBD, and QEC cases, plus 25 native dynamics cases, including two cold-process plan replays), with 1332 passed on the CPU-side regression; #970 adds a fail-closed native regression lane on pre-provisioned ARM64 runners.   • FlagGems converges cross-backend evaluation onto “one ruler” and completes backend key versioning the same week (11-03 / 11-05): At 16:41 and 16:42 on 11-03, 23 PRs merge within two minutes, 21 of them from the same author, migrating the H800 reference reads for four operator classes—attention and pooling, reduction and linear algebra, pointwise, and sparse and tensor layout—from scattered readers to unified JSON; previously the source, units, and rounding conventions of reference values were scattered across the code, leaving cross-backend scores without a common baseline.   • Community governance: seven FEPs enter review within an hour, turning 2.3’s multi-backend capabilities into acceptance items (11-05): From 08:29 to 09:31, seven single-file FEPs, all in Provisional Draft status and all from the same submitter. The list is FEP-0163 FlagMega v0.1, FEP-0164 TLE distributed fusion and auto-tuning, FEP-0165 TLE Raw five backends, FEP-0166 FLIR Ascend and GPGPU optimization, FEP-0167 twenty operator–device pairs, FEP-0168 FlagTree multi-platform and Triton API compatibility, and FEP-0170 FlagPrism v0.2. All seven bodies state “no GPU or model acceptance run performed,” separating scope claims from completion claims.

  • Industry side: FlagOS 2.2 cases begin to show quantifiable gains, and the edge-side narrative is filled in the same week (11-05): Two CSDN columns. China Telecom’s Xingchen Xing4.0-29B-A4B mHC multi-channel residual operator cuts cluster slowdown on Ascend 910C from 25% to 12%, with the adaptation already merged into MindFormers; after rewriting 12 long-sequence operators for the Agent scenario, first-token latency drops 19.87%; iSoftStone brings GLM-5.2 single-token latency on four 910C machines from 17.79 ms down under the 16.13 ms admission line. A set of 2.2 totals: 1454 operators across nine operator libraries, FlagTree covering 20 vendor backends, 27.6% overall performance improvement on 10 chips, Day0 adaptation for 12 chips, 107 community members, and 460,000 quarterly downloads (4.76× the previous quarter). The edge-side session gives adaptation statistics for 21 vendors and 35 chips, with measured gains of 21%–25% for MiniCPM and Qwen running on pure ARM CPUs.

  • Why the Ascend base uses python 3.12: Two hard constraints are stated in the PR description—on flagos-pypi-ascend, the wheels for torch 2.13, torch-npu 2.13, torchvision 0.28, and torchaudio 2.11 are only available for cp312; CANN’s set_env.sh injects its bundled python tree into PYTHONPATH, so the base interpreter must exactly match the runtime venv, otherwise imports will mix versions. numpy is also pinned to 1.26.4.

  • Why the base deliberately omits FlagTree: 9.2.1 has neither a corresponding flagtree version pin nor a corresponding flagcx wheel, so this layer leaves flagtree, flagcx, and their dependencies all empty; the build order is changed to base, runtime without FlagTree, FlagTree builder, FlagTree wheel, and runtime refresh; when flagtree is absent, the compiler falls back to triton.

  • Retirement mechanism: The freeze of cuda12.8 is not a deletion—an enabled field is added to configs.yaml and set to false; the specs, images, and documentation are all retained, and the image stays on the flat tag from its last build; the script backend_enabled.py becomes the sole resolver, shared by generate_matrix.py, the two status reports, and the flagcx and vllm-plugin registries, so the frozen state cannot enter any build matrix.

  • Release status: The community release/2.3 directory currently contains only the release schedule; the manifest file is planned to be generated at the 11-30 feature freeze. According to the 2.3 schedule, the FEP merge deadline is 11-13, feature freeze is 11-30 with rc0 pulled, rc1 is 12-07, rc2 is 12-14, final freeze is 12-25, and GA is 12-30. Images running ahead of the manifest is within the planned cadence; 11-13 is the hard boundary for this version’s scope.

vLLM plugin: 0.28 closes on four backends on the same day, repack rolled out to 11 indexes

  • Four landing strategies: For the same upstream version, each of the four backends chooses its path according to its own conditions. Tsingmicro removes TXDA’s graph capture and partitioning implementation, directly rejects graph and compilation configurations at the configuration stage, and prompts for –enforce-eager; the rationale is that the segmented original graph has passed gating for two models under TP8, but Dense TP1 correctness has never been resolved. MetaX keeps all changes within vllm_fl and CI scripts, gating by vendor name or runtime detection; review explicitly rejected the approach of writing a .pth startup hook into site-packages. Moore Threads brings in native MCCL, FX/eager compilation, and segmented native graphs all at once, with graph handling consolidated into a single graph.py. Ascend implements only FULL_DECODE_ONLY decode graphs, leaving prefill, vision encoding, and sampling outside the graph.

  • repack mechanism: build-infra’s mechanism of “release steps write the directory, humans only write the copy” was formally put into operation this round, replacing the previous manual editing of the pypi_index field; one boundary fix splits the vendor at the first hyphen, avoiding truncating the platform name incorrectly when the version string contains both a + platform segment and a - segment. On the same day, a daily wheel line was added for FlagGems-vllm, the build and upload containers pass through the proxy, and the Ascend verify container was given an additional 1 GB /dev/shm.

  • 0.32 line in parallel: While 0.28 closes, five adaptations for 0.32 are advancing in the review queue—Enflame S60 (#675), Iluvatar (#674), AMD ROCm gfx1100 (#659), MetaX C550 (#693), and unified FL execution plus Enflame adaptation (#667). Running the maintenance line and the target line simultaneously has become the norm, and vendors need to maintain two sets of validation environments.

  • Sync mechanism: Four forward-syncs from 0.4.0-dev to 0.5.0-dev were merged this week (#676, #678, #688, #689); the method changed from whole-branch merges to replaying reviewed increments while preserving source commits, with conflicts disclosed via draft PRs; on 11-05, #698 completed another forward sync.

III. Components and Code Co-building

FlagGems: Five Batches in One Week, Shifting from Coverage to Comparability and Backend-Specific Implementations

  • Scale: Across the five daily-report windows, merged PRs numbered 55, 76, 35, 33, and 33 respectively, totaling 232 — the largest single-repo merge volume in the entire organization. The 76 merges on 11-03 were the weekly peak, accounting for 42.9% of the organization’s 177 merges that day.

  • XuanTie batch: The 13 merges on 11-02 concentrated on long-tail entries, including Chebyshev polynomials, igamma, 3D convolution and transposed convolution, quantized LSTM, RMSNorm backward fusion, matrix norms, 3D adaptive pooling backward, and Lanczos upsampling. These operators previously had low returns under generic Triton implementations; after specialization they can go directly into default dispatch. There is currently no record of integration with the FlagGems-vllm inference path.

  • Kunlunxin contracts and tuning: #8038 on 11-02 fixed a silent bug — the fast path for foreach-series out-of-place scalar overloads compared only element counts, not shapes, so lists with different shapes but equal element counts would all inherit the first tensor’s shape; the fix gates admission on full shape, and two other PRs changed sixteen scalar outputs to single-base-address addressing and merged list outputs into a flat buffer. On the same side, #8034 evaluates the autotuning grid before submitting JIT, and #7976 changes slot retrieval to use program_id(0); on the performance side, a dozen or so PRs cover pointwise fast paths, pool3d backward fusion, Chebyshev expansion, Jacobi blocking, and a single-formula linspace. #8123 reverted a BF16 pointwise optimization and #8245 redid it with a different approach — one revert and one replacement show this path is still being iterated.

  • Iluvatar and NVIDIA: Between 16:04 and 16:10 on 11-03, 10 BI-V150-specific operators were merged in six minutes (special polynomials, upsampling and padding backward, quantized max pooling, matmul smm, not_equal, pooling index backward); on the NVIDIA side, 12 PRs continued filling in sparse tensors, the cudnn family, views and shapes, and quantization and autograd internal operators. On 11-04 Iluvatar merged another 12, and #8396 reverted two prior updates that did not hold on BI-V150.

  • Enflame: All 5 merges on 11-04 were device-guard fixes (CUDA-only guard, im2col hardcoding, kl_div and arctanh, integer index clamping in upsample_nearest3d_backward, explicit failure on –override mismatch). The 41 GCU300 coverage tickets newly opened on 11-05 began landing in bulk the same day, with 25 cleared in one pass — approaches included narrowing 64-bit lane offsets to 32-bit, moving launch geometry to host-side precomputation, replacing _nv* external symbols with local implementations, and converting pointwise dynamic fallbacks into dedicated kernels.

  • Hygon and MetaX: #7388 on 11-04 added FP32 flash attention forward for Hygon; #7318 added channel-wise bmm_w8a8 INT8 fused scaling for MetaX; 11-04 also saw 9 Hygon FP32-specific open tickets (rsqrt, floor, nonzero, full-dimension reduction, masked_fill, nan_to_num, neg, masked_select, aminmax), all restricted to contiguous FP32.

  • Ascend and Moore Threads: #8110 on 11-05 added channel-wise INT8 BMM (+519/−3 lines); #7581 fixed UB overflow in Flash Attention forward and backward, and added varlen forward and dense D=256; Moore Threads #7246 rewrote mm on MP31 (single file, +2472/−146 lines), and #6706 added shape-aware TLE/SQMMA implementations for bmm and bmm.out (9 files, +2369 lines).

  • Backend key versioning: #8446 touched 12 files, +39/−38 lines, replacing seven bare vendor names with version keys. Its value is not naming consistency but that a wrong version fails instead of being silently skipped — under the old scheme, uv pip install ".[tsingmicro]" matched no extra, neither torch nor flagtree was installed, and setup.sh’s FlagTree check threw ModuleNotFoundError outright.

  • Tsingmicro and SiliconFlow: #8287 on 11-05 updated the tsingmicro backend to 1103 — 190 files, +23195/−914 lines, the largest single backend sync of the week; #6501 on 11-03 added three embedding bag operators in one go (supporting SUM/MEAN/MAX, weighting, padding exclusion, empty bags, and terminal offsets), +7494/−222 lines across 24 files.

FlagTree: Enhancements Become Runtime Switches, Hygon Gets TLE-Raw Working

  • Runtime switches: #1565 merged on 11-04, +123/−27 lines across 15 files, standardizing each FlagTree enhancement into a four-part pattern — a compile-time macro, a runtime knob (FLAGTREE_<NAME>, on by default, added to CACHE_INVALIDATING_ENV_VARS), a guard block consistent with surrounding code, and #else/#endif annotated with the macro name. Previously, once a compiler enhancement reached production, regressions could only be contained by rolling back the entire release; with switches pushed down, just that one enhancement can be turned off.

  • Stacked tickets: Between 13:02 and 13:15 on 11-04, 38 tickets were opened in fourteen minutes: 12 [TLE][NVIDIA], 4 [TLE][AMD], 7 [TLE][Iluvatar], 5 [FlagCX], and 5 [Iluvatar][Native]; #1563 also documented the dependency graph and validation gates for feature PRs, specifying which must merge in order and which can be validated independently.

  • Hygon TLE-Raw: #1508 on 11-05 added the tle.raw path for HCU — 53 files, +5106/−17 lines; it supports both eager and deferred modes, both HIP and MLIR sources (compiled to LLVM IR and embedded in Triton IR), and integrates DUSHMEM streaming collective communication, with three tutorials on ring shift, allgather GEMM, and GEMM allreduce. In the examples, sort’s HIP path is 1.29× faster than torch.

  • Correctness and observability: #1460 on 11-02 fixed TritonGPU pipelined cp.async degradation and WGMMA operand buffer reuse (a class of bugs that compute wrong results rather than erroring), and #1451 aligned WGMMA warp selection with upstream MMAv3; #1530 on 11-05 parses register, spill, stack, and shared-memory usage from ptxas diagnostics and writes them into TLE compiler metadata, with the repo side reporting 154 TLE MLIR tests and 11 new C++ unit tests all passing.

  • Other: #1236 on 11-02 added the XuanTie ZW810E cost model 1.1.0; #1524 on 11-04 added trigonometric compatibility and independent-rounding multiply APIs for AMD native, and #1370 changed DEB/RPM packaging to verify-only; #1572 on 11-05 made Moore Threads preserve explicit blocked layouts during dot conversion, and #1576 enhanced SoC-level dispatch for Enflame TLE.

FlagQuantum: Three Acceptance Lines Reach UP-04/05/06, Fault-Tolerant Compilation Integration Ticket Takes Shape

  • Scale: Merges across the five windows were 35, 21, 28, 29, and 5, totaling 118; the repo’s share of submissions fell from 58% in the three-day window to 43–47%, with the non-quantum side back above half.

  • 910C and CI: #958 and #970 on 11-02 promoted Ascend 910C to a native provider and built a fail-closed regression lane, with dependency boundaries spelled out: Torch-FL taken from commit 3aebd0c on the feat/ascend-complex-lowering branch, and FlagCX from commit 6fe3631 of v0.14.1 plus a patch — binding reproducibility to source commits rather than binary packages.

  • Synthesis line: The 21 merges on 11-02 spanned three lines — UP (semantics), AO (arithmetic), and PF (performance): UP-01 lets functions decorated with @synth.qfunc directly write allocations, gates, and outputs; AO-00/01/02 successively establish an arithmetic baseline and independent oracle, protected common subexpression elimination and bit-fact normalization, and protected squaring plus constants and terminal XOR; PF-00/01 establish compile-latency and OS RSS baselines and reuse validated plan fields.

  • Frontend and algorithms: UP-04 on 11-04 added bounded uniform state preparation, scalar coherent control, bounded predicates, and checked phase helpers; UP-05 normalized frontend entry dispatch and extracted arithmetic lowering into a shared path; UP-06 supports U gates and array Hadamard for both Qmod and native qfunc. On the algorithm side, it added gate-level lowering for sparse chemistry SELECT, executable THC SELECT, and THC rotations lowered via QROM and phase gradients.

  • Detector error models: #1052 through #1069 on 11-04 successively added mutually exclusive fault parity, duplicate fault merging, multi-chunk round trips, sparse chunk projection, and per-round decoding, alongside the FTQC W1-B reference coverage map in #1072 — this line fills in the data path for quantum error-correction decoding, not algorithm demos.

  • Fault-tolerant compilation and CI: About 20 commits on the feat/ftqc-native-integration branch on 11-05 ported QVR’s phase gradients, scaled addition, measurement adjoints, and Hamming-weight phases section by section; the main integration ticket #1084 is open as a draft — 276 files, +31011/−92 lines. All 27 commits that day centered on CI quality gates — decoupling capacity from the hosted queue, restricting image eligibility, having required merge gates reject cancelled dependencies, bounded retries for artifact uploads, and migrating the core suite to a qualified offline environment.

FlagCX: Hygon bitcode Unified Packaging, Ascend 910C CI and traits Abstraction

  • Hygon: #667 on 11-02 was a structural change to the UIL line, adding the Hygon bitcode build and unifying how Device IR is packaged. Previously each backend packaged its own IR, and Hygon onboarding required maintaining a separate set of artifacts; after unification, the packaging cost for new backends drops.

  • Testing and semantics: #687 on 11-02 enforced SymMem coverage by adapter type in CI, preventing a pipeline from staying green when an adapter never ran symmetric-memory tests; #688 and #689 made the DU stream guard select by torch generation and back-ported the same fix to 0.14.0-rc2.

  • Ascend CI: #669 on 11-04 added the Ascend 910C pipeline and unified unit-test orchestration (+1559/−664 lines, 41 files), running Torch Python coverage on four platforms and removing duplicate CUDA performance tests; #686, raised from the FlagQuantum side, handles Ascend device completion tracking and tensor keep-alive.

  • Abstraction layer: #693 on 11-05 added CoopOps and Clock traits with an NVIDIA implementation, abstracting device capability queries into traits; #691 made Window resource teardown retryable; #694 added shared Net provider dispatch to DefaultBackend.

Torch-FL: Expansion at Both Ends (Apple MPS and Cambricon MLU), BPU Fixed to Usable

  • Scale: Merges across the five windows were 13, 5, 2, 29, and 12. All 29 merges on 11-04 came from the same author, yet pointed in two directions.

  • Apple Silicon: One end brought the MPS backend to “trainable” — adding addmm (the operator nn.Linear with bias actually lands on), convolution backward, comparison and reduction families, trigonometric and exponential families, cumprod, and tril, with convolution backward bit-identical to the reference implementation.

  • NVIDIA default path: #700 on 11-02 fixed the default NVIDIA conf routing 1485 operators to boxing kernels — the highest-impact fix of the week; #684 changed FP4’s half-byte shift to tensor operations to hit the already-routed overload; #687 narrowed the day-0 FlagGems override to the operators its own description defends.

  • Horizon BPU: #721 on 11-04 established that the eager path was in fact running every operator on CPU — the operator bucket ladder never exceeded 512 lanes, dual-input artifacts were submitted with a single-tensor descriptor causing silent wrong values, and hbdk4 could not import numpy under box64. After the fix, Run is exposed through the C ABI so the eager path bypasses the aten dispatcher; on RDK S600, relu at 262144 lanes dropped from 0.176 ms to 0.073 ms, and one conv block from 23.2 ms to 11.1 ms. #726 added DeviceChain, letting multiple BPU artifacts execute back-to-back on device without intermediate activations returning to the host: serialized elementwise dropped from 0.417 ms to 0.104 ms, and four independent dispatches from 1.667 ms to 0.673 ms.

  • Iluvatar and Kunlunxin: #698 on 11-02 pinned Iluvatar CI’s FlagTree to 0.7.0 (the version actually running on that chip), and #697 routed baddbmm through the vendor GEMM; #745 on 11-05 lifted Iluvatar’s operator cap so mul.Tensor can land on FlagGems; #756 on 11-05 kept Kunlunxin’s 3.2 GB FlagTree wheel out of the pip cache.

FlagOS 2.2’s industry cases deliver quantifiable returns for the first time

  • China Telecom: Xingchen Xing4.0-29B-A4B (29B total parameters, 4B activated) was trained end-to-end on an Ascend 910C cluster, with precision alignment and deployment validation across 10 domestic chips completed on the day of release.   • iSoftStone: Its Tianyuan intelligent computing platform bills by token, and GLM-5.2 generates a single token in 17.79 ms on four Ascend 910C servers, while the model provider’s published API admission threshold is 16.13 ms; after adopting FlagOS, it enabled PD disaggregation (Prefill and Decode each on two servers) with optimization on both sides, bringing latency within the admission threshold.

VI. Trend Observations

  • Images first is a planned cadence; 11-13 is the hard boundary: All 23 base and 23 runtime images are tagged 2.3.0, while the manifest file will only be generated at the 11-30 feature freeze; per the schedule, the FEP merge deadline is 11-13, rc0 is 11-30, rc1 is 12-07, rc2 is 12-14, code freeze is 12-25, and GA is 12-30. Images running ahead of the manifest is not a disconnect; what is really worth watching is whether the seven new FEPs can be merged before 11-13, and whether the cuda13.3 line will move the torch version again.

  • Multi-chip adaptation has shifted from “template copying” to “per-backend accounting”: For the same vLLM 0.28.0, the four backends yield four strategies — SpacemiT proactively gives up graph mode in exchange for a correctness boundary, MetaX makes the plugin self-sufficient when there is no upstream branch, Moore Threads folds graphs into a single file and brings in native MCCL, and Ascend only does decode graphs. Adaptation work no longer has a unified template; each vendor trades off according to its own validation capability and downstream model requirements, and the hidden cost is maintaining both 0.28 and 0.32 environments simultaneously.

  • Benchmark comparability is becoming new infrastructure: FlagGems converges the H800 reference readings of four operator categories into a unified JSON with 23 entries in two minutes, optest probes the operator lists actually registered by each vendor and fills in NVIDIA baselines, and FlagTree and FlagCX push results to Feishu multi-dimensional tables and extend coverage to four platforms. This line does not produce operator counts, yet it determines whether cross-backend scores are trustworthy; this week 26 entries fell on the CI and benchmarking side, accounting for 14.7% of single-day merges.

  • The bottleneck sits stably at the review end, not the production end: The 11-04 daily report records 1,602 open items under review across the organization, a merge-to-in-review ratio of about 1 to 9; within the 11-05 to 11-06 window there were still 288 and 255 updated in-review items, with the FlagGems and FlagTree repos accounting for 60%. During the same period, several PR bodies carry AI agent tool, model, and human review fields, indicating that the coding end has already been amplified by agents and the capacity bottleneck has shifted to review and validation environments.

  • Both paths for onboarding new chips have been proven; the gap lies in validation depth: Biren enters TransformerEngine-FL via the SUPA backend, a skeleton-first approach with no hardware validation record; Cambricon enters Torch-FL via its native platform and tests on eight MLU590-M9 cards, reverting the training step to zero the next day; SpacemiT runs a dedicated operator line at ten operators a day. Onboarding speed is no longer the threshold; whether hardware gate records can be produced is the dividing line.

  • The engineering path for the quantum stack is now clear: The 167 hardware acceptance items on 910C, the resource accounting for x*256 dropping from 28 allocated qubits and a 4 GiB state buffer to 19 and 8 MiB, and the fail-closed regression lane together constitute three verifiable pieces of evidence — “semantics, resources, hardware.” None of this week’s 27 commits concerns the quantum algorithm itself; all are for making tests trustworthy and the runner reproducible, indicating a consensus that paying the infrastructure bill first is the right move at this stage.

  • The operational shortfall remains external narrative: The 2.2 industry cases can already cite hard numbers such as 25% to 12%, 19.87%, and 10x, yet component-level news has had zero hits for several consecutive windows, and third-party media coverage has yet to take shape. For FlagOS, the gap between technical output and external visibility may affect ecosystem expansion speed more than any single backend’s feature gap.
