FlagOS Weekly Report (2026-09-30 ~ 2026-10-07)

2026-10-07 Kubuds

Reporting period: September 30, 2026 (Wednesday) to October 7, 2026 (Wednesday), 8 days total — National Day holiday special summary period Sources: The last pre-holiday issue (2026-09-30) and the first post-holiday issue (2026-10-08, for window-tail continuity verification) of this publication’s daily FlagOS updates; daily reports were suspended from October 1 to 7, so holiday activity is based on verification of commits, PRs, and tags across all 54 GitHub repositories of the organization (verification extended to the morning of 10-08), while news coverage is based on public searches using multiple keyword sets over the holiday window (about 9 days) Editorial note: Development within the organization did not stop during the holiday window: a total of 801 commits (712 for FlagQuantum), 412 PRs merged (312 for FlagQuantum), and 447 new PRs opened (331 for FlagQuantum); 16 repositories had pushes, and 12 repositories recorded commits to the default branch within the window. On the external news side, component-level searches remained quiet, while the industry focus was DeepSeek’s September 30 open-sourcing of infrastructure components for Ascend (see Chapter 5). The post-holiday first day (10-08) saw work resume on schedule: as of verification that morning, 14 PRs had already been recorded as merged.

I. Weekly Highlights

  • Development intensity undiminished over the holiday: 801 commits and 412 PRs merged in an 8-day window (09-30 ~ 10-07): FlagQuantum alone contributed 712 commits (88.9%) and 312 merges, making it the undisputed mainstay of the organization’s holiday development; the remaining repos totaled 89 commits, concentrated in multi-vendor CI migration and documentation batches. Commit cadence rose steadily from 10-01 onward (FlagQuantum alone: 60 commits on 10-01, 101 on 10-05, 194 on 10-06, 122 on 10-07), with intensity peaking in the middle-to-late part of the holiday.
  • FlagQuantum distributed certification campaign concludes: two-node fully enters the certification release track (10-01 ~ 10-08): On 10-01, #312 promoted two-node state vector, MPS, and tensor network training to production_supported; #318 / #321 re-recorded the two-node claim with empirical evidence and back-derived blockers; on 10-06, #535 certified two-node state vector and measured MPS / TN capacity ceilings; on the morning of 10-08, #587 certified two-node MPS training and recorded the TN capacity ceiling — two_node_mps_training was promoted to release_certified. The certification tolerance gate (#292) and evidence pinning (#348) were completed in tandem.
  • FlagGems: major KernelGen supply-side progress + multi-vendor CI migration (10-01 ~ 10-06): The kernelgen-dev branch saw 34 commits — 226 operator definitions and tests (#6853), 70 original operator definitions (#6860), test suites for batches 1–28 and the final batch (#6799 ~ #6839), and missing definition backfill (#6862, thnn_conv2d); the main branch saw 14 commits — backend tests for hygon / metax / mthreads / iluvatar migrated into runtime images (#6844), CPU runner preinstall flattening (#6847), and flagtree 0.7.0 backend parsing and PPU support (#6863 / #6852). A total of 47 merges in the window.
  • build-infra finalizes 2.2, opens 2.3 (10-01): At 17:30 in the evening, annotated tag v2.2.0 was cut (labeled “Release 2.2.0”; this repo manages its version line via tags rather than GitHub Release objects); afterward, “Bump base version to 2.3.0” (#1316) and root directory cleanup (#1317) were merged, and a workflow preparation batch (#1318 ~ #1321) was completed in the evening — the infrastructure repo’s development baseline officially switched to the 2.3 line.
  • FlagCX CRL transport hardening streak of six (09-30 ~ 10-04): #633 kernel proxy integrated into shared RMA transport, #635 collective proxy integrated with ordered multi-channel / multi-QP transport, #636 symmetric window and MR lifecycle hardening, #638 GET data flushed before publish completion, #639 unified remote visibility flush semantics (plus #637 cleaning up the now-defunct image build workflow) — transport-layer correctness hardening ran through the entire holiday.
  • FlagFFT multi-platform optimization branches advance over the holiday (10-02 ~ 10-05): MetaX maca-3d-opt and Ascend npu-2d / npu-3d-opt branches saw multiple pushes on 10-02 ~ 10-03; on the Hygon side, codex/hcu-fourstep-c2r, codex/hcu-3d-route-fix, and -dev sub-branches were opened in succession on 10-03 ~ 10-04; Moore Threads musa-3d-opt continued advancing after launching before the holiday. No new merges on the default branch; results await review in branch form; the repo’s last push was 10-05 16:10.
  • Member unit holiday batches continue to flow (09-30 ~ 10-08): MetaX W8A8 INT8 matrix multiplication (#6572) merged, Hygon bmm / baddbmm new tickets (#6865 / #6866), Kunlunxin XPU3 SDNN 46-marker fix batch (#6867), DAMO XuanTie ADDMM series performance batch (#6859), Ascend fix streak of three (#6848 ~ #6850) and Qwen3.6 inference path optimization (sglang-plugin-FL #137), Enflame specialized operator streak of four (FlagGems-vllm #889 ~ #892), Cambricon rotary embedding backend (FlagAttention #84) — see Chapter 4 for details.
  • Industry focus: DeepSeek open-sources Ascend-oriented infrastructure components (09-30): Covering TileLang high-level language compilation tools, compute libraries, and distributed communication libraries, with all components corresponding one-to-one with its NVIDIA-oriented open-source components (DeepGEMM, DeepEP, TileKernels, FlashMLA, DeepSelect); concurrently disclosed a joint effort with Huawei on a 128-card supernode solution based on Ascend 950 (UBL128 networking and ASC-COMM communication library support). Not a FlagOS organizational development; included as industry background (see Chapter 5).

V. Ecosystem and Community

  • Industry side (background inclusion): DeepSeek open-sources Ascend-version infrastructure components (09-30): The open-source components correspond one-to-one with their NVIDIA platform versions — TileLang high-level language compilation tool (encapsulating Ascend C low-level instructions, carrying most operators for V4-series model training), DeepGEMM (matrix operations), DeepEP (cross-device communication), TileKernels (vector computation and memory access), FlashMLA (sparse attention), DeepSelect (data filtering); jointly advancing the Ascend 950 128-card super-node solution with Huawei (UBL128 full interconnect + ASC-COMM communication library), with multiple test groups showing compute and communication performance approaching hardware limits. This aligns with FlagOS’s multi-chip unified software stack narrative and is the most closely watched industry development of this cycle (not a dynamic of this stack).
  • Competition: FlagOS Competition Track1 (SGLang cross-chip operator optimization) submission queue continues to flow through the FlagGems-sglang main repo — gelu_tanh_and_mul (#121), deepep_permute (#123), dsv3_fused_a_gemm (#125), group_norm_silu (#127), concat_mla_k (#128), l2norm (#133), add3 (#134) and 15 others in queue; the holiday window saw a new build_trtllm_mha_page_table submission (#135, 09-30), and on the first day post-holiday add3 (#134) resumed review progress.
  • Conference: 2026 AI Open Computing Conference & Zhongzhi FlagOS Technology Conference (October 17–18, Beijing) — entering the final preparation period post-holiday (fewer than ten days countdown); the conference’s quantum-intelligence fusion session aligns with the holiday FlagQuantum certification / compiler line progress.
  • External channels: Official WeChat account and community channels silent during the holiday; public queries on components and release lines continue a calm sequence (the 09-30 daily report was the 23rd consecutive calm window, with no component-level new announcements across the entire holiday window); the BAAI community during the holiday only reposted third-party technical content (unrelated to this stack, not included in this report).

VI. Trend Observations

  • Holiday concentrated development refreshes understanding again: FlagQuantum became the absolute engine of the window with roughly 90% of commits (including a single-day peak of 194 on 10-06), while the rest of the organization ran in low-power mode (CI migration, documentation, ticket filing) — the post-holiday review queue will be digested in one burst (14 merges on the first day already signal this).
  • Release engineering completes the 2.3 handover: build-infra accomplished “2.2 final tag → 2.3 baseline switch → workflow preparation” in one smooth sequence; the 2.3 development line (FlagGems 5.5, FlagGems-vllm 0.3.0, vLLM 0.28 adaptation) is expected to accelerate convergence post-holiday.
  • KernelGen moves from “generating operators” to “definition and test supply” phase: 226 + 70 definitions and 29 test suites merged in a concentrated batch — the operator supply side is laying industrial groundwork for the 2.3 coverage sprint.
  • Vendor collaboration’s holiday low-amplitude normalization: Four vendors’ backend runtime image migrations and multiple optimization / fix tickets continued to flow during the holiday, showing that collaboration cadence no longer depends on the workday rhythm; large tasks (Hygon bmm series, Ascend fix triple) chose the rhythm of filing before the holiday and reviewing after.
  • The quantum line’s “evidence-first” methodology output: From certification campaigns to evidence revision declarations (#595 declaring sixteen revisions in one statement), the acquisition of release_certified status is grounded in reproducible measurements and auditable declarations — this engineering discipline has methodological reference value for other component lines in 2.3.
  • Next observation points: The 10-17 conference’s external releases and quantum-intelligence fusion session; 2.3 development line convergence and first tags; review and landing of Hygon bmm series, Ascend fix triple, and FlagFFT branch optimizations; the convergence direction of KernelGen batches; community manifest snapshot refresh for the 2.2 wrap-up.
