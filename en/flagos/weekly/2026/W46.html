FlagOS Weekly Report (2026-11-09 ~ 2026-11-13)

2026-11-13 Kubuds

Reporting period: Monday, November 9, 2026 to Friday, November 13, 2026 — 5 working days, 5 daily reports; plus supplementary checks on the release day (November 14) covering org pushes, merged-PR searches, and the image line, capturing wrap-up changes from 11-13 06:00 to 11-14 18:00

Part 1 · Brief

This section is the week’s curated highlights, kept to 800 words by default (799 words this issue); full entries, itemized lists, and sources are archived in Part 2.

Of the 64 repos this week, 19 to 22 had pushes; across the five issues there were 1,077 commits and 1,030 merges. The open-review backlog fluctuated between 252 and 343, ending the period at 272.

  • The 2.3 roadmap opens, targeting a 12-30 release: #176 sets three main lines, with vLLM 0.32 and SGLang 0.5.18 as the primary release versions; FEP-0163 FlagMega merged the same day, requiring a 20% compile-efficiency gain for the full Qwen3.8 model and GLM5.3 Flash decode throughput 5% above vLLM, and it must land in main by 12-07.
  • The image line is settled backend by backend: T-Head PPU 2.1.0 ships its first runtime image, ROCm 7.2.4 is recorded, Tianshu corex5.0.1 replaces 4.4.0, Ascend CANN 9.2.1 adds 910C support, and the release day adds a MUSA 5.3.0 base.
  • FlagTree 0.7.1 released in batches: the official release covers only ascend3.5 and xpu3.6; the rest remain a1, a2, or dev1; Intellifusion joins as ifwa:100, and Ascend 3.6.x is under review.
  • Operator production remains the main output: FlagGems merged 591 PRs this week, 57.4% of the whole org; KernelGen peaked in a single day at 87 for Ascend, 63 for Enflame, 89 for MetaX, and 22 for Hygon, with the operator table trimmed to 141.
  • Cambricon adds queue ordering: Torch-FL #809 routes 17 operators in the Qwen3 training step through CNNL and fixes the silent defects of USE_CAMBRICON not being compiled and Triton launching on an empty queue; runtime is still 1.86× that of torch_mlu.
  • Kunlunxin completes the 618-to-256 routing: after P800 measurements, 256 operators are routed to FlagGems; vLLM 0.32.0 was pulled within two hours of listing due to a py3.10 import failure.
  • vLLM 0.32 becomes a ten-vendor migration checklist: NVIDIA and T-Head have merged, eight are under review; 17 competition-track operators merged, with 9 more entering review in the early hours of 11-13.

This week’s assessment: 2.3 has moved from collecting proposals to filling in deliveries backend by backend; the risk lies not in API renames but in platform differences and release speed. The 272-item review backlog remains the bottleneck, with only three weeks left until 12-07.

I. Weekly Highlights

  • The 2.3 roadmap is published, targeting a December 30 release, with model compilation elevated to stand alongside operators and frameworks (11-12): community #176 published the 2.3 roadmap in the early hours of November 12, listing proposals, tracking tickets, and owners by SIG, with checkboxes serving as release acceptance evidence. The three main tracks are model and framework coverage, compiler and runtime performance, and developer experience and delivery; on the framework side, it requires completing vLLM 0.24 acceptance and designating vLLM 0.32 as the primary release version for the twelve-vendor matrix, with SGLang upgraded to 0.5.18 covering ten vendors. On the same day, FEP-0163 FlagMega was merged and marked as in implementation by its companion PR, with tracking ticket #173 taking over delivery; the FEP requires compiling the full Qwen3.8 and GLM5.3 Flash models into MegaKernel, validating on three backends—NVIDIA, AMD, and Iluvatar CoreX 5.0—with a 20% optimization target set for Qwen3.8 and GLM5.3 Flash decode throughput required to exceed vLLM by more than 5% on four H800s. feature/flagmega must be merged into main before the FlagTree release branch is cut on December 7, with release acceptance completed by December 25.

  • FlagTree 0.7.1 official releases roll out by backend in batches, with Intellifusion becoming the first new backend in the 2.3 cycle (11-12): Two official releases, 0.7.1+ascend3.5 (15:43) and 0.7.1+xpu3.6 (19:28), were published on the same day, with the Release notes containing only one line: “pypi 0.7.1+"; the remaining backends remain at a1 (hcu, tsingmicro), a2 (iluvatar, mthreads), or dev1 (ppu). Releasing by backend in batches under the same version number is standard FlagTree practice, but this time it runs directly up against the 12-07 branch-cut deadline: backends not released before the cut can only enter 2.3 with pre-release versions. On the same day, #1598 merged the Intellifusion backend, targeting ifwa:100 and Triton 3.8, including lowering for memory access and matmul, software pipelining, async copy, LHS-to-TMEM promotion, and a separate manual wheel workflow. The Ascend Triton 3.6.x port (#1637) is still under review and, if merged, will return from the triton_v3.5.x branch to main.

  • The 2.3.0 image line and version data were filled in backend by backend over the week, with the smallest unit of release preparation being one backend, one document (11-07 ~ 11-14): T-Head PPU 2.1.0 first received its base and runtime changelogs on 11-07, completed its first runtime image delivery on 11-11, and added cp312 vendor wheels for torchvision 0.25.0 and torchaudio 2.10.0; on 11-10, the 2.3.0 change date was recorded for AMD ROCm 7.2.4; on 11-12, Iluvatar corex5.0.1 fully replaced corex4.4.0, and on the same day the 910C base and flagcx packaging lines for Ascend CANN 9.2.1 were added; the release-day check also saw build-infra #1792 add the Moore Threads mthreads-musa5.3.0 base layer and #1789 add the ppu builder and daily line. One tag per PR, with version data and changelog landing in the same batch, was build-infra’s sole working method this week.

  • FlagGems merged 591 commits in one week, as KernelGen turns “writing operators” into “reviewing generated results” (11-06 ~ 11-12): The five-period window saw 123, 166, 149, 85, and 68 merges respectively, accounting for 57.4% of the organization’s 1,030 commits—the only single repo above half. Single-day peaks occurred at three vendors: Ascend with 87 (11-06, landed by the same author within one hour), Enflame with 63 (11-09, all boundary-correctness fixes for GCU300), MetaX with 89 (11-10, the entire foreach family rolled out), and Hygon with 22 (11-12, 20 foreach operators within 100 minutes). On 11-12, #8740 deleted 7 host-side operators that violated repository conventions, reducing the experimental operator table from 142 to 141—showing that while capacity is scaling up, a reverse cleanup has begun.

  • Cambricon reclaims 17 operators in the Qwen3 training step to native implementations and fixes a silent queue-ordering defect (11-10): Torch-FL #809 has the in-place arithmetic family, three fused CNNL entry points, four view operator categories, and zeros, sqrt, and div supplied directly from the code generator to Cambricon native implementations, so the training step no longer detours through FlagGems for these operators, while also fixing the fill_ lifecycle issue. More critically, there was a build defect: torch_bindings had never been compiled with USE_CAMBRICON, so the binding that tells Triton which cnrt queue to dispatch to simply did not exist—Triton was actually running on an empty queue with no ordering guarantee relative to CNNL kernels. Such defects raise no error; they only cause occasional wrong numbers. The PR itself states that the training step still takes 1.86× as long as torch_mlu, addressing only coverage and correctness, not performance parity.

  • Kunlunxin uses two-stage measurement to narrow 618 operators down to 256 routed ones, as vLLM 0.32.0 hits its first hard blocker on py3.10 (11-11): Torch-FL #806 took over the empty list left the previous day—#789 had disabled four breakpoints on the P800 and achieved end-to-end execution, but the allowlist was empty and the config file was byte-for-byte identical to before the change, on the grounds that the first route must be backed by measured data. The next day, #806 performed two-stage measurement on the P800, filtering 256 out of the 618-operator ceiling; the config changed from “no route 1985, Kunlunxin 57” to “no route 1729, Kunlunxin 57, FlagGems 256”; the route count went from 316 to 315 to 256 across three revisions, with the author shifting focus from “listing names” to “fixing the filtering criteria.” On the same day, build-infra added vLLM 0.32.0 to deps_app in the early morning, then recorded the version’s import failure under py3.10 and withdrew it within two hours.

  • vLLM 0.32 goes from a single line on the roadmap to a migration checklist for ten vendors (11-09 ~ 11-13): Two landmark adaptations merged within a single day on 11-12—NVIDIA #737 migrated KV cache initialization from a private function removed in 0.32 to the public allocate_kv_cache and bind_kv_cache, while T-Head #706 brought PPU-ZW810E onto 0.5.0-dev using the “0.32-empty” approach (deleting removed symbols and their call sites rather than stubbing them out). Since then, eight vendors are under review: MUSA (#715), Hygon (#711), MetaX (#693), Kunlunxin P800 (#726), Ascend Runner V2 (#703), Iluvatar (#674), Enflame (#675), and AMD W7900D (#659). Framework migration has entered the execution phase, and the risk has shifted from “whether to upgrade” to “who gets stuck first.”

  • The competition track enters its final stretch: 72 problem definitions, 17 implementations, and 9 submissions opened for review in the early hours, with judging starting after 11-13 (11-09 ~ 11-13): On 11-09, 72 SGLang competition operator definitions were merged at once, along with 23 missing definitions; on 11-10, 17 implementations tagged Track1 were merged, concentrated on block-scaled matrix multiplication for fp8 and mxfp8, quantization at three granularities, KV cache mxfp8 write-back, and AWQ dequantization; on 11-12, awq_gemm was merged; between 01:07 and 01:08 on 11-13, the same author opened 9 PRs in succession. The competition runs from August 17 to November 13, with acceptance completed within one month after it ends. The repository contains only definitions and submitted code, which does not mean they have passed the performance leaderboard; this report draws no conclusions on results.

VI. Trend Observations

  • 2.3 release preparation has shifted down to “backend × component”: FlagTree gating by backend, 2.3.0 adding image docs per backend, and vLLM 0.32 closing out per vendor — none of these are one-shot actions. This means that until 12-30, the weekly report’s unit of observation is no longer the version number but “whether a given backend has been gated on a given component.”
  • Model compilation has become a third mainline alongside operators and frameworks: FEP-0163 sets two verifiable targets, 20% and 5%, tied to Qwen3.8, GLM5.3 Flash, and three backends. The previous mainlines were operator coverage and backend adaptation; from now on there is an additional layer — “whether whole-model compilation meets the target” — which is also the most uncertain item before the 12-07 cutoff.
  • With operator throughput peaking, focus shifts to correctness and clearing the books: all 63 Enflame merges are GCU300 boundary fixes, Kunlunxin added non-atomic overwrite, Iluvatar switched to single-warp launch, FlagGems removed 7 non-compliant operators and cut the experimental table from 142 to 141, and another 11 merges converged test drift. This work generates no news, but it determines whether “which is right, the vendor branch or mainline” can be decided automatically.
  • Version-migration risk has shifted from APIs to platform differences: Kunlunxin’s vLLM 0.32.0 failed to import on py3.10 and was pulled two hours after listing; T-Head used “0.32-empty” to delete symbols outright rather than stub them. For the same upstream version, each vendor must recompute against its own Python and driver stack, with the hidden cost of maintaining two validation environments at once.
  • Review remains the sole bottleneck: the review backlog stands at 252, 343, 317, and 272 items, consistently above half the current merge volume; 120 new items were opened on 11-12 alone. KernelGen has scaled up the code-writing end, but merge speed is limited by review and hardware validation environments — which explains why the day of “less writing, more deciding” matters more.
  • The distribution layer is converging; the gap remains in operator artifacts: TransformerEngine-FL ships a single pure-Python wheel for all non-NVIDIA vendors, FlagTree’s deb is one package per backend, and the rpm pulls published wheels directly; but T-Head PPU still lacks a cpp wheel because the FlagGems release mapping omits that backend, so operator capability can only go through the compilation path. Once distribution is unified, the next gap is “whose operators have prebuilt artifacts.”
  • The quantum stack moves noticeably faster than other components, but should not be read as release-grade: 107 commits and 63 merges in a week advanced Hubbard and t-V condensed-matter models, parity symmetry, and native implementations of 216 programs simultaneously; the value of this work is making simulators trainable and verifiable, but it remains far from productization, and the AI for Science section in 2.3 is still marked TBD.
