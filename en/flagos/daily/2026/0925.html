Today’s Highlight: 3 Days to 2.2 GA (09-28) — Delivery Scope Audit Begins: 18 FEPs Retained, 6 Deferred to 2.3

Date: 2026-09-24　Source: community #115, community #114

The rc2 stabilization period ended on 09-24 per the official schedule, with GA set for 09-28. Within this window, the main release-engineering activity shifted from “release artifact freeze” to “scope and claims freeze” — at 14:29 on 09-24 (Beijing time), the community repo opened PR #115 “docs: define delivered FlagOS 2.2 FEP scope and defer remaining work to 2.3” (branch docs/fep-2.2-rc2-audit-20260924, pushed twice that day through 16:12):

  • Scope bifurcation: the 2.2 index contains 18 Implemented FEPs (retained); FEP 0019, 0069, 0081, 0084, 0093, and 0100 have their target version changed to 2.3 and are marked Deferred.
  • Tightened retention criteria: each retained FEP is limited to “implemented interfaces + verified configurations,” with all other goals moved into an explicit 2.3 section and the associated deferred-work index.
  • RC2-aligned update sources: source revisions, APIs, packaging details, test commands, and associated PRs updated item by item, while preserving verification provenance, platform failures, and known limitations.
  • Terminology clarification: explicitly distinguishes Tree primitive QA from NVSHMEM fusion / FlagCX-backed fusion, and Compressor artifact testing from full-model inference acceptance — avoiding any framing of “component-level verification” as “end-to-end acceptance.”
  • Style constraints: concise English required; template text and “unsupported completion / performance claims” removed.

In parallel, the formal tag list for the ten L1 operator library components (community #114: flaggems v5.4.0, flagfft v0.2.0, flagsparse v0.3.0, flagdnn v0.3.0, flagblas v0.3.0, flagtensor v0.3.0, flagaudio v0.3.0, flagattention v0.4.0, flaggems-vllm v0.2.0, flaggems-sglang v0.1.0) remains under review (waiting merge).

Assessment: Yesterday’s observation was that “the manifest, tags, and scope cross-validate one another”; today #115 advances that validation to a third layer — the public claims themselves. With 3 days to GA, the focus is not new features but separating “implemented / verified / deferred” into distinct statements, each backed by evidence. This is a release-discipline action: the public introduction of 2.2 will use this version of the FEP document as its factual basis, and the subsequent 2.3 development narrative (including new directions such as 3D compute and quantum) is now formally cut off from the 2.2 delivery boundary.

III. Member Unit Deep Dive

3.1 Moore Threads: Full-Pipeline Inference Support for Protenix-v2 — Closing the Loop on Three AI4S Scenarios (09-24)

Date: 2026-09-24　Source: ITHome (via ifeng), Yicai

Moore Threads announced that, based on its in-house MUSA software stack, it has completed full-pipeline inference support for the open-source biomolecular structure prediction model Protenix-v2 on its training-inference integrated AI compute card MTT S5000. Protenix-v2 was developed by ByteDance’s Seed team (released 2026-04) and is one of the few fully open-source foundation models worldwide capable of high-precision end-to-end prediction of proteins, DNA, RNA, and small-molecule ligand complexes. Measured results: S5000 delivers roughly 26% average speedup in end-to-end inference performance over mainstream international GPUs, with 3D conformation prediction accuracy highly aligned. With this adaptation, Moore Threads has completed end-to-end engineering validation across three core AI4S scenarios: macromolecular structure prediction, whole-genome sequence modeling, and clinical medical imaging. This dovetails dynamically with yesterday’s report on RLinf × S5000 on the training side — the training side (embodied RL) and the inference side (AI4S) each landed a move in the same week, and the scenario narrative of “domestic compute power taking on scientific research workflows” continues to broaden.
