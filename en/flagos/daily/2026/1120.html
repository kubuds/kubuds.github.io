Today’s Focus: New BIS Rule Takes Effect, Triggering Broad Selloff in Domestic Compute Stocks; Enflame Hits Limit Down, Then Announces “Robust Order Backlog” (11-19)

Date: 2026-11-19　Sources: Enflame Technology Abnormal Fluctuation Announcement · National Business Daily · EE Times China · 21jingji.com reposting Shanghai Securities News

  • The rule itself: On November 16, the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) issued an interim final rule, “Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors” (RIN 0694-AK50), which was published in the Federal Register and took effect on November 19. Existing compute services have a 90-day license transition period.
  • Three tightenings: Overseas data centers providing compute exceeding a cumulative 12 million TPP to companies in the D:5 country group must apply for a license; Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil are added to the licensing scope; for 16/14 nm and below FinFET logic chips, foundries can no longer override the controlled presumption under 3A090.a by self-verifying via transistor count.
  • The third point is key: Design companies must be placed on BIS’s “Approved IC Designer” list before foundries can accept their orders. This was not fully priced in during the final two trading days before the rule took effect.
  • Market action: On November 19, Enflame Technology sealed a 20% limit-down, closing at RMB 364.33, down RMB 91.08, with turnover of RMB 1.962 billion and a turnover rate of 28.35%. MetaX, Moore Threads, Hygon, Centec, and Hong Kong-listed Iluvatar CoreX fell the same day, most down more than 10% intraday.
  • The other side: On the same day, SMIC’s A-shares rose 4.38% and Hua Hong Semiconductor rose 7.47%. Capital did not leave domestic compute; it merely shifted from the design side to the manufacturing side.
  • Rumor and debunking: Starting on the evening of November 18, rumors circulated that Samsung was conducting strict, penetrating scrutiny of foundry arrangements; on the 19th, a screenshot naming each company’s foundry dependencies spread, claiming Iluvatar CoreX, ASR Microelectronics, and Enflame Technology were most dependent on TSMC. After seeking confirmation from multiple parties, Shanghai Securities News reported that Samsung has no formal document on strict foundry scrutiny and the rumor is inaccurate; approval certainty for 14/16 nm orders is relatively high, while 5 nm and below are reviewed case by case. ASR Microelectronics responded the same day that chips which have obtained an ECCN classification code and are confirmed to fall outside restricted scope can proceed with tape-out normally.
  • Relationship to FlagOS: Five of the companies named are FlagOS member organizations, including Enflame, MetaX, Moore Threads, Hygon, and Iluvatar CoreX. This fluctuation does not involve any component code, but the capital-market environment for member organizations changed together within a single day.
  • Observation: The market traded the “licensing regime” as a “blanket ban.” What has actually changed is the commercial certainty of overseas advanced-process channels, not same-day capacity. The structural fact that domestic compute has “achieved design localization but not yet manufacturing localization” was brought to the surface by a rumor for the first time.

  • Observation: this is claim governance. The same doc had both stale version numbers and an inference error conflating two walls into one; the fix also labels the evidence sources.

1.7 FlagGems: Ascend A5 Architecture Detection and 950DT/950PR Scaffolding Merged (11–19)

Date: 2026-11-19　Source: #9543

  • Problem: Ascend generations can’t be distinguished by a generic lookup. On NPU, props.major is None, and both 910B and 950 SoC names start with 9.
  • First change: the backend framework exposes a get_arch_key hook so vendors can take over architecture determination. The returned key indexes ARCH_MAP; returning None falls through to the generic kernel.
  • Second change: the Ascend vendor layer declares ARCH_MAP = {"a5": "a5"}; the 910B series remains the baseline and needs no entry. The ARCH environment variable takes precedence, with aliases including a5, ascend_a5, 950, ascend_950; otherwise it substring-matches the driver-reported SoC name, with 950 covering all SKUs.
  • Fallback: returns None for CPU-only builds or when the driver is unavailable, leaving the generic path unaffected.
  • Scaffolding: new empty a5/ and a5/ops/ packages as the landing spot for future A5 operator specializations, with reserved per-architecture tune_configs and enable_configs.
  • Observation: this commit adds no kernels, only detection and a place to put them. The same day, FlagGems-vllm merged its first A5 operator on the ascend-a5-ops branch — two lines making the same preparation.
  • Note: A5 corresponds to Ascend950DT and Ascend950PR, with NpuArch DAV_3510.

1.8 FlagGems-vllm: v0.2.1.dev1 Tagged, First A5 Operator ~4.5× Faster (11–19)

Date: 2026-11-19　Source: v0.2.1.dev1 · #1338 · #1321

  • Tagging: v0.2.1.dev1 was released at 10:27 Beijing time, with a one-line release note: “Release version 0.2.1.dev1”. The same day, FlagTree’s CI bumped the HCU gems-vllm baseline to that version.
  • First A5 operator: #1338 adds a persistent tiled implementation of topk_softplus_sqrt for 950DT, landing in ascend/a5/ops/. With 256 experts, bf16, topk=6, 4096 tokens drops from ~250 µs to 55 µs.
  • Why it’s fast: each program handles BLOCK_M tokens and fetches the whole block of scores with one wide tl.load; the grid is capped at the AIV core count (950DT falls back to 96), so launch overhead no longer grows with token count.
  • Two write-back paths: wide blocks write directly to HBM by rank; narrow blocks assemble [BLOCK_M, BLOCK_K] first and write once contiguously. Indices take the smallest id among the max-value experts, preserving the original tie semantics.
  • 910 side aligned the same day: #1321 ports the A5 KDA kernel back to 910 and changes the benchmark to take values against the vendor AscendC operator. Two constants changed in the port: _ASCEND_BT from 64 to 32 (910B’s AIV core UB is only 192 KB, and 64 needs 224 KB), and the Neumann expansion from 0 to 3.
  • Rejected approach: a tiled port of A5’s causal_conv1d_fn ran at 63.9 ms on (10, 4096, 4096) versus 1.20 ms for the current 910 version. The cause: channel-first tiling pins BLOCK_N at 32, producing 1664 small programs. This was empirically rejected and documented in the PR.
  • Observation: this is day one of A5 moving from scaffolding to measurement. The same batch also fixed the benchmark methodology — earlier speedups were inflated by taking values against torch loops or vllm-ascend’s Triton fallback.

3.2 Cambricon: The Trade-off of 4.7.6 and 4.7.2 Coexisting (11-19)

Date: 2026-11-19　Sources: #1906 · #1907 · Changelog

  • Three lines coexisting: 4.4.3, 4.7.2, and the newly added 4.7.6. The changelog explicitly states that 4.7.6 is the vendor’s latest SDK line, while 4.7.2 remains in service.
  • Not switching the default: FlagCX’s default_for_vendor is set to false for the new line; 4.7.2 continues to provide libflagcx-cambricon until the new line is validated on nodes.
  • Build-side differences: The 22.04 deb for 4.7.6 sits in the cambricon/ root directory of the filestore, whereas 4.7.2’s is under u2204/. The recipe’s FILE_STORE therefore points to different locations.
  • Dependency gaps: torch-mlu 1.34.1 and torch-mlu-ops 1.13.0 are not yet complete on Cambricon’s private index; the wheels must wait for the FlagCX build image to finish before they can be compiled.
  • Why it’s worth waiting: 4.7.6 corresponds to torch 2.13.0 and Triton 3.4.0+mlu2.1.3, one step newer than the 4.7.2 set. If 2.3.0 can use it, operator coverage for the Cambricon backend will move up accordingly.
  • Observation: This is a deliberately slowed line switch. Not switching the default, not deleting the old line, and landing only the layers that can land—all three steps leave room for rollback. With only ten days to rc0, this pace means 4.7.6 will most likely miss the 2.3.0 default matrix.

3.3 Intellifusion: IFWA Explicitly Integrated via GEMS_VENDOR, Still Under Review (11-19)

Date: 2026-11-19　Source: FlagGems #9545

  • Change: Adds explicit IFWA backend selection to FlagGems, registering the ifwa device via GEMS_VENDOR=intellifusion. Generic heuristics are used until tuning data is available.
  • Bug fixed along the way: The shared pointwise generator used out_bptr.dtype.element_ty to get the storage type on Triton 3.8. It previously used out_bptr.type, which on 3.8 is an aggregate type without element_ty, causing generated kernels to throw AttributeError.
  • Verification scope: Compared 135 generated kernel bodies; only the storage type expression changed.
  • Status: Still under review, performance data pending, unit tests not yet covering it.
  • Observation: Intellifusion was not previously on the member unit list. If this merges, FlagOS will have one more explicit backend. It also shows that backend integration is becoming standardized—one environment variable plus one device registration, no longer requiring core changes.

IV. Summary

  • The base layer is entering its final stretch: Cambricon’s 4.7.6 landed across three layers, Hygon added amdsmi, and Ascend 910C switched to dispatch. Of the 17 merges, 11 were line switches and fixes, with no new features. Ten days remain until rc0 on 11-30.
  • The operator layer is doing two things: KernelGen generated 43 operators in a day, while 40 commits fixed precision and timeouts across various backends. Coverage is being added quickly, but alignment is slow, making acceptance the bottleneck.
  • A5 has moved from scaffolding to real testing: FlagGems added architecture detection and an empty directory, and FlagGems-vllm merged its first 950DT operator on the same branch with a 4.5x result. Both lines are making the same preparation.
  • Generative hosts have become a pipeline: Native hosts from MetaX, Hygon, and Enflame merged on the same day, and all three PRs left subpar numbers in place as-is. This approach supports subsequent decisions better than a pretty speedup ratio.
  • vLLM 0.32 ties six vendors into a synchronized action: This wave is the most concentrated part of the 261 under review, with MetaX, Kunlunxin, Hygon, Moore Threads, VeriSilicon, and Sunrise all doing the same thing. The merge count of 167 being lower than the submission count of 196 is explained here.
  • Only one hard news item on the news front: On the day the new BIS rules took effect, the share prices of five member units fell sharply together, and Enflame announced ample orders after hitting limit-down. Nothing changed on the component side, but the capital market environment for member units changed together on the same day.
  • To watch: Whether Cambricon 4.7.6 can make it into the 2.3.0 default matrix; whether A5 operators can be backfilled on the 910; whether the six vLLM 0.32 items can all merge this week; and when Intellifusion’s IFWA tuning data will be ready.
