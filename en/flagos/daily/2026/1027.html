Date: 2026-10-26　Source: FlagBLAS PR #154 · FlagBLAS commits

  • Assessment: A backend’s maturity in FlagOS is typically measured in three stages: can run inference, can run training, numerical library self-consistent. XuanTie crossed the first step of the third stage this window; next to watch is whether BLAS2 and BLAS3 follow, and whether the FP8 path (the XuanTie branch of the fp8 GEMM in review on the FlagTrain side) can share the same numerical library.

II. News Coverage and Ecosystem

2.1 Tsingmicro: Open3D-PIMC Goes from Open-Source Debut to a Teacher-Training Classroom in Thirty Countries (10-26)

Date: 2026-10-26　Source: JRJ (reposted by Sohu): From Tech Conference to Classrooms in 30 Countries · Xinhua: Open3D-PIMC Fills a Key Gap in the Domestic Computing Software Ecosystem

  • Project: Open3D-PIMC, jointly developed by Tsingmicro and the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI), is a programming model and open-source software framework for 3D computing chips. It was officially open-sourced at the China Computing Conference in September and is positioned as one of the main Zhongzhi FlagOS solutions for next-generation 3D chips.
  • Technical approach: Developers describe only data distribution and task partitioning; the framework automatically handles the split and invocation of graph compilation and just-in-time execution, thereby adapting models to the hardware. It is built on mainstream ecosystems such as PyTorch, and on the inference side natively integrates with vLLM and SGLang. Highly dynamic operators such as Flash Decoding and MoE retain hand-written optimization entry points. Vendors’ proprietary instruction sets and microarchitecture capabilities are plugged in as plugins.
  • Hardware background: Tsingmicro’s second-generation high-performance cloud AI chip uses 3D memory-compute integration and multi-chiplet packaging to stack memory and compute vertically, cutting data movement distance from millimeters to micrometers and turning latency from the non-deterministic, conflict-prone behavior of centralized architectures into predictable, deterministic behavior at the near-memory side.
  • This period’s progress: The 10-26 report focuses on ecosystem outreach rather than code—the global “AI Open Computing Teacher Development Program” puts Tsingmicro engineers into the classroom, with nearly 50 leading university teachers from more than 30 countries as participants (over 20 from Africa, more than 10 from Asia, and the rest from Europe and South America).
  • Note on figures: The statement that “FlagOS has adapted 20 chip vendors and more than 30 AI chips” reflects the reporting outlet’s wording and is not fully consistent with the official figures of 21 vendors and 35 chips given at the October Open Computing Conference. When citing, it is best to attribute the former to the report and the latter to the conference.
  • Assessment: The value of this story is not “yet another open-source release” but that it demonstrates a new path for the FlagOS ecosystem—first a member organization takes the lead in building a programming model for a new hardware form factor (3D stacking), then plugs it into FlagOS as a solution. Compared with “Day-0 model adaptation,” this is another kind of expansion: “hardware form factor first.”

2.2 MetaX: Full-Stack RLinf Adaptation Merged into the Official Mainline; Thousand-Card Run Completes SFT-to-RL Post-Training Validation (10-26)

Date: 2026-10-26　Source: Tonghuashun Finance reposting MetaX’s official WeChat account · MetaX official news center

  • Event: MetaX completed a full-stack adaptation for RLinf, a large-scale reinforcement learning framework for embodied intelligence. The adaptation has been merged into RLinf’s official mainline, so developers can run RLinf training tasks on MetaX GPUs without changing code.
  • Framework background: RLinf was launched by Tsinghua University and Infinigence AI, together with institutions including Beijing Zhongguancun Academy. It is positioned as the first large-scale reinforcement learning framework for embodied intelligence that integrates simulation, training, and inference, and its results have been accepted at conferences including OSDI, RSS, and NeurIPS.
  • Validation scale: On Xiyun C-series GPU clusters, two tiers of SFT were completed—512 cards and 1,024 cards (128 nodes × 8 cards)—training the Pi0.5 model on the RoboTwin dataset. Success rates on all four task groups matched or exceeded the values reported in the paper, with one group 8.8 percentage points higher; multi-node scaling linearity remained above 97%.
  • Integration approach: Four layers of alignment in plugin mode—a new device manager added to the accelerator abstraction layer and registered in the RLinf registry; environment variables to control multi-card visibility and coordinate with Ray scheduling; communication defaulting to MetaX’s collective communication library MCCL; and a software stack based on MXMACA’s PyTorch and FlashAttention supporting BF16 and FSDP.
  • Roadmap note: MetaX describes this as a continuation of its “upstream first” strategy, following earlier merges into the official mainlines of LMCache and ms-swift.
  • Assessment: This is not a FlagOS component release and should be recorded as an open-source ecosystem move by a member organization. What is noteworthy is the direction—the reinforcement learning training stack for embodied intelligence overlaps in scenario with FlagOS-Robo and the FlagScale training tools on the FlagOS side. Whether a relay pattern emerges in which “member organizations first merge into upstream frameworks, and FlagOS then consolidates a unified backend” is a thread worth following.

  • Test infrastructure: optest’s vendor conflict rules place XuanTie ahead of NVIDIA on the grounds that their device node probing may collide; the rule itself confirms XuanTie is already in CI’s real machine pool.
  • Background: In the 10-23 to 10-25 window, XuanTie completed delivery of the Zhenwu Express edition and had PPU sparse attention under review; this window shifts to the numerical library.
  • Assessment: XuanTie’s activity in FlagOS has held a “one full block per window” cadence for three consecutive windows — inference, attention, numerical library. Next to watch: BLAS2 and BLAS3, and whether the CPU side (optest’s thead vendor key) shares the same check scripts as the accelerator card side.

3.2 Kunlunxin: Three foreach Fusions, In-Place GEMM, and Bitcode Build on the XPU3 Side (10-26)

Date: 2026-10-26　Source: FlagGems PR #7676 · PR #7635 · FlagCX PR #676 · FlagCX PR #677

  • Operator side: Six changes on XPU3 — addmm_ writes GEMM directly into the in-place target, eliminating one intermediate hop; less_equal uses NaN-safe compare-exchange operands; isfinite kernel optimization; and three foreach fusions (ternary scalar, div’s List kernel, log mask removal).
  • Communication side: FlagCX merged Kunlunxin XPU device bitcode build (#676) — a Makefile compiles the shared device API wrappers into xpu3 LLVM bitcode, extracting device modules from the XTDK offload package, retaining public symbols before optimization, and producing libflagcx_device.bc plus consumer-side headers, with communication traits going through XSHMEM. #677 under review fixes flagcxGetVersion decoding for version codes below ten thousand.
  • Fixes: FlagGems fixes logical_and and a registration error; four under review (two cholesky_solve fixes for the complex path and upper-triangular handling, foreach_div’s _result_dtype localization, and batched operators depending on the day’s FlagTree build).
  • Assessment: All of Kunlunxin’s activity this period is about making existing paths faster and more stable — no new features. This matches its form over the previous two weeks: earliest to integrate and broadest in coverage, hence also earliest into long-tail tuning. The bitcode build is the exception — it plugs Kunlunxin into FlagCX’s unified device abstraction, with long-term value exceeding any single-operator speedup.

3.3 MetaX: SGLang Plugin Adds Full MetaX Backend Package, Three Model Adaptations Under Review on the vLLM Side (10-26)

Date: 2026-10-26　Source: sglang-plugin-FL PR #50 · vllm-plugin-FL PR #642 · FlagGems-vllm PR #998

  • Ecosystem side: RLinf mainline merged (see 2.2), its “upstream-first” landing in the embodied intelligence direction.
  • Assessment: MetaX’s focus this period has shifted from the inference plugin to two lines — the full SGLang backend package and upstream framework merges. The SGLang plugin previously had only Ascend, Hygon, Iluvatar, and a few others; with MetaX joining, the vendor matrix on the inference framework side is essentially complete. Next to watch: whether the vLLM 0.28 upgrade aligns with the SGLang 0.5.18 cadence.

3.5 Enflame, Hygon, Iluvatar, Moore Threads: One Structural Change Each Across Four Backends (10-26)

Date: 2026-10-26　Source: FlagPrism PR #17 · Torch-FL PR #654 · Torch-FL PR #660 · FlagTree tags

  • Enflame: FlagPrism merged tops header and library dependency resolution (#17, 16 files, 2,126 lines), making Enflame SDK header and library lookup explicit rather than implicit; FlagAttention has an Enflame-side fused attention operator (fused_attnres) under review.
  • Hygon: Torch-FL fixes the build source for the DCU runtime (#654) — DCU’s runtime_dir previously pointed to a directory holding only ABI shims, so the runtime library was linked from 32 stubs; the build passed but importing immediately raised undefined symbols. The change touches 71 files and 11,000 lines; #627, also under review the same day, wires real streams into boxed DCU builds.
  • Iluvatar: Torch-FL #660 fixes bf16 GEMM falling back to host execution on Iluvatar — a 2048×2048 bf16 mm measured 0.41 TFLOPS, below the same machine’s CPU at 0.54, while the same operator in fp16 hit 80.2 and the vendor’s bundled torch 84.6; the root cause was that the support check omitted bf16 and that mm and addmm copied transposed weights on the host side. After the fix it goes through the vendor BLAS.
  • Moore Threads: FlagTree issued an MThreads-specific tag (0.7.1a1+mthreads3.6); FlagGems-vllm merged the MThreads optimization for chunk_kda (#990); the MUSA backend’s TLE cache persistence change landed last week.
  • Assessment: What the four share is fixing “runs but runs slowly or blows up halfway” problems rather than adding new operators. Such defects are the hardest to reproduce externally and best illustrate the true completeness of the backend matrix — vendor-side submissions still focus mainly on their own platform’s correctness and performance, while cross-platform unified semantics work is still driven by the FlagOS mainline.

IV. Summary and Trend Observations

  • 2.3.0 has been through a complete pipeline once, but the tag still hasn’t been cut: The runtime gates, changelog, rebuild authorization, and version parameterization were in place from 10-23 to 10-25; on 10-26 the application-layer rebuild was authorized and the tag was converged to 2.3.0-0.3.0. The step from “runtime refresh” to “application version follow-up” usually comes right before an official release, so this week remains a key observation window.
  • The backend matrix is expanding rather than converging: A new AMD ROCm 7.2.4 base image was added, indicating that the 2.3 cycle is still adding platforms. Correspondingly, DAMO XuanTie added BLAS1, SpacemiT took on RPM packaging, and Kunlunxin added device bitcode—these four items span three layers: numerical libraries, packaging, and communication, and all are work that “only becomes necessary after integration.”
  • Numerical libraries and the training stack have become the new battleground: FlagBLAS added fifteen BLAS1 operators in one go, and FlagTrain rolled out a batch of operators across seven backends; both lines are accelerating in the 2.3 cycle. The marginal returns on inference operators are declining (FlagGems this period focused mainly on KernelGen auto-generated batches and backend tuning), while the gaps in training and numerical libraries are more pronounced.
  • The vision direction has taken shape for the first time, but lacks performance evidence: FlagCV defines its positioning as “an OpenCV-shaped vision operator library implemented in Triton, benchmarked against the OpenCV CUDA baseline,” built on FlagTree. Currently there is only documentation and a skeleton, with no benchmarks or non-GPU backend data, so it is too early to judge its value.
  • Review bandwidth has become the bottleneck: Submissions up 37%, under review up 23%, merges down 18%, new opens down 18%. The development side is accumulating, while the review side is digesting. The gap between the under-review volume (52 and 9) and the merge volume (49 and 4) is most pronounced in the FlagGems and FlagTree repos, and these two repos happen to be the source of the compiler and operator versions for 2.3.
  • The quantum stack has entered a phase of scenario rollout: Three types of physics scenarios—error-correcting codes, neutral atoms, and thermal baths—are being advanced in parallel, still following the four-stage evidence-preserving workflow. It continues to account for nearly 60% of submissions across the organization, but its daily average has fallen by two-thirds relative to the previous period.
  • For the third consecutive window, the information front has only member-organization press releases: Of 12 candidates, 2 were valid; FlagOS itself had no version release, no new repos, and no external announcements. The main content still comes from GitHub, a typical window of “code filling in backends while communications wait for a milestone.”
  • Limitations: Submission retrieval uses GitHub search’s total_count metric (185 records), which differs from the merged-PR retrieval (133 records) due to squash merges and indexing delays; the case where FlagGems shows a merge count (49) higher than the submission retrieval count (28) falls into this category, and the report uses the merge count as authoritative. FlagQuantum’s 109 records include merge and branch-sync commits and do not equate to 109 feature changes. FlagTree’s 0.7.1a1+mthreads3.6 was cross-checked via both the Release list and the Tag API and confirmed to be a pure Tag with no Release notes. Statements in vendor press releases such as “no code changes required” and “matches or exceeds the paper” are company claims and have not been independently reproduced by third parties. In the SpacemiT article, “20 vendors, 30-plus chips” is inconsistent with the conference’s stated 21 vendors and 35 chips; the attribution has been noted in the main text.
