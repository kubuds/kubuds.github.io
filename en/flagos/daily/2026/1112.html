Today’s Focus: FlagOS 2.3 roadmap published, model compilation elevated to stand alongside operators and frameworks (11-12)

Date: 2026-11-12　Sources: community #176 (2.3 roadmap) · community #163 (FEP-0163) · community #173 (FEP-0163 tracking issue) · community #175

  • Timing and scope: The community published the FlagOS 2.3 roadmap (#176) in the early hours of November 12, with a target release date of December 30, 2026. It lists proposals, tracking issues, and owners by SIG, with checkboxes serving as release acceptance evidence. In parallel, FEP-0163 was merged and marked “in implementation” by its companion PR, with tracking issue #173 carrying the delivery.
  • Three main tracks: Model and framework coverage (vLLM, SGLang, and PyTorch aligned with supported accelerators, expanding to quantized inference, multimodal generation, and training workloads); compiler and runtime performance (FlagMega model compilation, FLIR/TLE capabilities, communication-computation fusion for key models); developer experience and delivery (debugging, profiling, compatibility validation, offline packaging).
  • Version baseline fixed: On the framework side, the requirements are to complete vLLM 0.24 acceptance and make vLLM 0.32 the primary release version for the twelve-vendor matrix; upgrade SGLang to 0.5.18 covering ten vendors and complete Empty-mode operator integration for the Qwen3.6 series, plus validate and optimize W8A8 workloads on NVIDIA and at least one other vendor.
  • FlagMega’s quantified targets: FEP-0163 requires compiling the full Qwen3.8 and GLM5.3 Flash models into MegaKernel, integrating with vLLM’s batch-size-1 decoding, and validating on three backends: NVIDIA, AMD, and Iluvatar CoreX 5.0. Qwen3.8 has a 20% optimization target, and GLM5.3 Flash decoding throughput must exceed vLLM by more than 5% on four H800s.
  • Branch cut milestones: feature/flagmega must be merged into the main branch before the FlagTree release branch is cut on December 7; the model and backend matrix, accuracy criteria, and executable test commands are to be published the same day; release acceptance is to be completed with results attached by December 25.
  • Not yet scheduled: The 2.3 proposals from the five SIGs—operators, networking, training, KernelGen, and chips—remain marked as pending; the Hexagon experimental backend for edge-side llama.cpp is listed as a deferred candidate for 2.3; the OS and packaging track proposes a self-extracting offline installer (FEP-139), starting with vLLM on the general/NVIDIA backend.

3.4 T-Head: PPU 2.1.0 runtime Enters the 2.3.0 Line for the First Time, torch Ecosystem Trio Completed (11-11)

Date: 2026-11-11　Source: build-infra #1738 · build-infra #1740 · PPU 2.1.0 changelog · FlagGems #8508

  • Deliverables: PPU 2.1.0 base image adds a python 3.12 closure with torch 2.10.0, numpy 1.26.4, FlagGems 5.4.0, default compiler FlagTree 0.7.0+ppu3.6, and vendor triton 3.5.0 installed in the same image as a fallback.
  • Completing the trio: torchvision 0.25.0 and torchaudio 2.10.0 cp312 vendor wheels enter the closure, giving PPU the same torch ecosystem combination as other vendor images; the base image adds libjpeg and libpng to support image decoding.
  • Known limitation: The FlagGems release mapping has no PPU backend, so this image does not include the cpp FlagGems wheel, and operator capability depends on the FlagTree compilation path—a substantive gap versus mature lines like NVIDIA and AMD.
  • Terminology clarification: PPU here belongs to T-Head’s AI accelerator card product line and is unrelated to DAMO XuanTie’s RISC-V processor line; in the FlagTree backend matrix, T-Head and DAMO ACADEMY occupy separate rows and must not be conflated.
  • Operator side: FlagGems #8508 optimizes LayerNorm row scheduling on PPU while retaining FP32 statistics—the only operator change tagged PPU across the organization this period.

IV. Summary and Trend Observations

  • Roadmap shifts the narrative focus: 2.3 writes FlagMega model compilation alongside operators and frameworks in the mainline, with two verifiable metrics of 20% and 5%. Previous daily reports’ mainline has been “operator volume plus backend adaptation”; from this period on, an additional layer—”whether full-model compilation meets targets”—must be watched.
  • Version main axis locked in early: vLLM 0.32 and SGLang 0.5.18 are written as 2.3’s primary release versions, and Kunlunxin hit a py3.10 import failure on 0.32 and rolled back the same day—version migration risk has shifted from “whether to upgrade” to “who gets stuck first.”
  • KernelGen structural change: Yesterday’s 130 PRs (MetaX 89, Hygon 20) were concentrated single-vendor volume; today’s 64 (MetaX 46, Moore Threads 9, NVIDIA 4) shift to multi-vendor parallel small batches—single-vendor peaks converge while coverage widens.
  • Organizational cadence halved but actions not light: Commits and merges dropped from 256/245 to 148/148, pushed repos from 21 to 20, but 317 remain under review; the same day opened two dev-version tags, one roadmap, and one implementation-type proposal—a “write less, decide more” day.
  • runtime backend line keeps widening: Yesterday added AMD ROCm 7.2.4, today adds T-Head PPU 2.1.0; the 2.3.0 vendor line now has 24 changelog entries; the common shortcoming of new lines is the lack of a FlagGems cpp wheel, with operator capability depending on the compilation path.
  • Edge side still waiting: llama.cpp’s Qualcomm Hexagon experimental backend is marked as a deferred candidate in the roadmap; 2.3’s edge capability has no hard metrics and will not become the mainline in the short term.
