  • Ecosystem claims: Reports state that the FlagOS ecosystem now brings together 21 chip companies and 86 ecosystem partners, covering 35 AI chips. The nine operator libraries have accumulated more than 1,454 open-source Triton operators, of which nearly 800 achieve over 80% of native operator performance on at least 5 domestic chips. On the Decode side, LI quantized write-back merges dynamic quantization, scale conversion, and HBM cache write-back into a single kernel, cutting the 910C microbenchmark from 18.03 microseconds to 2.04 microseconds. End-to-end ITL drops by more than 10%, and TPOT falls by over 8%.
  • Existing 910B fleet: BAAI Token Hub forms a Prefill/Decode disaggregated cluster from 48 910B cards — 32 cards split into 4 Prefill domains and 16 cards into 8 Decode domains — running GLM-5.3-Flash service with 1M context. Cross-Prefill-domain Prefix Cache reuse raises the hit rate from 82.65% to 94.15%, and cuts median time-to-first-token during the reuse phase from 16.63 seconds to 3.10 seconds.
  • Cross-platform comparison: On T-Head 810E, FlagOS 2.2 improves DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash by 77% to 82%, supports 16-way concurrency in service with up to 80 concurrent requests, and achieves 136.2 tok/s decoding per request. On Hygon BW1100, GLM-5.3-Flash improves by more than 25%, with per-stream throughput exceeding 80 TPS under multi-concurrency.
  • Caveat on claims: The Huanqiu Tech version additionally states that on 910C, GLM-5.3-Flash overall output rises from twenty-plus TPS to fifty-plus TPS, logical service throughput is 2.04x pre-optimization, and unit compute cost is 19.4% to 27.6% lower than commercial API billing; it also claims the auto-tuner improves search efficiency by 298x. 3.1 Moore Threads: Kimi K3.1 Pro Training Adaptation, Dual-Machine 16-Card Convergence in 400 Steps (11-17~11-18)

Date: 2026-11-17 ~ 2026-11-18　Source: Yicai · China Business Journal reprint · EEFocus technical article

  • Event: After Moonshot AI open-sourced the 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3.1 Pro, Moore Threads completed training adaptation based on MTT S5000 intelligent compute cards and the MUSA software stack. It built a native FSDP2 multimodal training path, connecting the full chain from model loading and shard scheduling to gradient synchronization. Yicai and National Business Daily first reported on the evening of 11-17, and China Business Journal added technical details on 11-18.
  • Validation configuration: The trimmed training setup used a 13-layer language backbone, 16 experts (top-8 routing), and the full 27-layer vision tower, with 16 MTT S5000 cards across two machines, starting training from random initialization on MedPix-VQA image-text data. Sixteen processes completed 400 consecutive optimizer updates; training loss converged from 8.9365 to 2.1802, validation loss dropped from 6.5297 to 4.6873, and peak memory was 44.80 GiB.
  • Engineering highlights: Completed forward and gradient implementations for newly added operators, and integrated expert dispatch and combine into a unified computation graph; for parallelism, used a hybrid of FSDP2 and EP with parameter prefetching and gradient communication scheduling; on the loss side, used Chunk Loss blocking (block length 256) to control memory for the large vocabulary.
  • Scope caveat: China Business Journal explicitly noted that training adaptation does not equal pretraining the model on that chip; the two must be described separately. Moore Threads previously completed from-scratch training of the 021 scientific foundation model (236 billion parameters) and, together with BAAI, completed full-process training of the embodied brain model RoboBrain 2.5.
  • Observation: The technical substance of this news lies in “training adaptation” rather than “inference usability,” and the validation scale is a trimmed configuration. It shows that domestic cards are advancing their engineering work on trillion-level models, but there is still a gap to full-scale pretraining.

3.2 MetaX and Enflame: The Chip and SDK Scope Behind Two Line Switches (11-18)

Date: 2026-11-18　Source: base layer changelog directory · metax-maca3.10.1.11 · enflame-tops4.1.6

  • Chip correspondence: The new MetaX line targets C550, while the two Enflame lines target Zixiao C200 (S60) and L600 respectively. The two previously retired Enflame lines had already been replaced with transaction lines on 11-17; that day marked the first delivery of the newly built lines, closing out the same generation switch.
  • Driver strategy: MetaX makes the driver user-space-only installation, with no kernel modules installed inside the container. This aligns with NVIDIA’s approach; the benefit is that images can be built without a card, at the cost that the host driver version must match the image.
  • SDK component surface: The component lists for the two Enflame lines are completely identical, indicating that the difference between the two lines lies only in chip and stack versions, not in capability scope. Triton GCU is 1.0.20261116 for both, i.e., the build from November 16.
  • Directory consolidation: The maca3.7.2.1 image directories in the base layer and runtime layer have been deleted, leaving only historical changelogs; the directories of the old Enflame lines are likewise no longer in the tree. This “delete directories, keep records” approach keeps the build matrix clean while preserving traceability.
  • Observation: The base layer’s generation-switch scope is “one line per card.” Three vendors completed this on the same day, indicating that the platform list for the 2.3 cycle is basically finalized, and what follows is acceptance before rc0.

IV. Summary

  • The base layer was the day’s main thread: FlagOS 2.3’s base and runtime layers completed the MetaX line switch, the first delivery of both Enflame lines, and the retirement of the old Moore Threads line within one day; none of the twenty merges introduced new functionality. With twelve days left until rc0 on 11-30, the platform list is converging.
  • The operator layer is doing two things: FlagAttention added four attention variants at once, while FlagGems-vllm rolled out fused operators backend by backend. The former expands capability boundaries; the latter fills in coverage density.
  • Compiler interfaces are changing generations: The TLE pipeline iterator was removed, and FlagGems, FlagGems-vllm, and FlagAttention all switched to tl.range on the same day. This is a cross-repository interface alignment.
  • The quantum line’s share rose: FlagQuantum and FlagQAI totaled 34 merges, nearly a quarter of the day’s merge volume. The error-correction side is being engineered, while the research side is being interface-ized.
  • Only one main thread in public reporting: The Token factory solution from BAAI and Qingmao Intelligence is the only industry news with substantive content; the rest are partner-side retrospectives and reprints of old news. Component-level version news still only goes through GitHub.
  • To watch: One flagcx line for the old Moore Threads line is still under review; one test case is disabled when the FlagGems-vllm 0.2.0 baseline is enabled on Hygon BW; 2.3’s GA is set for 12-30, with three intermediate milestones: rc0, rc1, and rc2.
