RISC-V Weekly Report (2026-10-12 ~ 2026-10-16)

2026-10-16 Kubuds

Reporting period: Monday, October 12, 2026 to Friday, October 16, 2026 — 5 working days, material from 5 daily issues.

Sources: Daily RISC-V Global Updates (five issues: 10-12, 10-13, 10-14, 10-15, 10-16; the 10-12 issue covers a 44-hour window starting from the previous issue’s National Day holiday make-up cutoff of 10-10 09:20); additionally verified through a 168-hour window review of followed GitHub organizations and key repositories, dual-source verification of openRuyi, and supplementary checks of riscv.org and RISC-V International member official websites; the Ubuntu 26.10 release image was verified against the official cdimage site.

Editorial note: Core items from the five daily issues are aggregated under four dimensions: “Instruction Set & Architecture Ecosystem / Operating Systems & Distributions / Chips & Hardware / Community & Industry Activities”; developments on the same thread across days are merged into a single narrative. Facts, version numbers, and dates are taken from verified sources in the relevant issue or from this period’s hands-on verification; single-media claims and vendor self-statements are flagged in the text.

I. Weekly Highlights

  • DAMO XuanTie C950 runs a 27B large model on a 64-core target configuration without a standalone accelerator; results amplified via an interview on the RISC-V International Foundation website (10-15): The RISC-V International Foundation’s “V Questions” column published an interview with Meng Jianyi, Chief Scientist of XuanTie and Vice Chair of the RISC-V China Summit 2026, disclosing that the XuanTie team natively ran Qwen3.8-27B (27 billion parameters) on a 64-core C950 configuration without relying on a standalone GPU/NPU, achieving a decode speed of over 30 tokens/s (roughly 20 to 22 words/s, reaching natural real-time conversation level); this workload requires about 400 GB/s of memory data movement capability, and the interview emphasizes that quantization, matrix instructions, on-chip memory, bandwidth, and operator optimization must be co-designed as a single system. The C950 is a server-class 64-bit superscalar processor with the RVA23 profile (including RVV 1.0) as its general-purpose computing baseline; after XuanTie’s self-developed matrix extension was silicon-validated, it co-founded the AME (Attached Matrix Extension) task group with members including Qualcomm, IBM, VRULL, ISCAS, and BOSC, and is pushing the specification toward formal ratification. In design, AME uses separate tile registers rather than reusing vector registers, and natively supports INT4/INT8→INT32 and FP4/FP8/FP16/BF16→FP32 mixed-precision accumulation. Caveat: the decode speed is the company team’s own figure, without third-party benchmarks or full-system power data. Source: riscv.org

  • Lanxin Computing completes nearly RMB 2 billion Series B financing; RISC-V + AI server chip enters mass-production validation (10-12 Chinese first / 10-13 English follow-up): Shenzhen Lanxin Computing completed a Series B financing totaling nearly RMB 2 billion (about USD 299 million), with existing shareholders Shenzhen Capital Group, Huakong Fund, and Longqiu Capital increasing their investments, and new entrants including Jiangsu State Investment, Suzhou Venture Capital, GigaDevice, Cowin Capital, CCB International, Yueke Financial, and JiuKun Venture Capital; the funds are directed toward RISC-V CPU mass production, AI heterogeneous processor iteration, and general-purpose computing solutions for telecom operators/financial institutions/cloud service providers. The core product LX500 is a 48-core heterogeneous (32 performance cores + 16 efficiency cores) server CPU with an integrated 75 TOPS (INT8) AI engine, supporting RVV 1.0 and RVA23, and natively compatible with PCIe 5.0 and CXL 2.0; it taped out and lit up in February 2026, and in July the Lenovo Wentian WR5219 G5 carrying this chip rolled off the production line in Hefei (Lianbao), and has been adapted to China Mobile’s BC-LINUX and completed inference validation of Kimi K2.6 and Qwen3.6-35B. Caveat: the financing scale is per Chinese media and DIGITIMES, and the chip has no third-party performance evaluation yet. Sources: Phoenix Net · Pedaily, DIGITIMES

  • RVA23 goes from “specification” to “decidable fact”: 8 kernel profile-bit patches enter riscv/for-next, single-query capability reported by Phoronix (10-11/10-14): An LPC26 trip report discloses that the Linux kernel rva23u64 profile bit series has advanced to v9 (in flight), and the first 8 of the 11 patches in v8 were merged into riscv/for-next by RISC-V kernel maintainer Paul Walmsley on 10-09, with the remaining 3 to be submitted with v9 after incorporating on-site LPC feedback. Four conclusions from LPC26: v9 will add RVA22/RVA20 tiered bits to facilitate gradual fallback in glibc; glibc will switch to building by profile with automatic library selection via glibc-hwcaps (hwcaps sourced from kernel bits, with ifunc reserved for microarchitecture level); the userspace interface for dynamically disabling the V extension will be deprecated, and Samuel Holland (SiFive) sent out a 3-patch series the next day; future extensions with register state (such as matrix) will no longer introduce similar switches. This series has spawned no fewer than 5 mainline series, including the two-year-stalled extension-name case unification and Zicclsm, and ByteDance’s Qingwei Hu’s Zic64b support. On 10-14 Phoronix followed up with a report on the patch for “querying in a single call whether the system is RVA23-compliant” (the approach being a single-bit riscv_hwprobe KEY_BASE_BEHAVIOR: IMA RVA23U64); previously Ubuntu-side tools could only give “probably RVA23”. Sources: LPC26 trip report, Phoronix, LPC26 presentation materials

  • Ubuntu 26.10 officially released, first official RISC-V desktop image lands with the release, baseline explicitly RVA23 (10-15): Ubuntu 26.10 (Stonking Stingray) was released on October 15, and the official cdimage site now provides ubuntu-26.10-desktop-riscv64.iso (4.0 GB), labeled “for 64-bit RISC-V RVA23 and above processors”; the same batch of riscv64 images also includes live-server (1.6 GB), netboot tarball, and preinstalled-server (1.3 GB). This is the first time Ubuntu has provided an official RISC-V image with a desktop environment (previously riscv64 had only server/CLI versions), and Canonical simultaneously provides official support and installation documentation for the SpacemiT K3 Pico-ITX; an experimental Xubuntu Minimal image targets QEMU environments (GNOME takes over 30 minutes to load under pure emulation). A week before the release (10-12), the community published testing guidance on “what to test” along with Chinese media coverage. Significance: the desktop image is the first time the RVA23 baseline has landed in a consumer-downloadable finished product, forming a “specification—kernel—distribution” closed loop with this week’s kernel profile-bit progress. Sources: Ubuntu cdimage 26.10, Canonical Discourse

  • openRuyi firmware line releases on three consecutive days, K3 platform support becomes the main line (10-13/10-14/10-15): The openRuyi project released three versions in the window—“Ruyi Firmware 20261013”, “20261014”, and “20261015”—all centered on SpacemiT K3: the 13th version lands GOP graphics output and EDID modes for the K3 Pico-ITX, PCI option ROM, RTL8127 wired NIC driver, boot selection and console hotkeys, and provides two firmware variants with and without x64 emulation (x64emu) (AMD Radeon RX 6400 verified under x64emu); the 14th version adds K3 dual-cluster CPU mode selection—UEFI Setup offers three tiers: X100 only (default, 8 cores, RVV VLEN 256, H extension retained) / X100+A100 (16 cores) / A100 only (8 cores, VLEN 1024), with the setting persisted to a dedicated SPI sector, read by coreboot before OpenSBI starts, A100-only boot handing SEC from hart 0 to hart 8 via SBI HSM, and the device-tree root compatible string reflecting the mode accordingly; the 15th version adds raw SPI flashing installation documentation, an A100-only mode interrupt routing fix, a System Information page and DRAM list, and an IMG GPU clock device-tree fix. In the same week, the main repo (openRuyi) completed Go 1.27.2/OpenSSL 3.6.5/docker-cli package splitting, a full KDE Plasma 6.7.5 upgrade (19 packages), Mesa 26.2.4 and PowerVR backports, and a security maintenance batch for cups, python-nltk (CVE-2026-81726), capstone 5.0.9, llama-cpp 0.4.1, and multipath-tools 0.15.1. Sources: 20261013, 20261014, 20261015

  • SpacemiT opens up its full on-device Agent stack: Bianbu AgentOS components open-sourced in a batch, llama.cpp standalone backend released twice in a row (10-12 ~ 10-15): After a concentrated round of repo creation on the evening of 10-13, SpacemiT made its on-device Agent system software stack (Bianbu AgentOS) public on 10-14: the desktop BiBit GUI and offline voice Bibit Voice released 1.0Beta1, while also open-sourcing the Agent runtime (bianbu-agent-runtime, YAML-configuration-driven, sessions as event streams, securityd signed manifests and Tool approval, fail-closed in production mode, currently supporting only K3/Bianbu 4.0), the local-first memory service (bianbu-agent-memory, three tiers of L1 session events/L2 working summaries/L3 long-term memory, SQLCipher encryption and namespace isolation), and components such as intent routing and file search; on 10-15 it completed the capability distribution infrastructure—APM (agent-power-manager, consolidating retrieval, installation, and upgrade of the four Power types Skill/Plugin/MCP/Expert into a unified command, Apache-2.0), Bibit Agent Hub (capability center frontend plugin + Go CLI Provider, supporting riscv64 builds), and the Bianbu Skills read-only mirror (11 Skills in the first batch). On the AI inference side in parallel: the llama.cpp branch introduced a standalone ggml-spacemit backend (PR #43, enabled only when both the device backend environment variable and --device SPACEMIT0 are set) and released v0.2.1/v0.2.2 back-to-back (the latter fixing garbled output from Qwen3.5-2B on K1). Caveat: all components are first validated on the single K3 platform, and cross-platform portability remains to be seen. Sources: bibit 1.0Beta1, bianbu-agent-runtime, llama.cpp v0.2.2

  • RISE publishes RVV vector optimization results for SpeexDSP: resampling accelerated up to 4.88x (10-13): The RISE project published the RP020 completion results—introducing hand-written RVV assembly for the real-time audio foundation library SpeexDSP (widely used by VLC, Firefox, Wireshark, PulseAudio, Mumble, etc.): the floating-point resampling kernel is 3.71x (Clang 18)/3.59x (GCC 13) faster than compiler auto-vectorized C, and the fixed-point kernel 2.69x (Clang 18)/4.88x (GCC 13, which failed to auto-vectorize). Thanks to RVV’s length-agnostic nature, the same assembly is effective on both physical platforms, SpacemiT K1 (VLEN=256) and Canaan K230 (VLEN=128); the accompanying Meson + Checkasm validation also incidentally found and fixed an 8-year-old data-dependency defect in the SSE2 kernel. The project was advanced by Google engineers in collaboration with RISE, with RISE funding USD 40,000. Source: RISE Project

II. Instruction Set and Architecture Ecosystem

RVA23 and the Kernel: Profile Bits Merged, Auto-Vectorization Experiments, and “Compliance You Can Query”

  • Profile bits enter the merge pipeline: Of the 11 patches in v8, 8 have entered riscv/for-next (10-09), with the remaining 3 submitted in v9; v9 will add RVA22/RVA20 tiered bits to enable glibc to fall back level by level, and will document the legacy behavior of not inheriting IMA bits. On the glibc side, the approach shifts to profile-based builds plus automatic library selection via glibc-hwcaps; GCC maintainer Jeff Law is pushing in the same direction at the RISC-V BoF at GNU Tools Cauldron; the userspace interface for dynamically disabling the V extension will be deprecated (SiFive’s Samuel Holland has already posted a 3-patch series), and future extensions with register state will no longer introduce similar switches.
  • Spillover series: This series directly or indirectly drives no fewer than 5 mainline series—the two-year-shelved extension-name case unification and Zicclsm were absorbed, ByteDance’s Qingwei Hu contributed Zic64b, do not report disabled extensions (6 patches), cpufeature BUNDLE-to-SUPERSET streamlining, and the V-off deprecation series. This year’s LPC RISC-V MC featured 10 talks in total, 3 of them from Qualcomm.
  • Kernel auto-vectorization: At LPC26, Andy Chiu presented practice on optimizing the kernel via compiler auto-vectorization—LZ4 improved by up to about 15%, Btrfs by about 8.4%, XFS by about 5%, and Nginx scenarios by about 2%, at the cost of increased time and memory overhead for vector context management. RISC-V kernel builds still do not enable the V extension; these results prompt a re-evaluation of the long-standing taboo against using vectors in kernel mode.
  • Query interface: On 10-14, Phoronix reported on the single-bit query scheme (KEY_BASE_BEHAVIOR: IMA RVA23U64, computed once at boot from the full ISA bitmap), initiated as an RFC by Andrew Jones and advanced by Guodong Xu; the accompanying “RVA23 in practice” talk materials have been published on the LPC website. Sources: Phoronix auto-vectorization, LPC26 vector talk

XiangShan: Compressed ROB, Fully Associative vBTB, and L2 Memory Access Latency Advance on Three Fronts

  • Frontend and out-of-order structures: Within the window, the main repo (kunminghu-v3) landed the compressed ROB (crob) architectural feature—implementing the compressed ROB and integrating CSR state tracking, refactoring the compression state and timing paths, carrying event locations for ArchEvent, maintaining ordered store checks via entry offsets, fixing scommit bit width, and expanding the entry count; subsequently merged the MBTB (main branch target buffer) fully associative vBTB (#6587), fixed the MMU ignoring the PTE G bit when V=0 (#6695), selected the narrow-shift bias of the ALU by output format (#6689), and fixed store-queue forwarding of stale store data (#6693).
  • Memory access and cache: On 10-14, merged reduced L2 load-to-use latency (#6713), and fixed ICache exception propagation and bank selection for the fetch-block end offset under data ECC scenarios, and added support for decoding Zimop (MOP) instructions. At period end (10-15), the main repo had no new merges, with branch-level pushes dominating (kunminghu-v2 had 1 ChiselAIA backpressure fix).
  • Simulation and verification chain: NEMU decoupled AME matrix extension instruction dispatch from PMEM access (#1249), accelerated AME matrix hashing, fixed AME matrix store rollback records (#1251) and first-page write preservation for cross-page store page faults (#1240), and released v2026.10.r3; difftest added the MCSim timing backend (alongside DRAMSim3), delta encoding for mcsr/mcfg, lane-parallel CRC-64 store hash verification, GBD1 frozen-bank DMA drain, and trace handshake gating (#990); the GEM5 branch (xs-dev) added Zimop instruction support and TRACERTL/NEMU trace formats, restored macOS builds, and added a manual smoke-test CI (#1228); on 10-15 it further merged the MLP-aware fetch policy, enabled the private MBTB victim cache by default (#1235) and fixed its invalidation under shared writes (#1236), and added a weekly ideal-BTB SPEC benchmark (#1233).
  • Assessment: This week XiangShan’s investment runs in parallel on three fronts—”frontend structures (crob/vBTB) + memory access latency + verification infrastructure (timing backend, hash verification)”—with the verification chain’s engineering maturity advancing faster than public disclosure of core performance data. Sources: XiangShan, NEMU, GEM5, difftest

Architecture Testing and Toolchains: XuanTie ATS Adds BF16 Vectors, ESWIN Open-Sources AME Test Vectors, openKylin’s RVV Gains

  • DAMO XuanTie: The damo-rv-priv-ats privileged architecture test framework merged PR #37, introducing the T-SBI (Test Supervisor Binary Interface) protocol layer and migrating all test suites to the S-mode regular execution model—the boot phase completes M-mode-only initialization and then drops to S(HS) mode before main() to correctly model HS-perspective behavior such as Smstateen-gated CSRs; damo-rv-ats merged Zvfbfmin and Zvfbfwma (BF16 vector) instruction tests (vfncvtbf16/vfwcvtbf16 and vfwmaccbf16.vf/.vv cases plus build scripts), and added four framework documents covering common infrastructure, integer vectors, floating-point vectors, and vector cryptography; damo-rv-priv-ats also upgraded its Sail formal model dependency to 0.15; thead-extension-spec merged 3 commits (RV32 LWUD fix #80, user PR #78, README update #72).
  • ESWIN Computing: Open-sourced the riscv-matrix-gemm-vectors repository, providing a matrix multiply-accumulate test vector set (BF16 × BF16 → FP32) targeting the RISC-V Matrix Extension (AME) specification proposal v0.6.0—each case is a bare-metal self-checking program, accompanied by implementation-independent reference values and a checker; the criterion uses standard rounding error bounds ( Ĉ−C* ≤ γ(G)·Σ a·b ) rather than bitwise comparison, to accommodate the unordered-summation implementation differences permitted by the spec; two cases are also provided where “all compliant implementations produce bit-identical results” to detect incorrect rounding. The suite does not depend on a specific instruction set and can be reused for other matrix extension implementations.
  • openKylin: At Open Source Summit Europe 2026 (Prague, 10-07 to 10-09), disclosed RISC-V-side progress—RVV kernel optimizations speed up ONNX Runtime FP32 inference by 2 to 5 times, and the related kernel optimizations and inference framework adaptations are planned to be contributed upstream; the AI stack supports CPU inference with vLLM on the SpacemiT K3, with delivery forms including deb, Python wheel, Conda, and Docker; a K3 laptop preinstalled with openKylin 3.0 was exhibited on site.
  • Architecture proposal discussion: Community author FProx published an analysis of RVBNA (RISC-V Bulk Normalization Algorithm)—a scalable dot-product approximation scheme centered on a fixed-point accumulator, with round-to-odd rounding in both the product and accumulation stages; it has been written into the ISA manual draft (merged in May), is currently integrated into the Zvt family of the Vector Matrix Extension (VME) and the bulk dot-product extensions Zvdota/Zvbdota, and an error evaluator has been open-sourced. Sources: damo-rv-priv-ats, AME test vector library, MarkTechPost

Open-Source Cores and Ecosystem Projects: OpenHW Continues, Simulation and Tooling Niche Ecosystem Active

  • OpenHW Foundation: A 168-hour check shows pushes within the window to five repositories—cvw, cva6, cv-hpdcache, cv32e40p, and uap (cva6 and cvw remained active from period end through the check)—continuing the design and verification cadence of the CORE-V series; the organization’s repositories have migrated from openhwgroup to openhwfoundation.
  • Others: The Rust-written memory-safe kernel Asterinas 0.19.0 introduced an initial DRM subsystem for the first time and got X.Org running on top of a modesetting driver and Mesa LLVMpipe, while adding an AArch64 target (alongside the existing x86_64, RISC-V, and LoongArch64); riscv.org published a piece on an LFX mentorship program participant’s work, whose self-developed ISA Configuration Builder and Extension Sandbox automatically classify 1,867 instructions into 83 ISA subsets, reducing the error cost of configuring custom cores and custom extensions. Sources: openhwfoundation, Phoronix Asterinas, riscv.org blog

VI. Trend Watch

  • RVA23 has moved from “specification text” to “a determinable fact,” and a three-part closed loop took shape this week: eight kernel profile bits have already entered riscv/for-next (v9 in flight), the single-query interface has been covered by mainstream media, and Ubuntu 26.10 uses RVA23 directly as its image baseline and explicitly states “RVA23 and above.” This means “is this machine RVA23” will go from item-by-item comparison to a single system call, with glibc building by profile plus hwcaps automatic library selection as the next key milestone. If this chain proceeds on schedule, the binary fragmentation problem in RISC-V software distribution will have a systematic answer for the first time.

  • The high-performance narrative has split into two routes, and both gained interim evidence this week: one is “the CPU itself carries AI” — DAMO XuanTie C950, in a 64-core configuration with no dedicated accelerator, runs a 27B model at over 30 tokens/s decoding, relying on system-level coordination of quantization, matrix instructions, on-chip memory, and bandwidth; the other is “CPU + on-die AI engine” — BlueCore LX500 races toward mass production with a 48-core + 75 TOPS single-chip fusion. The former proves the upper bound of feasibility, the latter proves the commercial path, but both lack third-party benchmarks and full-system power data — the evidence most worth adding in the next stage.

  • Distribution competition is shifting from “can it boot” to “platform breadth + firmware experience + security maintenance”: openRuyi has turned firmware into a daily-update cadence (K3 dual-cluster CPU mode selection has already reached the SPI persistence and coreboot read layers), RevyOS consolidated platform configurations for K1/K3/EIC7700/TH1520/SG2042/SG2044 in one pass within two weeks, and Ubuntu participates directly with an official desktop image plus official K3 support. All three treating K3 as their primary validation platform in the same week shows that domestic SoC distribution support has moved from “patch-style adaptation” into “productized delivery.”

  • The on-device Agent stack has become a new track in the RISC-V software stack, but is still single-platform first: SpacemiT went within one week from “centralized repo creation” to a “full-stack open source + capability distribution loop” (GUI/runtime/memory/offline voice/security + APM/Agent Hub/Skills images), and added RGBD launch commands to its humanoid robot MuJoCo simulation. This combination is a first on RISC-V; the risk is that all components currently support only K3 and Bianbu 4.x, so cross-chip portability and the scale of outside community contributors are points to watch.

  • Capital and mass production appeared in the same week, but “funding amount” and “performance delivery” still need to be viewed separately: BlueCore’s nearly RMB 2 billion Series B and Ubitium’s $14 million seed round landed in the same period; the former already has full-system mass production and carrier system adaptation, while the latter is still in tape-out validation and the 2027 evaluation-sample stage. Domestic RISC-V server chips are moving from “power-on” to “mass-production validation”; the next hard metrics are orders, third-party performance reviews, and software-stack maturity, not valuation.

  • Ecosystem event density hit a year-to-date high, and a batch of technical disclosures should follow summit week: RVSC2026 (Oct 18–20) overlaps with the SpacemiT Developer Conference (Oct 17), the OpenAnolis RISC-V sub-forum (Oct 18), the XiangShan Ecosystem Conference (Oct 20), the AME Matrix Extension Workshop (Oct 20), and the XuanTie Beijing stop (Oct 22) — five events in one week covering four main threads: compilers, matrix extensions, automotive-grade toolchains, and on-device Agents. The next weekly report should redo a fact check centered on on-site summit materials, especially the progress status of the AME specification and each vendor’s chip timelines.
