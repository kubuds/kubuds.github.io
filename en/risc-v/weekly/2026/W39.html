RISC-V Weekly Report (2026-09-21 ~ 2026-09-25)

2026-09-25 Kubuds

Reporting period: Monday, September 21, 2026 to Friday, September 25, 2026 — 5 working days, source material from 5 daily issues Sources: Daily RISC-V Global News Briefing (five issues: 09-21, 09-22, 09-23, 09-24, 09-25; the 09-21 issue covers a three-day window including developments from the prior weekend); additionally reinforced by a 168-hour window review of GitHub organizations and key repositories, dual-source verification of openRuyi, and website inspections of member companies Editorial note: This period covers four thematic dimensions: instruction set and architecture ecosystem, operating systems and distributions, chips and hardware, and open-source community and industry activities. Core items from the five daily issues are aggregated by theme; facts, version numbers, and dates all come from verified sources for the respective period plus hands-on verification during this period. Vendor, park, and media one-sided claims are noted in the body text.

1. This Week’s Highlights

  • ESWIN Computing passes HKEX listing hearing, poised to become the HK stock market’s “first RISC-V stock” (09-21): According to HKEX filings, Beijing ESWIN Computing Technology Co., Ltd. passed the main board listing hearing on 09-21 (joint sponsors CITIC Securities and CITIC Securities International), and if the offering proceeds smoothly it will fill the gap in listed vehicles in the RISC-V chip sector. The company uses RISC-V as its technology foundation, with products covering human-machine interaction and multimedia processing chips, interconnect and compute chips, focused on smart terminals and embodied intelligence; revenue for 2023 to 2025 was RMB 1.752/2.025/2.431 billion, with a net loss of RMB 1.516 billion in 2025, and Q1 2026 revenue of RMB 494 million (up 18.4% YoY); compute product revenue grew from RMB 375,000 in 2023 to RMB 320 million in 2025. According to Frost & Sullivan, China’s RISC-V main control chip market grew from RMB 1.1 billion in 2021 to RMB 37.7 billion in 2025, and is expected to expand to RMB 219.4 billion by 2030. On the same day, the company created a new release-EIC7X-2026.06 release branch on GitHub and landed the eic7700_sample repository (see Section 4). Boundary: the hearing still leaves process to go before formal issuance and pricing, and narrowing net losses and the pace of compute product ramp-up are the most direct test indicators. Source: Yicai, Sina Finance / Guandian

  • EVAS Intelligence completes nearly RMB 2 billion financing, the largest single disclosed round in China’s RISC-V space (09-18 news, included in this cycle’s briefing): RISC-V cloud AI compute chip company EVAS Intelligence completed a new round of nearly RMB 2 billion (about USD 295 million), with a post-money valuation approaching RMB 15 billion, and more than 20 investors (including Xinyi Leasing, a sub-fund of the National Big Fund Phase I, Huatai Innovation, Zhongding Capital, and SMIC Capital). On the product side: the first-generation Epoch chip has entered scaled mass production and delivery, with native support for block-wise quantized FP8; the industry’s first RISC-V AI compute supernode (64 nodes, E200-L liquid-cooled OAM modules) has completed cluster deployment at an operator data center in Shijiazhuang; the new-generation cloud chip has been taped out, with an iteration cadence of “one generation per year”; the company is advancing open-sourcing of the VISA virtual instruction set, with a software stack compatible with PyTorch, TensorFlow, JAX, and ONNX, and collaboration with the vLLM, Triton, and FlagOS communities. Boundary: a valuation approaching RMB 15 billion does not equal performance delivery, and subsequent order and revenue cadence are the harder test. Source: Lieyunwang via Tencent News, 36Kr English

  • Monte Cimone v3: Sophgo SG2044 gains third-party HPC energy-efficiency benchmark (09-23): The RISC-V International official blog published an evaluation of the third-generation RISC-V HPC testbed (MCv3) jointly completed by the University of Bologna, Italy’s Cineca supercomputing center, and E4 Computer Engineering: a cluster built on Sophgo Sophon SG2044, measuring power consumption alongside HPL and STREAM benchmarks, benchmarked against Intel Xeon Platinum 8480+ (Sapphire Rapids) and NVIDIA Grace CPU Superchip. Data: SG2044 single-core performance is more than double that of the previous generation SG2042; energy efficiency reaches 3.08 GFLOPs/W (about 10x that of the first-generation testbed), with peak energy-efficiency point performance at 46% of Sapphire Rapids and 91% of Grace CPU Superchip. Significance and boundary: this is the first time a third-party academic team has placed a domestic RISC-V server chip into the same benchmarking framework as international server platforms to provide an energy-efficiency comparison; total performance still lags, and the realistic entry point remains energy-efficiency-sensitive and open, customizable scenarios. Source: RISC-V International blog

  • UPC research: using DAMO XuanTie C910 and on-chip performance counters to let RISC-V chips “self-detect” microarchitectural attacks, with 99% detection accuracy (09-24): A team from the Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC) in Spain completed experiments on real commercial silicon — DAMO XuanTie C910 (RV64GCV) mounted on a Sipeed LicheePi 4A, running Debian 13 — using hardware performance counters combined with machine learning for real-time detection of microarchitectural attacks; the paper was published in the journal Cybersecurity, and the dataset HARPY-V was released (about 64,000 labeled samples, 16 types of microarchitectural attacks including GhostWrite, and 16 benign programs); binary detection accuracy is 99%, and multi-class classification about 95%. The team also disclosed empirical findings: on the C910, monitoring multiple perf counters simultaneously causes mutual interference (some readings inflated by more than 3x), so counters must be collected individually, and perf event descriptions are often inaccurate, requiring reliance on the official manual’s counter mappings. Significance and boundary: one of the first studies completed using commercial RISC-V silicon plus a publicly reproducible dataset; it covers only a single core model, requires retraining across chips, and generalization to entirely new attack types remains to be verified. Source: Scienmag, paper

  • Yunqi Conference: DAMO XuanTie team unveils Zhenwu V900 AI chip, discloses Yitian 720/730/750 CPU roadmap for the first time (09-22): Zhenwu V900 adopts a self-developed parallel computing architecture, 216GB memory, 1,200GB/s inter-die interconnect bandwidth, and single-chip performance three times that of the previous generation Zhenwu M890, with native support for FP8/FP4; it will enter mass production in Q1 2027 and be deployed at scale in Alibaba Cloud data centers, paired with Panjiu supernodes and a self-developed ICN Switch (connecting up to 500,000 chips per cluster); Yitian 720 (192 cores, 96 PCIe lanes) and Yitian 730 (first based on a fully self-developed microarchitecture, supporting SMT, with single-core SPECint2017/GHz up to 1.4x that of Yitian 710) are scheduled for Q3 2027, and Yitian 750 will use a second-generation self-developed core with native ICN interconnect support. Boundary and background: neither Zhenwu nor Yitian is a RISC-V product (Yitian continues to use the Arm instruction set), and they are included under the “domestic core teams” criterion; DAMO XuanTie is a core team in the RISC-V ecosystem, and its RISC-V line (XuanTie C-series IP, the Wujian Alliance, and joint open-source XiangShan R&D) had new developments during the same week (see Sections 2 and 3). Source: ITHome, MyDrivers, TrendForce

  • Automotive RISC-V enters the “mass-production list”: Dongfeng DF30 set for scaled mass production in 2026, Infineon’s next-generation automotive MCUs shift fully to RISC-V (09-21 ~ 09-23): Dongfeng disclosed that the DF30 (jointly developed with China Information and Communication Technologies Group, domestic 40nm automotive-grade process, functional safety up to ASIL-D, Nuclei core) has completed validation in models including Dongfeng Yipai 007, M-Hero M817, and Aeolus Haohan, first deployed in engine ECUs, with plans for scaled mass production in 2026 and the first batch of production vehicles rolling off the line by year-end; the Nuclei core team has expanded to about 500 people, and the chip has completed more than 600 tests. Infineon announced that its next-generation automotive MCUs will shift fully to RISC-V (incorporated into the AURIX family), with its automotive MCU share rising to 36%, and it is working with Vector, Neusoft Reach, and Puhua to adapt AUTOSAR and advance FreeRTOS/Zephyr/NuttX. The final installment of Gasgoo’s series adds the “cost accounting” and cadence judgment: commercial RISC-V core licensing fees are about one-tenth of Arm’s, but commercial IP, tools, and verification environments all require payment, and ecosystem investment is the main barrier (Infineon invested 2 to 3 years ahead); automotive penetration is expected to rise from about 10% in 2025-2026 to about 31% by 2031, following an “edge-to-center” path. Source: Ifeng (Wangtongshe), Ifeng (Wangtongshe), Gasgoo

  • XiangShan processor: vector floating-point instructions merged into the default branch, cache subsystem “Oceanus” L2 advances in parallel (09-22 ~ 09-24): On 09-22 the main repository merged “vector floating-point instruction support” (adding a floating-point vector operation path to the Kunminghu vector unit) and added Zicbop instructions to decode’s uopTable; on 09-23 the XSCache “Oceanus” next-generation L2 development branch advanced 20 commits (L2 port parameterization, AMBA CHI port exposure, StashShared/StashUnique transaction support, and multiple coherence issue fixes); on 09-23 the main repository fixed a pingpong deadlock on loads/stores crossing 16-byte alignment (#6590), and on 09-24 the default branch merged uBTB hit-check timing and CommonHR redirect history maintenance fixes; on 09-25 multiple branches advanced in parallel with fixes and tuning for vectors, TAGE branch prediction, free list refactoring, and load-to-use timing. The simulation and verification chain (NEMU/GEM5/difftest) followed in step (see Section 2 for details). Source: XiangShan commit log, XSCache commit log

  • Aowei Lingxin completes over RMB 200 million Pre-A+ round (09-23): Shenzhen Aowei Lingxin announced the full closing of its Pre-A+ round, with the amount exceeding RMB 200 million, bringing in state-backed institutions such as Suzhou Venture Capital, Guoxin Guozheng, Puhua SME Phase II, and Chengdu Peikun, as well as industrial capital including SigmaStar, Juhe Capital, and Shenzhen Industrial Investment, with existing shareholders participating; the Pre-A series totals more than RMB 300 million, with funds directed toward next-generation chip R&D, team expansion, and commercialization. Its technical path is a “RISC-V foundation + DSA modular configurable heterogeneous architecture,” and its self-developed RV9000 series (up to 24 heterogeneous compute cores, with a built-in national cryptographic security engine) was taped out and lit up on 09-15 (reported in the previous briefing). Boundary: the financing amount and use are per the company’s official announcement, and the chip still lacks third-party performance data and a mass-production timeline. Source: PEdaily

II. Instruction Set and Architecture Ecosystem

XiangShan Processor: Vector Floating-Point Merged into Default Branch, Cache and Frontend Progressing in Parallel

  • Vector floating-point enters main branch: On 09-22, the main repository merged feat(vector): support vector-float instructions, adding a floating-point vector computation path for the Kunminghu vector unit and cleaning up vecRegion connections; on the decode side, the missing Zicbop instructions were added to uopTable (#6586). The vector development branch (0923) continued to fix six items—vector store mask support for the new store path, vector load VL dependency tracking, vle exception handling, vldSNMergeInfo initial value correction, merging old vd on writeback, and cancellation of vstd uops outside the physical store queue—representing final polish of the vector memory access path.
  • Default branch merges and cache subsystem: On 09-23, the main repository fixed a pingpong deadlock on loads/stores crossing 16-byte alignment (#6590); on 09-24, the default branch merged uBTB hit check timing (#6489) and a CommonHR redirect history maintenance fix. The XSCache “Oceanus” L2 branch advanced 20 commits on 09-23: L2 port parameterization (compatible with multiple D-Cache bank combinations), exposing ports externally via the AMBA CHI interface, CHI semantic StashShared/StashUnique transaction support, and coherence fixes including EVT commit, peer metadata writes, and ReplRd returning to I state—the memory system is evolving toward multi-core and coherent interconnect.
  • Frontend and microarchitecture tuning track: The TAGE branch predictor fixed bank conflicts and CommonHR maintenance errors; the free list refactor branch uses bitmap enqueue counting to rotate and refill banks (performance); the ECC RERI feature branch was committed; the load-to-use timing branch fixed specMiss timing; on 09-22 the main repository switched the docker base image to xs-env:stable (#6577), added nightly fuzz to CI, and improved ckpt_select.py (#6604). Overall, the rhythm is dual-track: “vector memory access wrap-up + frontend prediction and cache microarchitecture tuning.” Source: XiangShan, XSCache

Simulation and Verification Chain: Matrix Extension, Prefetching, and Multi-Core Convergence in NEMU, GEM5, and difftest

  • NEMU: Made mseccfg a Kconfig-configurable option (#1221), disabled the virtualization supervisor timer only when nemu_timer is enabled (09-21); changed NutShell unimplemented CSRs to be omitted (#1222), kept CY/TM/IR writable when Zicntr is absent (#1223) (09-22); released v2026.09.r17 (09-23); completed CUTE AMU callbacks and optimized the share-ctrl controller (09-24). Earlier in the window (09-18 to 09-20), there was another batch of AME matrix extension work: detection of reads from undefined tile register regions (#1224), limiting matrix load comparison range to active regions (#1217), SHARE REF marking CSR dirty bits (#1220), and memmove-accelerated GCC register export (#1219).
  • GEM5 (XiangShan branch): PDB (prefetch data buffer) model and replay cleanup, prefetch admission fixes; mem-cache fixed probeHit boundaries and then enabled hit-under-block (#1164); cpu-o3 fixed pflush vset (#1135); SMT added a per-IQ safety watermark policy (#1175); FP division latency aligned across formats (#1167); KMHV3 vector computation issue queue aligned (#1179); L2 low-precision offload made configurable (#1183), H2P page table enabled by default; weekly performance coverage refreshed (#1188) and branches such as Hybrid ROB and MaskRAT modeling advanced.
  • difftest: AME packed events adapted to the NEMU ABI (#962), iotrace preserves the last zstd chunk at EOF (#972), OpenIOMMU file manifest changed to conditional append (#974), and AME MinimalMatrixConfig validator compatibility fixed.
  • Merge observations: matrix extension (AME) boundary semantics, prefetch mechanisms, and multi-core compute alignment are the three main lines of the simulation chain this week; XiangShan’s verification infrastructure continues to do two things: “simulate more accurately, run faster.” Source: NEMU, GEM5, difftest

Compilers, Toolchains, and Ecosystem Libraries: Binary Translation, Vector Backends, and Open-Source IP Tooling

  • felix86 (running x86/x86-64 programs on RISC-V Linux): Five commits on 09-23—updating RIP after x87 operations, fixing a signal race before futex locking, a VK_AMD_buffer_marker graphics thunk, and MSVC string lookup optimization; four commits on 09-24—optimizing the MOV+SHUFPS combination to PSHUFD, fixing a REPL regression, semaphore lock/unlock around fork, and Zydis decode optimization, with game and workload compatibility continuing to be polished.
  • Emulators and specification tools: RVVM merged five floating-point fixes (fcvt dynamic rounding mode, fcsr reserved bits and frm clarification, NaN and sqrt result normalization, involving #259, #260, #280); qtrvsim fixed window sizing and modal event loop issues in the WebAssembly build, and switched packaging for older SUSE distributions to GCC-13; riscv-unified-db (machine-readable specification library) added WFI timeout and U-Mode support parameters (#2526).
  • Vector backends and library ecosystem: simdjson gained an experimental RVV (RVV 1.0) length-agnostic (VLA) backend, with accompanying GCC/Clang validation, runtime dispatch, and reproducible benchmarks (a personal experimental repository; upstream acceptance progress remains to be verified); the Sipeed SLogic toolchain migrated drivers from a private implementation to the shared libslogic library and released slogic16u3-tools v0.1.3 (more detailed flashing verification logs).
  • Open-source IP and build layer: The Bluespec open-source hardware IP library Tape-Out (FukuzSoC) opened a batch of new repositories—three reference SoCs (MCU/Linux/SMP) and components such as PLIC, with each IP including ip.yaml (with measured area), regmap.yaml, and full-configuration-matrix behavioral tests, paired with the self-developed xirang package manager and maturity displayed via a five-level badge system; Tiny-Vedas released the arxiv-v0 snapshot (RV32IM core + 8x8 int8 GEMM accelerator + complete PyTorch JIT to RV32 ELF pipeline, entering the paper version); GNOME Flatpak riscv64 unofficial builds continue to be updated; the Exercism programming platform opened a RISC-V track; the AG32 (RISC-V MCU + FPGA hybrid chip) open-source SDK toolchain AGaMEMnon remains active. Source: felix86, RVVM, Tape-Out, Tiny-Vedas, simdjson RVV

Embodied Intelligence and Robotics: SpacemiT Robotics Stack Continues to Strengthen and “Brain-Cerebellum Fusion” Plans

  • Linglong humanoid (humanoid_linglong): Hip joint configuration fix and retention of legacy policy damping (09-21); corrected dynamics mimic policy and station-keeping policy gain fixes (09-22); integrated the SONIC motion tracking policy (PR #16)—registering the SONIC model and eight optional reference motions, configuring 28-DOF motion mapping and observation inputs; humanoid_common simultaneously added support for SONIC motion selection and RL protection (per-joint target bounds, inference timeout diagnostics) (09-24).

  • Chip planning: At the 2026 Global AI Chip Summit (Shanghai, reported 09-21), SpacemiT disclosed its “isomorphic fusion computing” roadmap—using a unified architecture, unified memory, and a unified software system to allocate CPU compute, AI compute, and real-time compute on demand; the K3 is eight-core at 2.4GHz with 60T of AI compute and supports deployment of 30B models, and a “brain-cerebellum fusion chip” will be developed next. Sources: humanoid_linglong SONIC, ros2_humanoid, Xin Dongxi

International Developments: Openchip, Nordic, and India’s Aheesa

  • Openchip BER10 (included 09-17): An interview disclosed details of Europe’s sovereign RISC-V high-performance application processor—the team was assembled from scratch in early 2024, has more than 350 employees, and completed tapeout in under two years with first-pass success and no respin; the chip is 64-bit, can run Linux, and integrates a vector unit, coherent fabric, and an independent management tile; it is backed by EUR 111 million in support from the EU IPCEI microelectronics program. Its positioning emphasizes “delivering systems” rather than just chips, and it is in discussions with multiple European parties about a sovereign system stack based on seL4.
  • Nordic nRF54LC10A (included 09-17): A multiprotocol SoC with a 128MHz Cortex-M33 main core plus a 128MHz RISC-V coprocessor (for software-defined peripherals and time-critical task offload), supporting Bluetooth LE, Thread, Zigbee, and Matter, offered in CSP29/CSP39 and QFN48 packages, with mass production expected in Q2 2027—another instance of RISC-V entering the mainstream IoT product lines of a major international vendor as an internal control core.
  • India’s Aheesa VIHAAN-I (09-21): The Chennai-based fabless company showcased a domestically developed RISC-V broadband network SoC (28nm, targeting GPON/EPON and telecom infrastructure); tapeout has been completed, MPW manufacturing is planned, and the commercial target is Q1 2027; the project has been recognized under India’s MeitY Design Linked Incentive (DLI) scheme. Caveats: the product is not yet in mass production, and process and performance details are vendor and exhibition framing. Sources: RISC-V International, LinuxGizmos, EE Times

VI. Trend Watch

  • RISC-V has moved from community narrative to capital-market pricing, with the test shifting to delivery and revenue: Within a single week, ESWIN Computing passed its HKEX hearing, Epoch Intelligence closed nearly RMB 2 billion in funding (the largest single domestic round), and Aowei Lingxin completed over RMB 200 million in Pre-A+; combined with Nuclei’s Beijing Stock Exchange tutoring filing and being named in the 15th Five-Year Plan (previous cycle), the capital pricing window for RISC-V has opened. Industrial moves in the same week (EIC7X release branch, EIC7700 example repository, RV9000 tape-out) corroborate each other. The key variables ahead are product delivery and order fulfillment.
  • Third-party quantitative benchmarks are landing densely, with “comparable and reproducible” becoming the keyword of the new phase: Monte Cimone v3 places a domestic server chip (SG2044) into the same HPC benchmark framework as Intel and NVIDIA (peak efficiency points at 46% of Sapphire Rapids / 91% of Grace CPU Superchip), while UPC achieves 99% detection accuracy using commercial silicon plus public datasets — the external narrative has upgraded from “it can run” to “there is comparable data,” and the research side is beginning to consolidate reusable methodologies (datasets, testbeds, tutorials).
  • The automotive side has moved from “discussion” to a “mass-production checklist”: DF30 is scheduled for volume production in 2026, and Infineon (36% automotive MCU share) has announced a full transition to RISC-V for its next generation. A series of analyses brings the discussion down to delivery and cost accounting (commercial core licensing fees are roughly one-tenth of Arm’s, with ecosystem investment as the main barrier), with penetration coordinates from 10% to 31% (by 2031), and 2026 to 2028 as the critical three years for E/E architecture transition.
  • Open-source cores and distributions advance on dual tracks, with pace mismatch as the norm: XiangShan maintains a high development density with “vector memory access wrap-up plus front-end and cache microarchitecture tuning,” while XSCache evolves toward CHI coherent interconnect; openRuyi focuses on security updates and packaging specifications, with the 2026.09 release entering its release window but not yet shipped — the pace mismatch between open-source hardware and open-source software (month-level vs. quarter-level) continues to be the main variable in ecosystem expectation management.
  • The form-factor boundary of edge RISC-V continues to expand: ESP32-S31 brings an MCU vendor close to SBC form (Sv32 MMU, Gigabit Ethernet, Linux BSP preview), Sophgo’s new BM1688 board covers edge vision and storage interfaces, and SpacemiT modularizes the inference backend (ggml-spacemit) — the edge is moving from “it can run” to “it runs well” in the engineering phase; RVV vector acceleration continues to sink from flagship projects into routine optimization targets for general-purpose libraries (simdjson, etc.).
  • Embodied intelligence has become a shared narrative among domestic vendors, with the software stack consolidating layer by layer: SpacemiT’s robotics stack filled in SONIC motion tracking, RGB-D SLAM and Nav2, and motor and peripheral drivers within a week (advancing layer by layer as “runtime interfaces — policy assets — simulation loop — peripherals”), and disclosed plans for a “brain-cerebellum fusion chip”; Suzhou Ruixin binds to the humanoid robot ecosystem with a dataflow architecture; on the academic side, reproducible methodologies (datasets, tutorials) are thickening the infrastructure.
  • Next issue’s focus: Whether openRuyi 2026.09 will be released (including RVA23 integration and cloud-native stack updates); ESWIN Computing’s IPO pricing and EIC7X release cadence; XiangShan XSCache “Oceanus” and vector branch merge developments; the pace at which the ESP32-S31 Linux BSP moves from developer preview to official; upstreaming developments for SpacemiT’s ggml-spacemit backend.
