RISC-V Weekly Report (2026-09-30 ~ 2026-10-07)

2026-10-07 Kubuds

Reporting period: September 30, 2026 (Wednesday) to October 7, 2026 (Wednesday), National Day holiday special summary period (8 days); 10-01 to 10-07 was the National Day holiday, with daily reports suspended. Material is based on the 09-30 daily report, with holiday-window developments supplemented through public sources and repository verification (coverage through publication before noon on 10-08) Sources: RISC-V Global Developments Daily (09-30 issue); GitHub organizations and key repositories 216-hour window review (including Atom fallback); openRuyi dual-source verification; member company website inspection (391 companies); Google News Chinese and English multi-query 9-day window search (approximately 86 candidates) Editorial note: This period covers four thematic dimensions: instruction set and architecture ecosystem, operating systems and distributions, chips and hardware, and open-source community and industry activities; media-only claims and corporate press releases during the holiday are noted in the main text

1. This Week’s Highlights

  • openRuyi 2026.09 officially released: RVA23 support advances on three fronts, cloud and storage stacks expanded (09-30): The RISC-V rolling distribution led by the Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences, released its September edition on schedule. RVA23 improvements cover three fronts—compilation correctness (GCC 16.2 backports BCLR fix, resolving non-terminating m &= m - 1-style loops under zbb/zbs and Mesa graphics crashes), data plane (DPDK 25.07 built with rva23u64 enables RVV vector paths, covering lpm/fib/node/ACL table lookups), and containers (seccomp allows riscv_hwprobe; on SpacemiT V100, OpenSSL detects Zvkned/Zvknhb/Zvksed vector crypto extensions, and zlib-ng enables Zbc crc32). The distribution adds Cloudpods 4.0.3 multi-cloud platform, SPDK 25.09, and Ceph snapshots; it includes the NVK open-source NVIDIA Vulkan driver for the first time and upgrades the Go 1.27 build environment. GPG verification is enabled by default in the repository, and 15 groups of CVEs including expat, jq, vim, imagemagick, pcre2, runc, and crun are fixed in bulk. An experimental RVA20 version (Server/Container/Desktop) is also provided. Source: openRuyi 2026.09 Release

  • “First RISC-V stock” ESWIN Computing wraps up IPO subscription: ~4.5x oversubscribed, expected listing on 10-09 (10-01 ~ 10-07): Beijing ESWIN Computing (01256.HK) launched its global offering on 09-28 (1,570,204,000 H shares, offer price HK$1.48–1.59, raising up to ~HK$2.5 billion, 2,000 shares per lot), with subscription closing on 10-06. As of the afternoon of 10-05, margin financing reached ~HK$1.37 billion, roughly 4.5x oversubscribed relative to the public offering size; 9 cornerstone investors subscribed a total of ~HK$1.16 billion (including Hefei Construction Investment, Greater Bay Area Homeland Investments, ORIX, etc.). The company disclosed 620+ IP modules, 20+ series of RISC-V cores, 1,740+ patent applications, 150+ products, and 220 customers; approximately 35% of proceeds go to new chip development and 30% to the RISAA (RISC-V+AI) platform. Sources: Securities report (10-06), Guandian (10-05), Sina Finance (10-01)

  • International narrative intensifies around RISC-V mainstreaming: MIPS collaboration with China, 25% share, and SPMP standard (10-03 ~ 10-06): During the holiday, multiple media outlets reported intensively—an SCMP feature said MIPS is advancing RISC-V standardization with Chinese companies “as never before” (CTO Yankin Tanurhan stated at a September media briefing in Shanghai; the company previously completed ARC integration and anchored itself in RISC-V Physical AI); digitimes followed up on 10-06 with “MIPS deepens RISC-V ties with China”; MyDrivers/Sina on 10-05 summarized data: RISC-V International institutional members come from 37 countries, with 89/87 from China and the US respectively (~42% combined); Chinese Academy of Engineering academician Ni Guangnan disclosed at the 2026 XuanTie RISC-V Ecosystem Conference that RISC-V has captured 25% of the global processor market, with global shipments exceeding 10 billion units; the report also mentioned that the privileged-state physical memory protection (SPMP) instruction set standard led by a Shanghai Jiao Tong University team was approved for inclusion in the RISC-V ISA standard on 08-31. Sources: SCMP, digitimes, MyDrivers via Sina

  • Efinix releases Sapphire RV64: 64-bit RISC-V soft core brings Linux into FPGAs (reported 10-03): Configurable 1 to 4 cores, 7-stage pipeline (RV64IM, optional A/F/D/C/Zba/Zbb/Zbs/Zicbom), 4KB to 512KB on-chip RAM, multi-way L1 cache and optional 64 to 512KB L2, optional SV39 MMU, supporting DDR3/HyperRAM/LPDDR4x (up to 3,200Mbps), retaining custom instruction capability to offload signal, image, and AI compute; covers Titanium, Topaz, and Trion T20 and above devices, with software flow requiring Efinity 2026.1 or later. It forms a “hardened + configurable” dual track with the 32-bit quad-core RISC-V block already hardened in Ti375 devices. Sources: LinuxGizmos, Efinix product page

  • ESP32-S31 official Linux branch enters developer preview (10-07): i-programmer reported that Espressif’s official espressif/linux repository added an ESP32-S31 support branch (integration/v6.18-esp32s31, marked developer preview, not recommended for production yet); the S31 is a dual-core RISC-V (320MHz) with two-level page table address translation and privilege-level isolation, providing the hardware foundation for running full Linux on an MCU-class chip. The community also has grieferpig’s unofficial 6.18 port (with bundled binaries, device tree customization, libgpiod, and BusyBox). Memory size and the lack of a GPU remain the main limitations; the “official fork” itself is a roadmap signal. Sources: i-programmer, espressif/linux, unofficial port

  • XiangShan doesn’t stop for the holidays: feature branches advance on multiple fronts into the morning of release day (09-30 ~ 10-08 morning): The main repository’s default branch saw no new merges during the holiday window (the latest being the bosc IOMMU integration #6560 on 09-28), but feature branches remained active—my-crob-difftest completed the CROB slot sharing fix and advanced to 10-07; feat-mmu-h-tlb-compression fixed duplicate memory broadcasts in PTW after H-TLB sector compression (10-03); frontend-bt completed funcov runtime integration and merged with mainline (10-08 morning); victim-btb continued mbtb refactoring (through 10-08 morning). The simulation chain also converged: NEMU landed a CSR shadow invalidation fix (#1237) and AME matrix hash comparison (#1238), and difftest added matrix result hash reporting (#978). Sources: XiangShan branches, NEMU, difftest

  • SpacemiT robotics stack continuously reinforced over the holiday: operator pipeline and on-device voice take shape (10-01 ~ 10-03): humanoid_linglong added operator startup and guidance services (#17), fixed-base arm demo configuration files (#18), and split startup services by control configuration (#19); humanoid_common added operator services and remote clients (#22) and fixed-base trajectory control (#23); omni-agent connected on-device KWS wake-up with SPV full-duplex software AEC (#19, with automatic model download); model-zoo-kws launched a streaming wake-up engine and added confusable-word rejection (10-02). Sources: humanoid_linglong, omni-agent, humanoid_common

  • Allwinner V861 board goes on retail sale: first V861-based Avaota F2 listed (reported 10-02): CNX updated its report confirming the Avaota F2 is now on sale on AliExpress ($51.59)—dual-core XuanTie C907 (up to 1.4GHz, RVV 1.0) + XuanTie E907 (800MHz), 128MB on-chip DDR3, 1 TOPS NPU, 4K H.265 encoding, PTZ motor control, and dual MIC plus speaker; hardware materials are open-sourced under CC0 (schematics, PCB, and Gerber all available), with the software side following the Allwinner Tina SDK (OpenWrt 21.04 branch + Linux 6.6). Sources: CNX Software, AvaotaF2 hardware repository

II. Instruction Set and Architecture Ecosystem

XiangShan Processor: Default Branch Silent Over the Holiday, Feature Branches Advancing on Multiple Fronts

  • Default branch and review queue: The Kunminghu default branch saw no new merges during the holiday window; the latest is the 09-28 bosc IOMMU integration (#6560). Backend fix PRs opened on 09-29 (#6649 preserving the FEX0 output slot for x0 conversion, #6652 vector std issue window, #6653 inverting the StdExeUnit out-of-bounds condition) await review progress after the holiday.
  • Feature branches (development lines spanning the holiday): my-crob-difftest completed two CROB fixes (single-slot multi-member sharing of robIdx/uopIdx, restoring mainline compatibility and slot retirement, 09-30), and on 10-07 cleaned up redundant bit widths in fusionNum/MaxUopSize; feat-mmu-h-tlb-compression, building on H-TLB sector compression (09-29), fixed duplicate memory broadcasts in HPTW responses (10-03); frontend-bt completed native Toffee coverage runtime integration and funcov cycle snapshot optimization (09-30), merging with mainline on the morning of 10-08; victim-btb advanced mbtb renaming and comment refactoring (through the morning of 10-08). Branches such as test-mbtb, timing-fense, and fix-flt have seen no new commits since their last entries on 09-29.
  • Source: XiangShan branch list, default branch commit history

Simulation and Verification Chain: AME Matrix Hashing Runs Through NEMU and difftest

  • NEMU: On 09-30 fixed CSR shadow copy invalidation during implicit state updates (#1237); on 10-03 added AME matrix hash comparison (#1238); on 10-04 added support for reading large zstd checkpoint images (#1239).
  • difftest: On 10-01 fixed MinimalMatrixConfig validator compatibility (#976); on 10-07 added AME matrix result hash reporting to DiffTest (#978)—forming a closed loop with the NEMU side for “comparable matrix extension results.”
  • GEM5 (XiangShan branch): No new commits during the holiday window (the previous batch was the apf-align prefetch alignment line).
  • Observation: AME (matrix extension) verification has moved from boundary-semantics fixes into a “comparable result hashing” phase, laying the groundwork for simulation consistency of matrix instructions.
  • Source: NEMU, difftest, GEM5

Architectural Testing and Toolchain Ecosystem: XuanTie ATS Continues, mold 3.0, and a Small Simulation Ecosystem

  • XuanTie architectural test suite: damo-rv-priv-ats refactored the Hypervisor suite’s common infrastructure on consecutive days, 10-05 and 10-07 (pushing duplicate helpers down into common, centralizing suite-level shared facilities); damo-rv-ats updated vector floating-point test cases on 10-04 (#6).
  • Toolchain: mold 3.0 high-speed linker released (rewritten in Rust), fixing RISC-V relocation and TLS-related issues (reported 10-05).
  • Small simulation ecosystem: rv32emu’s Buildroot ext4 root filesystem generation pipeline (included 09-29); BasisResearch’s bare-metal verification practice for the Sail RISC-V model (Lua/OCaml, included 09-29).
  • Source: damo-rv-priv-ats, damo-rv-ats, mold 3.0 report, rv32emu

Open-Source Cores and Confidential Computing: CVW’s 20 Commits Over the Holiday, CoVE PoC Open-Sourced, GSys-LibreCore Debuts

  • CORE-V verification model cvw: 20 commits within the window (latest merge #1924, 10-06), covering a set of fixes for CBO cache block operation permissions, CSH script fixes, WFI back-to-back timing rollback, deferred exception, and spill bytemask.
  • CoVE (Confidential VM Extension): cove-dp1-poc under the DAMO XuanTie GitHub organization released v0.2.0 on 09-30 (the initial open-source version, including a TSM reference implementation written in Rust and a QEMU RV64GC demo environment); on 10-01 the component was renamed from cove_tsm_poc to xvm2.
  • GSys-LibreCore: A Linux-class configurable out-of-order/in-order multicore plus AI processor design derived from CVA6 was released on HN (09-30); the RTL uses a combination of source-available licenses such as CERN-OHL-S, with an MIT toolchain, and a commercial license is also offered.
  • Source: cvw, cove-dp1-poc, GSys-LibreCore

  • Sources: Codethink article, Summit website, SingNova report

Community Projects: PasRISCV Gains Traction and OpenC6 v2.0

  • PasRISCV: The RV64GCV/RVA23 emulator written in Object Pascal continues to attract attention—on 10-04, a Chinese-language article dissected its use in driving in-game Linux in the game “SEEDS,” and on 10-07 the Adafruit blog reposted an introduction.
  • OpenC6 v2.0: The bare-metal BIOS and RISC-V microkernel project for ESP32-C6 released v2.0 (10-01, Show HN).
  • Sources: PasRISCV, OpenC6

VI. Trend Observations

  • No pause during holidays; development cadence decoupled from statutory holidays: XiangShan feature branches (my-crob-difftest, frontend-bt, etc.) and the SpacemiT robotics stack continued to advance during the holiday period, with openRuyi even rolling out a new batch including Python 3.14 on the morning of 10-08—the observation that “official channels go silent while code channels stay active” held true again during the long holiday, as the engineering cadence of institutionalized open source teams no longer pauses with holidays.
  • openRuyi’s monthly rhythm takes shape; RVA23 moves from “declared support” to “fixed until usable”: In 2026.09, issues that actually block users—such as BCLR miscompilation and container hwprobe—were closed one by one, and an experimental RVA20 version was provided for the first time—parallel dual baselines mean the distribution has begun conducting comparative experiments outside the RVA23 mainline.
  • Capital narrative and industry narrative resonate; “mainstreaming” becomes a cross-language media theme: ESWIN’s IPO approaching pricing and listing, the 25% share and ten-billion-unit shipment figures, and MIPS’ statement on China cooperation all appeared in concentrated fashion around the National Day period; the first-day performance of the 10-09 listing will be the first test point on the capital side.
  • Hardware form factors continue to expand: FPGA soft cores (Efinix RV64), camera SoCs (Allwinner V861), MCUs approaching Linux capability (ESP32-S31)—RISC-V’s presence in “non-general-purpose CPU” form factors is strengthening, and the experimental ground for hardware-software co-design is extending from single boards to programmable logic.
  • Verification and security become shared infrastructure keywords: AME matrix result hashing landed synchronously in NEMU/difftest, XuanTie open-sourced CoVE PoC (Rust TSM), and SPMP entered standardization—”verifiable, isolatable” underlying capabilities are the prerequisite for the next stage of productization.
  • Watch in the next issue: ESWIN Computing’s first day of listing on 10-09; RISC-V Summit China (10-18 ~ 10-20) agenda and on-site announcements; openRuyi 2026.10 and RVA20 experimental version feedback; XiangShan my-crob-difftest/frontend-bt merge trends with mainline; ESP32-S31 Linux BSP promotion timeline.
