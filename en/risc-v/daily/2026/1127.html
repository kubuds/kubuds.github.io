Today’s Focus: XuanTie IP cumulative shipments exceed 6 billion units; Android 17 officially supports RISC-V (11-26)

The 2026 Integrated Circuit Development Forum (Beijing) and the 32nd Integrated Circuit Design Industry Exhibition (ICCAD-Expo 2026) were held November 19–20 in Beijing Yizhuang. Meng Jianyi, Chief Scientist of Alibaba DAMO Academy, delivered the keynote “XuanTie RISC-V IP Technology Innovation and Scaled Application” at the event.

The industry data presented in the talk: after more than a decade of development, RISC-V now has 125 extensions and over 1,000 instructions, with applications extending from embedded systems to high-performance computing, AI, and cloud servers.

The stated figure for XuanTie IP deployment is cumulative chip shipments exceeding 6 billion units, covering IoT, industrial control, and automotive scenarios. The talk also mentioned the newly released XuanTie E908A, which supports the ASIL-D automotive safety level. According to Chip List reporting, Meng Jianyi also stated that Android 17 now officially supports the RISC-V architecture.

Old and new information must be distinguished. The 6 billion unit figure was first announced on October 19 at the RISC-V China Summit in Shenzhen; this instance is a restatement. The new information this time is the deployment claims for Android 17 and E908A.

Android 17’s support for RISC-V has independent corroboration on the open source side. The RISE Project published an announcement on October 20 titled Android 17 Available on RVA23 with RISE. Records from the RISC-V Android SIG show that on October 8 the group’s chair reported that Android 17 had booted and was running on the SpacemiT K3 and A210 development boards.

This does not mean RISC-V Android phones will hit the market soon. Getting the AOSP mainline working is the system foundation; adaptation of main chips, applications, and third-party SDKs still takes time. The scenarios most likely to land first are industry terminals, lightweight tablets, and automotive Android.

RISC-V 100 Leaderboard Updates

XSCC 2.0’s SPECrate2017 results on LineShine LX2 enter the leaderboard, lower than LLVM on the same platform (11-26)

riscv100.top added a new entry on November 26: the 256-copy SPECrate 2017 Base results for XSCC 2.0 Opter on LineShine LX2.

The announced figures are 381.00 for integer (estimated) and 430.00 for floating point (estimated). The previous LLVM 23.1.0 results on the same platform were 422 and 452, so XSCC is 9.7% and 4.9% lower, respectively.

The leaderboard displays by Ratio/GHz, with corresponding records of int 245.806 and fp 277.419.

This record belongs to the cross-architecture channel; the platform under test is not RISC-V. LineShine is the CPU-only supercomputer at the National Supercomputing Center in Shenzhen, which topped the TOP500 in November for the second consecutive time; LX2 is a 304-core Armv9 processor clocked at 1.55 GHz. The test used two LX2 processors and 256 copies, with all 23 subtests run with Base tuning and ref input sets.

For the XiangShan compiler team, this is a same-platform comparison. On the Arm platform, XSCC 2.0’s integer throughput is 9.7% lower than LLVM and floating point is 4.9% lower.

Date: 2026-11-26 Source: https://riscv100.top/whats-new/. See also: https://riscv100.top/zh/cross-arch/spec17/.

I. Open Source Community and Software Ecosystem

RVA23.1 and RVB23.1 Merged into ISA Manual (11-25)

PR #3298 in riscv-isa-manual (RVA23.1 and RVB23.1 v1.0 (Ratified)) has been merged, with a merge time of 22:20 UTC on November 24, i.e., 06:20 Beijing time on November 25.

The two profiles were ratified in October. The RISC-V Board approved RVS-4180 and RVS-4184 on October 20, 2026, and the TSC’s submission vote passed on September 24.

These are minor version releases. They only add optional extensions and do not change the mandatory set, so existing binary compatibility is not broken.

The new optional extensions in RVA23.1 include Svrsw60t59b (PTE software-reserved bits), Ssdbltrap (Double Trap), Sscfg (supervisor-mode counter delegation), Ssctr (control-flow recording, implying Sscsrind), and Ssqosid (QoS identifier).

Date: 2026-11-25 Source: https://github.com/riscv/riscv-isa-manual/pull/3298. See also: https://tech.riscv.org/development/.

II. Product and Ecosystem Updates

2026 RISC-V Industry Development Conference Opens Registration, December 10–11 in Zhangjiang, Shanghai (11-25)

The 2026 RISC-V Industry Development Conference (RVIDC) will be held on December 10–11 at the Haike Hall of the Shanghai Zhangjiang Science Hall, and registration is now open.

The organizers are the RISC-V Working Committee of the China Electronics Standardization Association and Shanghai Zhangjiang Hi-Tech Park Development Co., Ltd., with the theme “Standards as the Foundation, Innovation as the Guide.” The conference aligns with the RISC-V R&D and industrialization initiatives outlined in the “15th Five-Year Plan for the Development of the Electronic Information Manufacturing Industry.”

The agenda spans three half-day segments. The morning of December 10 features the opening ceremony and summit forum, including academician reports, achievement releases, and international collaboration sessions; the afternoon hosts the technology and industry forum. On December 11, seven thematic forums run in parallel, covering high-performance computing and AI, intelligent hardware and robotics, intelligent IoT and embedded systems, automotive electronics, RISC-V and DSA, EDA, talent development, and foundational software and ecosystem.

An ecosystem exhibition area of approximately 2,000 square meters will run concurrently, with about 25 company booths and participation from more than 20 open-source communities and research institutions.

Date: 2026-11-25 Source: https://www.ijiwei.com/n/1146459.

Meituan-Affiliated Company Invests in Lingrui Zhixin, Registered Capital Increased to RMB 7.9157 Million (11-26)

Qichacha shows that Shanghai Lingrui Zhixin Computing Technology Co., Ltd. has undergone a business registration change, with Meituan-affiliated Hanhai Information Technology (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. and others added as shareholders, raising registered capital to RMB 7.9157 million.

Founded in early 2025 and headquartered in Zhangjiang, Shanghai, Lingrui Zhixin develops fully self-designed RISC-V CPU cores, chiplets, and domain-enhanced processors.

On November 13, the company announced the completion of a Pre-A+ round worth hundreds of millions of yuan, led by Meituan’s strategic investment arm, with participation from a JD-affiliated fund, Ganbei Fund, SenseTime Guoxiang Capital, Shangqi Capital (SAIC CVC), and Amber Capital (Sangfor). Cumulative funding has reached nearly RMB 1 billion.

The core of its product lineup is the server-class agent CPU core P100. It employs dynamic simultaneous multithreading (SMT4), supporting up to 4 threads per core with switching between SMT1 and SMT4; the pipeline exceeds 20 stages with 8-wide decode and 10-wide issue. A 128-core server-class CPU based on P100 is planned for tape-out in 2027 and mass production in 2028.

Date: 2026-11-26 Source: https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/t/2026-11-26/doc-inizzsip3815206.shtml. See also: https://www.chinaventure.com.cn/news/80-20261113-394035.html.

2026 RISC-V Advocate Awarded to Tobias Scheipel (11-25)

On November 25, riscv.org announced the 2026 RISC-V Advocate, awarded to Tobias Scheipel of Graz University of Technology (TU Graz).

His achievements for the year include three items. First, co-hosting the inaugural RISC-V Community Challenge with RISC-V International, built around the university’s open processor design lab HaDes-V, which drew hundreds of registrants and awarded 28 gold medals. Second, leading hands-on workshops at the RISC-V Summit Europe in Bologna and organizing an online design showcase in August. Third, hosting the Graz stop of World RISC-V Day at TU Graz in February, giving students exposure to RISC-V chips designed by local researchers and to real-world applications at companies such as NXP.

Date: 2026-11-25 Source: https://riscv.org/blog/2026-advocate-year/.

Tang Dan: Inference Inflection Point Creates a Window for RISC-V, with Bottlenecks in Integration (11-26)

On November 26, the RISC-V+AI Emerging Computing Summit 2026 was held in Optics Valley, Wuhan. Tang Dan, dean of the Beijing Institute of Open Source Chip and current chairman of the RDI Alliance, delivered remarks at the summit.

His assessment: “AI is at an inflection point in the shift from training to inference. Training competes on peak performance; inference competes on efficiency.”

The evaluation metrics for the inference stage change accordingly. Response latency, unit cost, energy consumption, and deployment density directly determine business viability, with efficiency replacing peak performance as the constraint. Chip architecture flexibility and customizability therefore become more critical.

On industry bottlenecks, he stated that the issue is not any single technology but three integration gaps: chip and software, product and use case, and enterprise and ecosystem.

Date: 2026-11-26 Source: https://www.163.com/dy/article/LCMVE27Q051288FS.html.

III. In-depth Look at Domestic Teams

XuanTie Software Stack: 148 GCC Patches and 172 LLVM Patches (11-26)

At the IP and IC design sub-forum of ICCAD-Expo 2026, DAMO Academy disclosed XuanTie’s software-side progress.

On the toolchain side, XuanTie GCC and LLVM achieve 1.4x to 2x the CoreMark and Whetstone performance of the open-source versions. A cumulative 148 GCC patches and 172 LLVM patches have been contributed to the community.

The AI software stack is named TORQ, supporting frameworks such as PyTorch, vLLM, and SGLang, covering models including Qwen, DeepSeek, and Llama plus more than 100 operators. The official claim is over 90% hardware utilization for typical operators.

Ecosystem partnerships are organized by scenario. The Ruyi community corresponds to kernel upstream, OpenHarmony to endpoint AIoT, and OpenKylin to the desktop ecosystem. Ubuntu corresponds to the first RISC-V LTS release, and OpenAnolis to cloud servers.

Two other talks were held at the same event: Chen Chen, head of XuanTie processor R&D, spoke on “From High-Performance Computing to AI-Native: The Architectural Leap of XuanTie RISC-V Processors,” and senior technical expert Shang Yunhai spoke on building full-stack software capabilities.

IV. Special Chapter: Embodied Intelligence and Robotics

SpacemiT Robotics Stack: 18 Commits; G1 Gains MediaMTX Camera Gateway and Voice Greeting (11-26)

spacemit-robotics had 18 commits across three repos in the window: 13 in humanoid_unitree_g1, 4 in ros2_humanoid, and 1 in agent-tools-mcp.

The G1 application package targets the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, with 29 degrees of freedom (12 in the legs, 3 in the waist, 14 in the arms). Its architecture supports sim2sim cross-machine inference: simulation runs on a PC while reinforcement learning inference runs on the K3 board, with the two communicating via shared memory and UDP.

The capabilities added in the window center on deployment and interaction. One commit installs the MediaMTX camera gateway for G1, generated per target architecture (#33); one adds a voice greeting paired with Sonic and SuperMimic gestures (#32); one adds a continuous greeting motion sequence (#34). Another commit restricts motions such as warm-up, Tai Chi, and Gangnam Style to the simulation environment to prevent them from being run on the real robot by mistake.

On the ros2_humanoid side, a configurable minimum angular velocity, an independent ZeroMQ RGB-D preview, and MediaMTX installation documentation for the navigation board were added (#3).

One commit in agent-tools-mcp switches the system_monitor tool to @wraps and removes redundant kwargs.

Date: 2026-11-26 Source: https://github.com/spacemit-robotics/humanoid_unitree_g1/commits/main. See also: https://github.com/spacemit-robotics/ros2_humanoid/commits/main.

V. Summary and Trend Observations

The day’s information falls into three threads.

The standards thread is the merge of RVA23.1 and RVB23.1 into the ISA manual. Minor versions add only optional extensions, indicating that the profile system is catching up with the approval pace of new extensions on an annual cadence.

The endpoint ecosystem thread is Android 17 and RISC-V. XuanTie stated “official support” at the conference, and the RISE and Android SIG records corroborate this on the open-source side. The next observation point on this path is CTS and VTS pass rates on real hardware.

The capital thread is Lingrui Zhixin securing a Meituan equity investment. An internet company entering RISC-V server CPUs as industrial capital is two nodes of the same event as November’s Pre-A+ round.

Three judgments.

First, compilers remain the weak spot among comparable metrics. XSCC 2.0 is about 10% slower than LLVM on the Arm platform, indicating that XiangShan’s software stack has not yet caught up to general-purpose compiler levels. This gap is equally worth tracking on the RISC-V platform.

Second, the density of industry conferences is rising. The Shanghai RISC-V Industry Development Conference on December 10-11 echoes the RISC-V+AI Summit in Wuhan, with both agendas pointing to inference scenarios.

Third, the quiet surface is on the hardware side. There were no new chip releases in the window, nor any distribution releases; open-source activity centered on packaging and configuration.
