Today’s Highlights: RevyOS adds kernel support for Zhihe Computing A210 (Melon Pi), batch of patches in place

Date: 2026-09-29　Source: revyos/linux commit: MAINTAINERS adds Zhihe A210 support (09-29), commit: A210 thermal and watchdog device tree (09-29), commit: PWM reuses TH1520 driver to support A210 (09-29), commit: Melon Pi V2 audio routing (09-29)

On the morning of September 29, the RevyOS kernel repository (revyos/linux) pushed a batch of patches to the rv/a210-upstream-local branch (14 commits visible in the window), completing kernel support for the Zhihe Computing “Archimedes” A210 SoC and its Melon Pi V2 development board. The A210 is an eight-core RISC-V “unified training-inference” chip (12 TOPS@INT8) equipped with DAMO XuanTie C920 and C908 cores, targeting edge servers and embodied intelligence scenarios. This batch of patches covers: MAINTAINERS and defconfig entries; device tree (thermal and watchdog, PWM, ADC, Melon Pi V2 audio routing); driver-side changes including PWM reusing the TH1520 driver, IIO ADC single conversion, ASoC’s A210 I2S controller and Everest ES8156 stereo codec; plus one [NOT-FOR-UPSTREAM] deb-pkg DTB installation adjustment. Most are submitted in normalized form as dt-bindings, drivers, and device trees, continuing RevyOS’s rapid follow-up pace on new hardware.

1.5 Ecosystem Shorts: Bare-Metal Verification Practice for Lua/OCaml on the Sail RISC-V Model

Date: 2026-09-29　Source: ship-your-lua repository (09-29 commit), ship-your-ocaml repository (09-29 commit)

Two new developments have emerged in the formal verification direction: first, the ship-your-lua project performs end-to-end verification of Lua 5.4.7 compiled to bare-metal RV64 on the Sail RISC-V model using Lean 4 and iris-lean (completing an ELF disassembly survey and semantic scaffolding, among other work); second, ship-your-ocaml verifies the OCaml bootstrap compiler—its verification records show differential testing passing 9 out of 9, with boot/ocamlc completing one compilation on the Sail model (approximately 81.9 million steps). Both are frontier practices in “verifiability of the language runtime stack” (the previous generation, ship-your-interpreter, already proved the refinement relation for a WHILE interpreter ELF).

II. Product and Ecosystem Developments

2.1 GF’s MIPS: Completes ARC Integration, Anchors on RISC-V Physical AI, Partners with Xcelsa to Accelerate Custom Silicon

Date: 2026-09-29　Source: digitimes “GF’s MIPS bets on RISC-V physical AI, expands into autos and data centers” (09-29, Google News aggregator link), Industry Events reposted summary (09-29), Business Wire “MIPS and Xcelsa Collaborate to Accelerate Verified, Workload-Optimized Custom Silicon” (09-29)

digitimes reported on 09-29: MIPS, under GlobalFoundries, says it has completed the integration of Synopsys’ ARC processor IP business, anchoring on RISC-V as its foundation for Physical AI and expanding from AIoT into automotive and data center applications; it claims the merger has made GF and MIPS the world’s second-largest processor IP supplier. On the same day, Business Wire reported: MIPS has entered a strategic partnership with Xcelsa Labs — leveraging Xcelsa’s Apex design optimization platform (formal equivalence assurance) to accelerate custom chip development based on MIPS IP, covering AI, automotive, industrial, robotics, and edge scenarios. Earlier in early September, MIPS had already launched three workload-native platforms: Acies, Actus, and Aegis.

2.2 Nuvacore: Unveils “Core First” Development Model, Keeps ISA Choice Open

Date: 2026-09-29　Source: Notebookcheck “Apple Silicon and Nuvia veterans reveal unusual CPU core that keeps its ISA options open” (09-29), Tom’s Hardware “Legendary Qualcomm, Apple, and Nuvia alumni form new CPU startup” (background)

CPU startup Nuvacore, founded by Gerard Williams III, John Bruno, and Ram Srinivasan (with Apple/Nuvia/Qualcomm backgrounds), has disclosed the “Core First” model for its first general-purpose core, WarpCore: build an ISA-decoupled microarchitecture foundation first, then select the instruction set based on the target system; its recruiting materials list RISC-V, Arm64, and x86 side by side, interpreted as keeping ISA options open — media noted that RISC-V offers the most direct path since it avoids Arm-style architecture licensing, while decode, privilege levels, memory ordering, and software compatibility remain the decisive constraints on the final ISA. The company has not yet disclosed its ISA choice, process node, or tapeout plans.

2.3 SignOff: Demonstrates Full Custom Silicon Flow Capabilities with RISC-V “Kaveri” Soft IP

Date: 2026-09-29　Source: Electronics For You “SignOff Builds Toward a Bigger Role in Custom Silicon” (09-28/29)

Indian design services firm SignOff Semiconductor is expanding from VLSI design services into turnkey custom silicon: it showcases customizable RTL and application-centric peripheral functions as soft IP on its in-house RISC-V “Kaveri” platform, positioning it as a “proof of capability” rather than a product line — targeting customers who want a single partner to handle everything from architecture to silicon implementation. The company says it has completed multiple application demonstrations on the platform and is combining foundry and OSAT ecosystem partnerships with EDA investment to take on end-to-end projects.
