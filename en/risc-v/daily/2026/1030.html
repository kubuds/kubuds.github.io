Today’s Focus: Post-Quantum Instruction-Set Extension Runs Classic McEliece on Cyclone V, Cutting Decapsulation Cycles by 86.1% (10-29)

A paper published in Discover Computing provides full FPGA validation of this design. The authors are Haridhayal Maheswaran, Samuel Kennedy, and Basel Halak (paper title “Architectural exploration and FPGA validation of a RISC-V instruction-set extension for Classic McEliece,” Volume 29, Issue 1, Article 808, DOI 10.1007/s10791-026-10770-1), reported on 10-29 by Scienmag and Bioengineer.

There are three core conclusions. First, a tightly coupled instruction-set extension is feasible on real hardware: the full design occupies 10,531 adaptive logic modules on a Cyclone V, achieves a maximum fitted clock of 118.89 MHz, and fits on a DE1-SoC board. Second, the speedup is verifiable: under the same asynchronous-memory simulation setup, encapsulation cycles drop by 87.4% and decapsulation by 86.1%, equivalent to 7.18× the unaccelerated baseline; the synchronous FPGA version performs both operations using host-generated keys, requiring 3,654,113 cycles for decapsulation and 13,585 cycles for encapsulation including SHA-256. Third, the gains are unevenly distributed: the Berlekamp-Massey sub-operation drops from 5,832 cycles to 40, and Gaussian elimination cuts minimal-polynomial generation from 297 to 38 cycles (7.8×), but nearly all remaining decapsulation cost lies in support-list generation—over 99% of cycles under both memory configurations—with the bottleneck being the Benes permutation network that drives the support, not the already-accelerated decoding logic.

The most methodologically valuable part of the paper is a rejected approach. The authors originally intended to add a DMA-assisted instruction mapping for composeinv; after integration profiling, it yielded only a 1.0–1.1× improvement in the controlbits stage, because the recursive call structure repeatedly crosses narrow memory boundaries, canceling out all kernel-level gains, so they abandoned it outright. From this the authors derive an accelerator-selection rule: when call structure dominates data-movement cost, making only the bulk kernel faster is ineffective. This criterion applies equally to teams in China currently working on custom RISC-V instruction-set extensions.

Engineering details are also worth recording. The authors moved custom instructions into the standard RISC-V custom-0 opcode space, replacing the original binary patch stubs with GCC’s .insn directive, saving roughly 20 cycles of function-call overhead per invocation and letting the compiler schedule custom instructions into surrounding code. The inherited Berlekamp-Massey state machine revealed four defects during bring-up: an incorrect shift-loop bound, negated operands in field-fraction arithmetic, an implicit 32-bit promotion that broke mask computation, and a missing zero-extension on multiplier inputs that let high bits leak into the result; the transpose path also required a 64×64 crossbar transpose memory rather than an ordinary register file—an issue exposed only by full-system decapsulation testing. To fit the board’s area budget, test parameters were reduced to error-correction capability t=2, code length n=48, and field degree 12.

The limitations are stated clearly. Because controlbits remain on the software side, the key-generation path exceeded the 300-million-cycle simulation budget and can only be given as a lower bound: over 60 seconds at the board’s 5 MHz clock, and over 2.52 seconds if the entire system ran at 118.89 MHz; full device-side key generation was neither timed nor demonstrated on the board, and the FPGA image loads host-generated keys. On power, Quartus PowerPlay estimates 458.34 mW total at 5 MHz, with a single decapsulation taking about 731 ms and consuming about 335 mJ, roughly 90% of which is static leakage. Neither production parameters nor side-channel resistance were evaluated, and no side-channel experiments were performed; a scaling analysis using the full mceliece348864 parameter set shows that the packing matrix, the 255 KB public key, and processor memory cannot all fit within the target device’s roughly 496 KB of block memory, and the toy-scale acceleration block would need to be redesigned rather than simply scaled up.

I. Open-Source Community and Software Ecosystem

openRuyi: 20 commits in a single day — vkpeak lands in the repo, clang-wrap follows up, KDE Frameworks 6.31.0 bulk upgrade (10-29)

The 20 commits fall into three groups. The largest is 13 kf6-* packages synced to KDE Frameworks 6.31.0, covering kio, kwallet, ksvg, prison, kguiaddons, threadweaver, syntax-highlighting, syndication, sonnet, solid, qqc2-desktop-style, purpose, and networkmanager-qt. The second is the addition of the vkpeak package (upstream version 20260527), a synthetic Vulkan device peak-performance benchmark that measures peak vector and matrix throughput for fp32/fp16/fp64 and int8 through int64, bf16, and fp8; it depends on vulkan-headers and vulkan-loader, and the SPEC was submitted by a maintainer at the Institute of Software. The third is build-chain maintenance: clang-wrap updated to 20261029, ecbuild relocated its cmake modules to the multi-architecture library directory, three Go ecosystem packages had dependencies and build support fixed, and the duplicate package go-github-smartystreets-assertions was removed.

The real significance of vkpeak landing in the repo lies in the measurement baseline: Vulkan devices on RISC-V platforms previously had no unified peak-compute comparison tool, and can now be compared directly against x86 and Arm boards. The latest release on the official news page is still the 2026.09 version, and the October version has not yet been released, so these 20 commits are rolling maintenance rather than a release sprint.

Date: 2026-10-29　Source: openRuyi commit log, vkpeak SPEC

XiangShan difftest: three commits — MMA writeback reuses pooled buffers, gsim supports FST waveforms (10-29)

All three commits target verification efficiency. No. 1014 changes MMA writeback from “allocating and zeroing an entire physical result buffer per instruction” to reuse from a bounded pool, falling back to ordinary allocation when the pool is full; the new MatrixResult transfers result ownership from the software ROB to asynchronous verification, with its deleter responsible for returning pooled storage or freeing an ordinary array, and the shared pool’s lifetime guarantees that not-yet-completed results remain valid, with retirement, replay, and destruction paths finalized by RAII. No. 1013 fixes physical register layout fidelity on the MMA path on both the CPU and CUDA sides. No. 867 adds FST waveform output to gsim, making it easy to feed simulation waveforms directly into standard viewing tools.

Date: 2026-10-29　Source: difftest #1014, #1013, #867

XiangShan NEMU adds a NutShell fast-reference config, CI reuses PR-Agent review comments (10-29)

No. 1273 adds a NutShell fastref configuration profile to NEMU, allowing differential comparison against NutShell as a reference to take a lighter reference path; the PR description includes a host instruction count and throughput comparison table for XS interpreter tests, covering benchmarks such as bitmanip. No. 1263 changes the CI flow so the pipeline reuses review comments already generated by PR-Agent, avoiding repeated analysis of the same diff.

Date: 2026-10-29　Source: NEMU #1273, #1263

XiangShan XSCache switches CoupledL2 hint arbitration to round-robin, fixing long-term blocking (10-29)

Commit No. 46 changes the arbiter in CoupledL2 from fixed priority to round-robin arbitration, resolving an issue where one hint path continuously held the arbitration and left the other path unable to obtain a grant for a long time. The change itself has only a one-line description, but it is a classic starvation-type defect in the cache hierarchy that only shows up under prolonged stress testing.

Date: 2026-10-29　Source: XSCache #46

XiangShan CI performance flow allows disabling difftest (10-29)

No. 6804 adds a switch to disable difftest in the perf flow of the main XiangShan repo’s CI, allowing benchmark scenarios to skip differential comparison and devote machine time to performance measurement. The PR has no text description, and the change is limited to CI scripts without touching RTL.

Date: 2026-10-29　Source: XiangShan #6804

V. Summary and Trend Observations

1. The crux of post-quantum cryptography is “where to draw the instruction-set boundary,” not “making the big core compute faster.” Today’s paper provides a verifiable selection map: Berlekamp-Massey and Chien search deliver the highest cycle gains per unit of logic, each nearly eliminating a sub-operation within 1,000 adaptive look-up tables; streaming public-key generation occupies 4,705 adaptive look-up tables and is only worthwhile when keys must be generated on-device; the rejected DMA-based composeinv shows that accelerating batch kernels is ineffective when call structure dominates data-movement cost. This criterion is more practical than “how many times faster the benchmark runs,” and is directly usable by domestic teams building RISC-V custom instruction extensions.

2. Verification infrastructure continues toward “resource pooling + trimmability.” difftest pools MMA result buffers and replaces per-entry allocation with ownership transfer; the XiangShan CI perf flow allows difftest to be disabled. Both changes point the same way—treating verification as a budget to be managed rather than an overhead that must always be fully on. XSCache’s switch of arbitration to round-robin fixes a starvation-class defect, a long-term stability issue, and belongs with the previous two under “first make the infrastructure stop being a drag.”

3. openRuyi is filling in the “measurable” layer. With vkpeak merged, Vulkan peak compute on RISC-V boards now has a unified metric directly comparable with x86 and Arm; the continued follow-through on clang-wrap and KDE frameworks shows a steady rolling-release cadence. However, the official news page still shows the 2026.09 release as latest, with the October release unpublished; this period’s 20 commits are mainly framework and ecosystem packages, not a pre-release sprint.

4. The domestic side has entered a lull, and the lull is on the development side rather than the delivery side. XuanTie, ESWIN, and Nuclei had zero new development in the window; SpacemiT had only two documentation commits to docs-product, with its four robotics-stack repos stalled; Sophgo had only one line of performance data. In parallel, the DAMO embodied-AI team saw its third core personnel change in two months—this has no direct technical link to RISC-V, but it reflects talent and capital in embodied intelligence flowing from big-platform roles to startups in the Physical RSI direction, and the main suppliers of domestic RISC-V robotics software stacks are precisely the external partners of such teams; the impact of this personnel flow on future roadmaps is worth tracking.

5. Information volume and basis for judgment this period. After deduplication across 26 Chinese and English Google News queries in the window, only three substantive new items emerged: the post-quantum paper, the Tenstorrent India localization report, and the DAMO personnel change; dimensional, browser, and consumer-electronics items (XHEART X7 Ultra, LattePanda Sigma 2, BeagleConnect QuadESC) were verified to use Armv9, Intel, and TI solutions respectively, unrelated to RISC-V, and were excluded. Open source remains the main body this period: openRuyi’s 20 commits, XiangShan’s 6, CORE-V’s 4, and Sophgo’s 1 all have verifiable commit records. The GitHub organization-level API’s anonymous quota was exhausted in the latter part of this period’s collection, so commit feeds were used as a fallback; consequently, full push lists for organizations such as Espressif and Sipeed could not be expanded repo by repo.
