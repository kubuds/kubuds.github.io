Aheesa signed with networking brand Digisol to move its domestically designed RISC-V networking SoC VIHAAN-I into complete-system products. Today’s focus: Aheesa signs with Digisol; VIHAAN-I moves into networking productization (11-24)

On November 24, Aheesa Digital Innovations and Digisol Systems signed an MoU in Mumbai to jointly develop networking products based on the VIHAAN chipset. Aheesa is a Chennai-headquartered fabless semiconductor company supported by India’s MeitY DLI scheme. Digisol is an Indian networking brand and a wholly owned subsidiary of Smartlink Holdings.

VIHAAN-I is India’s first domestically designed RISC-V broadband networking SoC, using RV64GC and integrating a hardware packet engine and security subsystem. The chip completed tape-out on January 26, 2026, and lit up on first silicon on August 15, 2026. The company plans to enter commercial deployment in early 2027.

This partnership fills in the product side. Aheesa provides the chip platform; Digisol provides product engineering, manufacturing, and channels. Both parties frame the goal as moving VIHAAN-I from lit-up silicon to a deliverable technology stack.

I. Open Source Community and Software Ecosystem

openRuyi 20 commits: OpenJDK split and integrated with alternatives, 15 new Rust packages (11-24)

On November 24, the openRuyi main repository landed 20 commits within the window. The largest group is OpenJDK. Four specs—openjdk-17, 21, 25, and latest—were changed together, splitting the packages more finely and integrating the alternatives mechanism. Commands such as java and javac can therefore switch between multiple JDKs. The same batch unified the spec format.

On the Rust side, 15 packages were added in one go, including crossterm 0.27, sysinfo 0.33, fancy-regex 0.16, mio 0.8, and bytesize 1. The rest are yaml-rust 0.4, terminal-colorsaurus 1, terminal-trx 0.2, and dissimilar 1. The last three items are proc-macro-hack 0.5 and the execute-command series. The linux spec also received cleanup, removing duplicate arch_suffix and redundant %{name} usage.

The companion repository kiwi-image had 6 commits. The image definition added an x86_64 target, changed partitioning to keep only EFI+GPT, and added an RVA20 configuration. Branch pushes no longer trigger image builds.

The openRuyi blog also published an OSPP project article during the same period, dated November 12. The project restored upstream tests for four components: genimage, lua-json, LMDB, and LibTomCrypt. genimage generated inconsistent UUIDs on x86_64 and RISC-V. The cause is that the random32() call is written inside function arguments, and C does not specify argument evaluation order. After the fix, values are taken in a fixed order, and PR #345 was submitted upstream.

Date: 2026-11-24　Source: https://github.com/openRuyi-Project/openRuyi .

ruyifirmware: firmware Setup supports full-screen 80x25, K3 enables phased UFS high-speed switching (11-24)

The ruyi branch of ruyifirmware landed two commits within the window. One makes the UEFI Setup interface display at full-screen 80x25 on all boards, which was previously limited by terminal size. The other changes K3’s UFS initialization to switch to high-speed mode in phases.

The previous day’s briefing already recorded the same repository’s EDK II baseline upgrade and SG2042 runtime variable changes. These two commits are follow-ups on the same firmware line.

Date: 2026-11-24　Source: https://github.com/openRuyi-Project/ruyifirmware/tree/ruyi .

XiangShan GEM5 5 commits: optional pipelining for scalar floating-point division and square root (11-24)

The xs-dev branch of GEM5 had 5 commits within the window. The latest, #1328, adds optional pipelining for scalar floating-point division and square root, filling in a performance-model capability. #1380 requires KMH’s SStride training to hit in the TLB, avoiding training the prefetcher with incorrect addresses.

The other three commits are on the tooling and observability side. #1361 changes BPU and load-triple profiling to be enabled on demand. #1379 unifies how performance CI and solver workloads are executed. #1383 updates the codeowner.

Date: 2026-11-24　Source: https://github.com/OpenXiangShan/GEM5/tree/xs-dev .

wasmc: compiling WebAssembly to RV32IM on ESP32-C3 (11-24)

The open source project wasmc appeared on Hacker News on November 24. It translates WASM 1.0 modules into native RV32IM code, with the compiler itself running on the device. The target platform is ESP32-C3, with about 400 KB of usable RAM.

The project’s figures are: a typical module compiles in tens of milliseconds and uses a few KB of heap. The output uses the WAMR 2.4.4 AOT file format and can be loaded directly by WAMR’s AOT runtime. This allows WASM to run on MCUs without carrying LLVM.

Date: 2026-11-24　Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50626718, https://github.com/llllluca/wasmc .

II. Product and Ecosystem Updates

Gasgoo Discloses Automotive-Grade RISC-V Mass Production Timeline and Standards Roadmap (11-24)

On November 24, Gasgoo published two follow-up reports on the RISC-V Club seminar. The seminar itself was held on November 19 and was covered in a previous daily brief. What is new here are the mass production arrangements and standards details.

Automakers provided timelines. Qi Shilong, Executive Vice President of Chery, said Chery is among the first batch of users nearing mass production. Chery plans to jointly publish automotive-grade baseline interface specification white papers with Infineon in phases in 2027, 2029, and 2030, covering compilation, debugging, operating systems, and certification, and to feed back data at a scale of more than 2 million vehicles per year. Shu Chuncheng, Senior Vice President of Leapmotor, said automotive MCUs are still dominated by Arm and proprietary architectures, and that the company will fully integrate into the RISC-V ecosystem. Liu Jiaxi of Xiaomi Auto said the openvela platform has open-sourced RISC-V support, with more than 10 million IoT mass-production units shipped.

On the software side, Neusoft Reach’s NeuSAR has completed adaptation on the AURIX RC1 virtual prototype. The company plans to launch the DRIVECORE development kit alongside RC1 samples in 2027. Infineon said the RC1 virtual prototype can be applied for online, and the software kit supports free trials of up to 6 months.

On the standards side, Xia Xianzhao, Chief Expert at CATARC, presented progress on the automotive RISC-V industry standard. The standard is titled “General Technical Specification for the Fifth-Generation Reduced Instruction Set of Automotive Chips”; project initiation began this year, and the current core work is defining the Profile. CATARC is also leading the formation of the China Automotive Chip Standards, Testing and Certification Alliance, which operates by invitation; Infineon is the first overseas company invited to join.

Date: 2026-11-24　Source: https://auto.gasgoo.com/news/202611/24I70478926C108.shtml, https://auto.gasgoo.com/news/202611/24I70478936C108.shtml .

E4 and Openchip Sign MoU to Evaluate RISC-V AI Acceleration Platform (11-20)

On November 20, during SC26, Italian system integrator E4 Computer Engineering and Spain’s Openchip signed an MoU. The two sides will evaluate jointly developing a European energy-efficient computing platform for large-scale AI inference and HPC. Openchip is headquartered in Barcelona and develops RISC-V processors and accelerators, with the BERNIA accelerator in its product line.

The MoU points to the EU’s AI Factory and GigaFactory programs. The next step is a requirements survey across academia, industry, and data centers, after which solution design and validation would be discussed. Both sides made clear that this work depends on the survey results and a final commercial agreement.

This announcement was made on November 20, with English-language media following up on November 23-24. It was not covered in a previous daily brief and is added here.

Date: 2026-11-20　Source: https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/e4-computer-engineering-and-openchip-sign-mou-to-explore-eu-sovereign-ai-compute-platforms/, https://www.e4company.com/en/2026/11/openchip-e4-mou/ .

Espressif Releases esp-riscv-trace v0.3.0 (11-24)

On November 24, Espressif released esp-riscv-trace v0.3.0, with the version commit landing on master at 16:51 (Beijing time) that day. The contents of this release match the merged features recorded in a previous daily brief.

This release adds an instruction semantic decoder, adds a fault window for register origin backtracking, and adds callee names to calls entering untracked code. On the decoding side, it also extracts RV32 shared encoding rules into encoding.py. The previous version, v0.2.0, was released on November 11, 13 days earlier.

Date: 2026-11-24　Source: https://github.com/espressif/esp-riscv-trace/releases .

III. Deep Dive into Domestic Teams

XiangShan 5 commits: DCache visibility, TopDown grouping, and Debug exception redirection (11-24)

The XiangShan main repo’s kunminghu-v3 branch had 5 commits in the window. #7035 fixes DCache client visibility under a single memory channel, so all clients can see the complete data. #6567 adds L1/L2 grouping to TopDownCounters, enabling layered performance attribution.

Three commits target correctness. #7038 redirects exceptions triggered in Debug Mode instead of handling them directly in the debug state. One MBTB commit invalidates stale predictions when io.enable toggles, avoiding reuse across states. #6876 is an area optimization that reuses Sc’s tage meta.

The companion repo XSCache had 2 commits: one reserves a D slot for CBOAck, the other upgrades the OpenNCB submodule. Both difftest commits are performance-related: MMA writeback now submits in batches by compact block, and local XDMA FIFO reads on the FPGA side are also batched.

Date: 2026-11-24　Source: https://github.com/OpenXiangShan/XiangShan/tree/kunminghu-v3, https://github.com/OpenXiangShan/XSCache, https://github.com/OpenXiangShan/difftest .

SpacemiT: MPP fixes VDEC trailing NAL, XSlim fixes GridSample mode (11-24)

SpacemiT had 1 commit each in two repos that day. mpp fixes VDEC’s tail-frame detection when parsing a full bitstream, treating the trailing NAL as the last frame to avoid dropped frames. xslim fixes the executor’s GridSample mode decoding, mapping ONNX naming to PyTorch (PR #53).

llama.cpp’s mtmd-backend branch had no new commits in the window. No new activity has appeared since the sync batch recorded in the previous day’s report.

Date: 2026-11-24　Source: https://github.com/spacemit-com/mpp, https://github.com/spacemit-com/xslim .

Sipeed SPI2HDMI examples: CH32V003/004 and ESP32-S3 drive HDMI (11-24)

Sipeed landed 4 commits in the SPI2HDMI_demo repo, adding example code and documentation. The module converts ILI9341 SPI commands into HDMI output, so the MCU can keep its existing display driver while changing resolution.

The examples cover two platform families. CH32V003 and CH32V004 run Bad Apple, Conway’s Life, and real-time fractals. CH32V004 additionally has an 8-item menu arcade example supporting 720p and TE sync. The ESP32-S3 side provides an esp_lcd panel driver supporting SPI and QSPI, plus retro-go ports of NES, Game Boy, and Doom.

Date: 2026-11-24　Source: https://github.com/sipeed/SPI2HDMI_demo .

V. Summary and Trend Observations

  • Productization: India’s VIHAAN-I has moved from bring-up to full-system partnerships, and Europe’s Openchip accelerator has entered the EU AI Factory agenda. Neither is in RISC-V’s traditional stronghold, pointing instead to network equipment and data center acceleration.
  • Automotive: Domestic discussion has shifted from “whether to use it” to “when it ships in vehicles.” Chery has laid out a phased plan for 2027 to 2030, and the standards side has begun defining Profiles. The real test will be 2027’s silicon samples and design wins.
  • Packaging and validation: openRuyi splits the OpenJDK package and restores upstream tests, while ruyifirmware adds a Setup interface. Such changes never appear in release notes, but they determine whether a distribution can keep tracking upstream long term.
  • Performance models: XiangShan’s GEM5 adds pipelined floating-point division and SStride prefetch constraints, while XSCache and difftest move to batched submission. The validation tools themselves are getting faster.

Taken together, there were no new chip launches and no new benchmark scores that day. The verifiable progress is concentrated in two places: on the product side, pushing chips into complete systems and racks; on the engineering side, landing patches in distributions and validation tools. Media attention is on automotive, but the code is landing in packaging.
