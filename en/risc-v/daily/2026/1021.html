Today’s Highlights: BlueCore Computing releases the ZhiTong Fusion Super Node, RISC-V AI compute moves from single chips to clusters

Date: Released 2026-10-19 (widely reported on 10-20)　Sources: Economic Information Daily: BlueCore Computing releases the ZhiTong Fusion Super Node (10-20), Securities Daily Online: BlueCore Computing releases the ZhiTong Fusion Super Node (10-20), 36Kr: BlueCore Computing releases the ZhiTong Fusion Super Node, RISC-V accelerates expansion into new AI inference scenarios (10-20), PEdaily: BlueCore Computing releases the ZhiTong Fusion Super Node (10-20)

On the third day of the 6th RISC-V China Summit (10-19), BlueCore Computing (Shenzhen) released the ZhiTong Fusion Super Node, advancing RISC-V-based AI computing capabilities from single chips and servers to clustered computing infrastructure. On the same day, CTO Jia Xinxia delivered a keynote titled “LX500: Unlocking a New Path for AI Inference.”

  • Architectural proposition: Traditional “CPU + discrete accelerator card” solutions rely on PCIe to move data between different memory spaces, and the long context and first-token latency of large model inference are amplified by bandwidth and power consumption. ZhiTong Fusion integrates general-purpose computing and AI computing on the same chip and uses a unified memory architecture to reduce data movement; the super node is an extension of this approach toward larger-scale systems.
  • Chip specifications: The LX500 integrates 48 RISC-V cores (32 performance cores + 16 efficiency cores), along with AI Vector and Matrix compute engines, delivering 75 TOPS INT8 of AI compute and supporting multiple precisions from INT4 to FP32; it shares DDR5 memory resources and supports CXL expansion.
  • Product cadence: The chip powered on in February 2026, made its first public appearance in April, an all-in-one prototype was released in May, the Lenovo Wentian WR5219 G5 server equipped with the LX500 debuted in July, and since August samples have been sent to multiple industry customers and entered small-batch deployment testing. This has formed a four-tier product matrix: LX500 chip, WR5219 G5 server, LX5000 ZhiTong Fusion all-in-one machine, and ZhiTong Fusion Super Node.
  • Software and scenarios: Adaptation work has been carried out around large models such as Qwen, DeepSeek, and Kimi, covering different parameter scales, different precisions, and long-context scenarios; deployment directions include enterprise private knowledge base RAG, Agent office assistants, financial compliance inference, government offline Q&A, and industrial on-site inspection.

Date: 2026-10-20　Source: openRuyi main repo commit log (10-20), openRuyi official news page

The openRuyi main repo recorded 40 commits during the window, concentrated in a batch merge on the morning of 10-20: the minio series (server build switched to not using bundled vendor dependencies, plus a new console, the mc client, and madmin-go v3), Prometheus and prom2json, IBM sarama (TLS helper functions retained), gobwas-ws (DEFLATE compatibility and dependency fixes), xdg-scram (library dependencies completed), technosophos-moniker, plus two new packages, uwsgi and dropwatch. 1.2 OpenHW CVW: QM Tech K7 board support merged, new page-table A/D bit regression test added

Date: 2026-10-20　Source: openhwfoundation/cvw commit log (10-20)

The open-source core verification platform CVW (CORE-V Wally) merged 3 commits during the window: added support for the QM Tech K7 development board (PR #1601, further expanding FPGA board-level support), and added the hptwReservedAD regression test (PR #1993), providing targeted coverage for A/D bit update behavior on reserved leaf PTEs. For CVW, which is known for formal verification and regression suites, filling in such edge-case tests reflects verification maturity more than new features do. The CVA6 main branch under the same organization had no new commits during the window.

1.3 riscv.org: CERN discloses progress on SoCMake and the TriglaV radiation-tolerant RISC-V SoC

Date: 2026-10-20　Source: RISC-V International: SoCMake — Modular RISC-V SoCs for Radiation-Harsh and Safety-Critical Environments (10-20)

The RISC-V International blog published a summit poster paper from the CERN team (Benoît Denkinger et al.): SoCMake, developed under the SOCRATES (System-on-Chip Radiation-Tolerant Ecosystem) project, is used to modularly build radiation-tolerant RISC-V SoCs for high-energy physics; the companion prototype chip TriglaV is a fully radiation-tolerant ASIC targeting harsh-radiation environments such as LHC detector front-end readout. The technical combination presented in the paper—triple modular redundancy, hardened interconnect, memory protection—also applies to safety-critical scenarios in automotive and industrial control, with the goal of extending optimization from the design phase to the in-field operation phase.

II. Product and Ecosystem Updates

2.1 RICORE’s Gaoguang D35 Debuts at the Summit: RISC-V Dataflow Architecture, Running a 35B Model Locally with 128GB Memory

Date: 2026-10-20　Source: Shanghai Securities News (reposted by Sohu): When RISC-V Meets Dataflow, RICORE Delivers a New High-End Domestic AI Chip Answer (10-20), CSDN: RISC-V China Summit Opens, On-Site Photos of RICORE’s Gaoguang D35 (10-20)

Suzhou RICORE showcased its Gaoguang D35 chip at the summit: 128GB memory, Chiplet packaging, capable of running a 35B-parameter large model locally, with the booth also displaying a sign that its core IP has passed Intel’s global procurement validation. Its technical approach is “RISC-V + dataflow” — the dataflow architecture lets computation happen where data arrives, bypassing the power overhead of instruction scheduling and data movement, while RISC-V provides an autonomous and controllable instruction set foundation; the company calls it the world’s first mass-produced RISC-V dataflow physical AI chip (company claim). Target scenarios include embodied intelligence, video intelligence, smart campuses, cockpit-driving integration, satellites, and communications; the company also claims revenue exceeding RMB 100 million in 2024 and break-even in 2025.

  • Product roadmap: DAMO Academy for the first time published an automotive-grade processor roadmap targeting autonomous driving and embodied intelligence, with C925A, C930A, and C950A to follow the E908A, among which C950A targets 5x the overall performance of the first-generation automotive-grade product.
  • Server side: C950 serves as the high-performance foundation, paired with the multi-core interconnect product XT-LINK to further improve multi-core performance linearity; C950 is the first to achieve deep adaptation between RISC-V and server-side Java, with a 30% performance gain.
  • Open-source contributions: RISC-V-related contributions to the GCC community rank second worldwide, delivering more than 10% typical performance gains for the processor; more than half of the RISC-V code in the OpenJDK community comes from DAMO Academy; it participates in CSC certification baseline construction at RISC-V International and has open-sourced 2796 test cases.
  • Frontier collaboration: Together with the Beijing Institute of Open Source Chip and ZTE Microelectronics, it has achieved interim results on spatial multithreading (SMT), with a 30% performance gain from dual threads; it participates in building the Ruyi community of the Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and works with more than 20 institutions at home and abroad to advance software adaptation for the matrix computation AME extension.

3.3 Espressif esp-emu 0.50.0: P4/S31 Fused Multiply-Add Roughly 3x Faster, P4 PSRAM Window Follows the MMU

Date: 2026-10-20　Source: espressif/esp-emulator: esp-emu 0.50.0 (10-20)

Espressif’s open-source emulator esp-emu 0.50.0 was released on 10-20, with RISC-V-side changes concentrated on ESP32-P4/S31/C3:

  • Performance: Single-precision fused multiply-add on P4 and S31 now takes a host passthrough path, roughly 3x faster, with math functions such as sinf, expf, and powf benefiting accordingly, while results and flag bits remain consistent.
  • Memory model: The P4 PSRAM window now follows the PSRAM MMU, so firmware that remaps the external memory window at runtime no longer reads all zeros and no longer traps on illegal instructions.
  • Bug fixes: The BLE transmit power API on C3 and S3 no longer triggers a Load access fault after the controller is enabled; P4 boot for rev 0.x/1.x firmware is now rejected early on the emulator side, with two fixes offered: switch to the corresponding ROM or recompile.

The more precisely the emulator aligns with silicon behavior, the more viable it becomes as a substitute for real hardware in RISC-V endpoint firmware CI — which is the practical value of Espressif’s continued investment in this toolchain.

4.1 MIIT: Humanoid Robot Output Exceeded 40,000 Units in the First Half of the Year, Set to Break 100,000 Units for the Full Year

Date: Published 2026-10-19 (widely reported on 10-20)　Sources: Sina Tech (IT Home): China’s humanoid robot output exceeded 40,000 units in the first half of this year, expected to break 100,000 units for the full year (10-19), Sohu (Nanfang Plus): MIIT says China’s humanoid robot output exceeded 40,000 units in the first half of the year (10-20)

The State Council Information Office held a press conference on 10-19. Wei Wei, Director-General of the Department of Science and Technology of MIIT, stated: in the first half of this year, China’s humanoid robot complete-machine products exceeded 400 models, with output surpassing 40,000 units, and are expected to break 100,000 units for the full year; related products have been validated and deployed across more than 300 key scenarios, including warehousing and logistics, food and retail, and home services. Next steps include: using humanoid robots as a small entry point to drive the embodied intelligence industry, tackling key common technologies through the “open competition” mechanism, advancing real-world training, publishing the 2026 edition of the National Humanoid Robot Industry Standards System Construction Guide, building a high-quality humanoid robot open-source community, and establishing an industry monitoring system while strengthening quality and data security testing.

Interface with RISC-V: This scale simultaneously amplifies cost and energy-efficiency pressure across the “body—joints—controller” chain, and embodied intelligence chips are precisely where domestic RISC-V teams are currently most concentrated (Xinji Infinite’s full-stack RISC-V chips, SpacemiT’s robot perception stack, and Ruixin’s deployments in embodied intelligence scenarios). The two policy levers—the open-source community and the standards system—also directly address the software and interface specifications that the RISC-V side has long lacked.

V. Summary and Trend Observations

  • RISC-V’s AI narrative is upgrading from “chip” to “system”: Bluecore Computing pushes intelligent-computing integration from single chips to supernodes, Ruixin uses a dataflow architecture to handle local inference of 35B models, and Zhongke Tongliang directly offers a trio of workstation + all-in-one machine + accelerator card. All three coincidentally bypass “benchmarking general-purpose CPUs” and instead organize compute around inference scenarios—the clearest industry signal this issue.
  • Domestic teams’ differentiation lies at the architecture layer, not the process layer: dataflow (Ruixin, Zhongke Tongliang), unified memory and CXL (Bluecore), SMT and AME matrix extensions (DAMO)—under advanced-process constraints, these are performance gains that can be recovered through design. DAMO’s five-fold target for the C950A is essentially an extension of the same logic.
  • Software stack completion is shifting from toolchains to server-side middleware: among openRuyi’s 40 commits in a single day, cloud-native components such as minio, Prometheus, sarama, and uwsgi account for the bulk; Espressif, meanwhile, brings emulator silicon consistency down to the single-instruction level. The former determines whether RISC-V servers can handle real business workloads; the latter determines the iteration speed of edge firmware.
  • Capital and products are starting to synchronize: SpacemiT released the V100 on 10-19 and closed its B+ round on 10-20—a one-day gap; AgiBot ties bodies and upstream chips together with 47 investments. Compared with the past two years’ “raise first, then find scenarios,” this year’s order looks more like “ship products first, then replenish ammunition.”
  • The policy foundation for embodied intelligence is taking shape: MIIT gives a full-year expectation of 100,000 units and explicitly plans to publish the 2026 edition of the standards system construction guide and build a humanoid robot open-source community. For RISC-V, this is a window to write chips, middleware, and scenario interfaces into the specifications together.
  • Limitations: The number of chips per node, interconnect method, and delivery timeline for Bluecore Computing’s supernode are not public; existing reports offer only directional statements. Ruixin’s “world’s first mass-produced RISC-V dataflow physical AI chip” and “Intel global procurement recognition” are both company claims, with no public confirmation from Intel. Zhongke Tongliang’s product specifications come from the vendor’s public presentation at the summit, lacking third-party testing. The MIIT data was released on 10-19, and this issue’s inclusion is based on the press conference transcript and concentrated reporting on 10-20. Changes detected in the member website inspection are all routine homepage updates at universities and institutions, with no substantive product, funding, or architecture announcements found. The XiangShan repository organization-side pushed_at shows pushes, but atom re-checks of the default branch found no new commits within the window, judged to be non-default-branch activity.
