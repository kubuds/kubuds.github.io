Today’s Highlight: ESWIN Computing Officially Launches Hong Kong IPO, Listing Planned for October 9

Date: 2026-09-28　Sources: RTHK “ESWIN Computing IPO raises up to approximately HK$2.5 billion, may become the first ‘RISC-V’ stock” (09-28), Economic Reference Network “ESWIN Computing launches Hong Kong IPO” (09-28), Hong Kong Commercial Daily “RISC-V chipmaker ESWIN Computing (01256) opens subscription today, plans global offering of approximately 1.57 billion shares” (09-28)

On September 28, Beijing ESWIN Computing (01256.HK) officially launched its Hong Kong global offering: approximately 1.57 billion H shares (of which approximately 10% is the Hong Kong public offering), offer price range of HK$1.48 to HK$1.59 per share, 2,000 shares per lot with an entry fee of approximately HK$3,212, and total fundraising of up to approximately HK$2.497 billion (nearly HK$2.5 billion, approximately US$320 million) at the top of the range. Subscription will close on October 6, with listing on the Main Board of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange expected on October 9; joint sponsors are CITIC Securities and China Securities International.

The company has introduced cornerstone investors including CITIC Asset Management Hong Kong, Japan’s Orix, Hefei Construction Investment, and GBAHIL (Greater Bay Area Homeland Investments), subscribing to a total of approximately HK$1.161 billion; proceeds will be mainly used for product development of human-machine interaction and multimedia processing chips, interconnect and computing chips, and the construction of software and hardware technology platforms, as well as potential strategic acquisitions. According to the prospectus, as of the end of March 2026, the company has launched over 150 software-hardware co-designed products, serving more than 220 customers globally, and has accumulated over 620 IP modules and more than 20 series of RISC-V cores. If the offering proceeds smoothly, it will become the first RISC-V-focused chip company listed in Hong Kong (Hong Kong has already listed RISC-V as one of seven key emerging industries in its 2026-27 budget); this publication will continue to track the pricing and listing milestones.

II. Product and Ecosystem Updates

2.1 Efinix Releases Sapphire RV64: 64-Bit RISC-V FPGA Soft-Core SoC

Date: 2026-09-28　Source: Business Wire “Efinix Brings 64-Bit RISC-V Performance to Embedded FPGA Designs With New Sapphire RV64 SoC” (09-28), Efinix Sapphire RV64 User Guide (official documentation)

FPGA vendor Efinix has released the Sapphire RV64 SoC: extending the Sapphire soft-core family from 32-bit to 64-bit RISC-V—a seven-stage pipeline supporting optional A/F/D/C and Zba/Zbb/Zbs/Zicbom extensions; on-chip 4 to 512KB RAM, multi-way L1 instruction/data caches, optional L2 cache (64 to 512KB, 8-way), branch predictor, and hardware/software prefetching; paired with DDR/LPDDR4 memory controllers, configurable with 1 to 4 64-bit cores at 20 to 400MHz. The product targets edge AI and embedded Linux scenarios “where applications have outgrown the addressing, cache, and I/O limits of 32-bit cores,” and can be used across all Titanium/Topaz FPGAs and Trion (T20 and above) devices, with graphical configuration via the Efinity IP Manager. Official documentation states the core is similar to the open-source VexiiRiscv and has been optimized for the company’s own devices.

2.2 Xcena MX1 Detailed by EE Times: CXL Memory Integrating Over a Thousand RISC-V Cores

Date: 2026-09-28　Source: EE Times “Xcena Cuts Data Movement to Address Memory Bottlenecks” (09-28), IndexBox “Xcena MX1: CXL Near-Memory Processing with 2TB DDR5 and 1,000+ RISC-V Cores” (09-28)

EE Times and IndexBox published articles on 09-28 detailing South Korean semiconductor company Xcena’s MX1 computational CXL memory device (announced at Hot Chips 2026 this summer): a single card integrates up to 2TB DDR5 (4 channels, CXL 3.2 / PCIe 6.0), exposes SSDs as byte-addressable memory via InfiniteMemory, and integrates a near-memory compute unit comprising over 1,000 in-house 1.4GHz RISC-V cores (tech media reports 3,072 cores, with 24 subsystems sharing 128MB L3) plus an approximately 3 TFLOPS FP32/FP16 vector engine; official figures cite up to 4.7x throughput and 18.7x energy efficiency on data analytics workloads compared with a host CPU accessed via CXL. Developers program in C/C++/Rust via an LLVM toolchain, with mass production planned for end-2026 and revenue beginning in 2027. The large-scale deployment of RISC-V cores in “memory wall” scenarios is the highlight of these reports.
