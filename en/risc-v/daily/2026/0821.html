I. Open-Source Community and Kernel/Release Updates

1.1 Linux 7.2 Release Roundup: RISC-V NR_CPUS Raised to 256, ESWIN Enters defconfig (08-21)

Date: 2026-08-16 (kernel release, LKML) / 2026-08-21 (CNX architecture-level roundup, 06:00 Beijing)　Source: CNX Software, Linux Journal

Linus Torvalds released Linux 7.2 on 08-16 (LKML), with CNX publishing an architecture-level roundup on 08-21; RISC-V updates were dense. General highlights: cache-aware task scheduling (threads of the same process co-located by LLC domain), a CFS-inspired Fair GPU scheduler, USB4STREAM direct streaming, and phase four of the swap table. RISC-V specifics:

  • NR_CPUS 64-bit default raised to 256 — the official rationale points directly at domestic many-core machines: SpacemiT already ships an 80-core RVA23 server, and the dual-socket SG2042-based Sophgo Pisces reaches 128 cores, so the default of 64 is no longer sufficient;
  • ESWIN SoC added to the RISC-V defconfig — a domestic vendor entering the mainline default configuration, a higher bar than ordinary driver inclusion;
  • T-Head TH1520: PWM fixes, WiFi enabled on both the BeagleV Ahead and Lichee Pi 4a boards;
  • SpacemiT: SPI/thermal drivers and I2C enablement added for K1, USB2 PHY and ASoC drivers added for K3, reboot cell added for the P1 chip; extensive board-level peripheral enablement for K1 (Milk-V Jupiter eMMC, Muse Pi Pro EEPROM/PCIe/QSPI/USB, OrangePi R2S PMIC/USB3, OrangePi RV2 full peripherals); two new K1 boards added, CoM260-IFX and DeepComputing FML13V05;
  • Sophgo: bindings added for Milk-V Duo S; SG2042/SG2044 device tree fixes (CPU addresses converted to hexadecimal, MSI actually only 16 interrupts, PCIe RC marked cache-coherent);
  • StarFive: the JH8100 interrupt controller was renamed and reworked into the JHB100 version after the chip was discontinued without reaching mass production; JH7110 added to the SiFive ccache non-standard cache operation list (GPU DMA non-coherent); JHB100 gained new clint bindings and SGMII GMAC support;
  • Core fixes: kexec_file early boot with large memory, unconditional sfence.vma, kfence false-positive fix, CFI vDSO patches, ftrace_graph_ret_addr, BUILDTIME_MCOUNT_SORT to reduce boot overhead, and more.

The 7.3 merge window has opened with the release (Linus says 40 PRs are already pending); Phoronix reports that 7.3 will phase out legacy 32-bit ARM platforms on a large scale (an ARM-side restructuring, with impact on RISC-V yet to be seen). This roundup confirms that the Linux mainline is laying the groundwork at scale for “80-128 core domestic RISC-V servers.”

1.2 NetBSD 11 Lands 64-bit RISC-V Support (08-20)

Date: 2026-08-20 (The Register report 21:45 Beijing; HN in parallel)　Source: The Register, HN discussion

NetBSD’s 19th major release, 11.0 (released at the end of July), received a lengthy review from The Register: it adds 64-bit RISC-V support (a Tier II platform, targeting QEMU virtualization environments), making NetBSD the third BSD-family operating system after Linux and FreeBSD to provide mainline RISC-V support. 11.0 also brings: a MICROVM-specific kernel (roughly 10 ms cold boot for x86 guests, measured on 2020s-era processors), improved POSIX.1-2024 and C23 compliance, enhanced Linux syscall compatibility, Xen/QEMU runtime optimizations, 52 Tier II platforms and 9 Tier I platforms. The review also notes that NetBSD retains its “old-school” style: a text-based sysinst installer, manual edits to /etc/rc.conf, and scarce commercial drivers. The significance for the RISC-V ecosystem: the BSD camp’s RISC-V support is now filled in, landing first in virtualization scenarios (QEMU).

II. Product and Ecosystem Updates

2.1 Andes Shuts Down Condor Design Center, Cuzco Data Center-Class Core IP Returns to Parent (08-17/08-20)

Date: 2026-08-17 (Jon Peddie Research first report) / 2026-08-20 (EE Times follow-up, 06:00 Beijing)　Source: Jon Peddie Research, design-reuse repost, EE Times

Andes Technology has formally closed Condor Computing, its high-end design center in Austin, Texas (founded 2023-03, nearly 100 people, over 85% in R&D). Condor carried Andes’ flagship project for breaking into data center-class RISC-V: the Cuzco core — RVA23-compliant, licensable, and built on a new patented microarchitecture (deep out-of-order execution plus an unconventional time-scheduling design, disclosed at Hot Chips 2025). Current status: the Cuzco IP has returned to parent company Andes, with no further test silicon development; the team was cut from 50 to 15 and then to a single-digit number of engineers who truly understand the IP; former CEO Ty Garibay has moved to Ambiq as VP of SoC Design. The closure is part of a cost-cutting plan announced in 2026-04: Chairman and CEO Frankwell Lin acknowledged that the company expanded too quickly over the past three to four years while revenue failed to keep pace, and plans to cut operating expenses by 10-20% and reduce global headcount from 480 to about 450 (EE Times estimates it is already around 400).

Industry significance: Jon Peddie Research analyst David Harold said plainly that this is “a setback for Andes’ high-end ambitions and a retreat in RISC-V’s effort to establish a credible Arm/x86 alternative in the data center.” This stands in contrast to 1.1, where the Linux mainline is paving the way for many-core servers — the software side is accelerating, while the IP vendor camp is reshuffling and contracting.

With sustained high-intensity merges over multiple consecutive days, this round’s focus has shifted from memory access correctness to writeback queue scheduling and branch prediction performance fine-tuning — characteristic of the architecture performance wrap-up phase.

Date: 2026-08-20 (09:07 ~ 17:25 Beijing)　Source: spacemit-com/buildroot-ext, uboot-2022.10, mpp, v2d-test

The SpacemiT organization logged 22 commits within the window, centered on the K1 software stack v2.2.11 full-package update: buildroot-ext, uboot-2022.10, and v2d-test were synchronized to v2.2.11, with K1 buildroot changing GENERIC_HOSTNAME to “K1-Buildroot” and expanding the root filesystem to 1GB (accommodating a larger software set); mpp media pipeline received two engineering refactors (stream manager moved out of the mpi interface layer, AL module build targets split), and v2d-test fixed K1 buildroot build errors. Continuing the cadence reported on 08-18 of “K1 mainline kernel 7.2 landing in Yocto via meta-riscv,” this round represents BSP full-package versioned delivery, with the media pipeline evolving toward an independently maintainable module structure.

Data-center-class IP camp reshuffling: Andes shutting down Condor and Cuzco returning to the parent company is another signal following SiFive’s contraction; the weight of the “Chinese duo” (T-Head/StarFive) in the high-end RISC-V core race rises further, with software-side paving and IP-side contraction coexisting;
