TileLang Weekly Report (2026-11-02 ~ 2026-11-06)

2026-11-06 Kubuds

Reporting period: Monday, November 2, 2026 to Friday, November 6, 2026, 5 working days in total Sources: 5 issues of this publication’s daily TileLang community updates (11-02, 11-03, 11-04, 11-05, 11-06); the collection windows of the five issues are contiguous, together covering 168 hours from 10-30 07:00 to 11-06 07:00 (the Monday issue used a 72-hour window covering the previous weekend; Tuesday through Friday used 24-hour windows), with no overlap with the previous weekly report (10-26 to 10-30); additionally verified via a supplementary 168-hour window check of the repositories in the tile-ai organization, each repository’s releases page and tags, and a four-source inspection review of TileLang×RISC-V (see appendix)

I. Weekly Highlights

  • Ascend 950’s persistent FlashMLA kernel lands directly in the mainline, written in a compiler-schedulable style rather than ported from a separate repo (merged 11-02, #3678): A single change adding 1,997 lines, deleting 17 lines, across 20 files and 42 commits, covering both the BF16 prefill and compact FP8 decode paths, with support for optional FP8 and FP4 extra KV cache, attention sink, invalid indices, and per-query independent lengths. The attention loop, online softmax, dequantization, and scheduling are all expressed in TileLang, relying on T.Stage to let the compiler manage buffer versions and synchronization; QK and PV share two different layout views of a single L0B storage ring, L1 placement is specified directly via T.annotate_buffer_addresses; prefill splits four disjoint output slices within a single accumulator allocation, draining each slice as soon as its normalization completes. To make this style viable, the compiler side gained three capabilities in the same period: L0C-to-UB copies preserve NZ output, each AIV stream is selected before its own pipeline, and explicitly scheduled stages preserve source order when their physical issue phases coincide. The target shapes are written very narrowly: 64 heads, Dqk and Dv both 512, topk a multiple of 64. Source: https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/pull/3678

  • ThreadSync changed three times in one week—first making way for the migration, then restoring performance and proofs (11-01, 11-04, 11-05): #3733 on 11-01 fully reverted the precise thread counting (#3291) merged three weeks earlier, with a blunt rationale—that change moved expensive count queries ahead of full-block barrier checks, so even when all threads reach the barrier the proof still runs; once TVM’s solver budget is exhausted it returns partial counts, and legitimate remap and sequence helper kernels fail to compile outright with “Cannot prove a thread rectangle”; three correctness improvements (quotient guards, exact participating-thread bounds, non-rectangular thread sets) were withdrawn together, with #3732 left as the tracking issue for re-optimization after the migration. #3761 on 11-04 addressed a side effect of the revert: to fix the duplicate named-barrier IDs in #3643, the same group of threads cannot reuse an ID across scopes, so TileKernels’ mhc_pre_big_fuse kernel with 96 threads per block gained an extra ID on its two-stage cp.async pipeline, and the compiled artifact’s declared barrier resources went from 4 to 5, dropping the barrier occupancy ceiling on B300 at hidden dimension 1280 from 16 blocks/SM to 12 blocks/SM; the fix lets ThreadSync accept an ordered scope list and, before sharing an ID across scopes, uses a new analyzer to prove the participation predicate always holds—acceptance criteria being resource declarations back to 4 and occupancy back to 16 blocks/SM, without reverting the #3643 fix. #3787, under review on 11-05, continues to fill prover blind spots: at 192 threads, forms like tx < 160 and tx // 32 % 2 == 0—a boundary term and a warp-index modulo term joined by and—are misreported, the root cause being that the FloorDiv and FloorMod encodings cannot establish the relationship between the two divisions under the “upper bound plus modulo” combination; the fix rewrites threadIdx.x as warp_index * warp_size + lane. Sources: https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/pull/3733, https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/pull/3761, https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/pull/3787

  • The TIRx migration went from “verbal rationale” to three pieces of hard evidence in one week, with the foundation swap entering public preparation (11-03 to 11-06): #3736 on 11-03 deleted tilelang/language/ast/, tilelang/language/parser/, and tilelang/language/tir/entry.py together—leftovers from an early local TVMScript parser fork—adding 1 line, deleting 3,384 lines across 10 files, with the language layer now using the upstream tvm.tirx.script.parser and AST and statement override registration in tilelang.language.overrides. #3752, merged 11-06, together with its prerequisite tile-ai/tvm#91, turns Z3’s rlimit from a hardcoded 100,000 into something configurable per kernel; the author’s words: “this indirectly fixes a lot of Z3-prover-related issues.” #3803 on 11-06 submitted a complete migration draft: replacing the vendored TVM with a standalone Apache TIRx, migrating the frontend, IR transformations, code generation, JIT adaptation, and packaging all to the native object model, pinning apache/tvm-tirx at 7e49a148 and apache/tvm-ffi at 3e906bc6, keeping the dependency tree cleanly upstream with no injected patches. All three lines point to the same thing: the top priority for the mainline at this stage is swapping the foundation, and all other optimizations must make way for it. Sources: https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/pull/3736, https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/pull/3752, https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/pull/3803

  • “Silently generating wrong code” became the week’s dominant defect type, spanning four release versions (11-03 to 11-05): The most serious is #3808—after condition-related scalarization, the shared-memory epilogue is missing a __syncthreads(), so versions 0.1.12, 0.1.13, 0.1.14, and 0.1.15 all silently generate incorrect CUDA with no compile error whatsoever; the reporter provided a complete runnable reproduction script. The same family includes #3800 (with warp specialization disabled, T.Pipelined reloads the next stage before the tcgen05 barrier wait, so the MMA reads a rewritten operand), #3721 (on sm_100a, T.gemm on TMEM with threads/32 not dividing N produces not a single tcgen05.mma in the PTX, and the product is silently dropped), #3775 (in production TP2 sglang with the nvrtc backend, CUDA graph capture is corrupted, with three byte-identical configurations reproducing three different first failure points), and #1991 and #1992 (on Ascend 910_9362 with threads=2, AscendVidReduction unconditionally halves the injected tmp workspace too, and sin/cos on an indirectly addressed tile computes 3978/4096 elements wrong, i.e., 97.1%). This family reports no errors and passes routine tests; it can only be caught by targeted reproduction. Sources: https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/issues/3808, https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/issues/3800, https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang-ascend/issues/1991

  • TileFoundry pushes synchronization from “the analysis can prove it” to “emission must match the proof” (11-02 to 11-06): #283 builds “how shared-memory accesses should be ordered” into a provable analysis layer—walking each function in lowering’s emission order, planning commits, waits, and scope-local fences before conflicting accesses; of 83 scheduling fixtures, 52 are planned and 31 rejected (24 use TMA copies, 6 use wgmma, whose completion semantics are not yet declared); because by default no caller requests it, the 75 fixtures that finalize are byte-identical to main. #285, merged 11-04, is its execution side: CUDA lowering emits according to the synchronization plan and verifies each item in reverse, throwing LoweringError on mismatch; on a single sm_89, 6 of 7 buildable programs match the reference exactly with synccheck and memcheck fully clean, while those same 7 lowered the main way produce 4 wrong results, the direct symptom being a maximum deviation of 659 in sm_89 E4M3 GEMM. #286, merged 11-06, adds WGMMA’s asynchronous completion semantics, and #284, merged 11-05, builds FSDP and HSDP training semantics into the HIR evaluator and aligns with real 2×2 PyTorch HSDP on four-card sm90. The author kept an explicit caveat on #286: the matrix instruction emitter is still not wired up, and this change neither proves execution correctness nor delivers performance. Sources: https://github.com/tile-ai/TileFoundry/pull/283, https://github.com/tile-ai/TileFoundry/pull/285, https://github.com/tile-ai/TileFoundry/pull/286

Also in the period: a roofline pricing rewrite (three physically impossible bandwidth rows corrected from 7.48, 7.50, and 5.30 TB/s to 0.62, 0.62, and 2.65 TB/s, #2830) and separate fitting of three-stage tile factors (selection regret down from 0.89% to 0.06%, #2836). Sources: https://github.com/tile-ai/TileOPs/pull/2808, https://github.com/tile-ai/TileOPs/pull/2832, https://github.com/tile-ai/TileOPs/pull/2830

Compiler, Language Layer, and Execution Path

  • Ascend 950 FlashMLA kernel and its supporting compiler capabilities (#3678, merged 11-02, 20 files +1997/-17): see the first item in Top Stories. Notably, this change added the three low-level capabilities required for “explicit scheduling” to the mainline at once rather than patching them in the Ascend branch—L0C-to-UB copies preserving NZ output, each AIV stream being selected before the pipeline, and source order being preserved when physical issue phases coincide.
  • Two consecutive fixes for the vector-width regression from cast decoupling (#3734 merged 11-02, #3759 merged 11-05): #3734 fixed Cannot convert type float32x16 to CUDA type in ViT forward on SM100—the vectorization planner chose 16 lanes for 256-bit BF16 storage and deferred cast constraints, and after #3579 made decoupling skip the memory-read guard, final legalization still followed the deferred strategy, stretching the residual FP32 expression into a 512-bit vector; the fix makes defer_cast_constraints a C++ parameter of the planner rather than an IR attribute, so the guard’s FP32-to-BF16 cast drops to 8 lanes while successfully decoupled BF16 copies keep 16. #3759 cleaned up its tail: an int64 cast in a condition previously squeezed the data vector width from 16 lanes to 4 even when the condition was fully uniform across 16 lanes; the fix reuses condition-uniformity analysis for the cast-width exemption, visiting if statements, tirx.if_then_else, and Select conditions with scalar predicates separately, while casts inside clauses remain restricted.
  • The three-stage evolution of ThreadSync (#3733, #3761, #3787): see the second item in Top Stories. #3733 was explicitly a stopgap, stating “making way for the TIRX migration,” and the accompanying TVM fork optimization proposal tile-ai/tvm#89 has been closed. The most noteworthy part of #3761’s 401-line change is the attribution experiment: the author reproduced the same slowdown by changing only the number of barrier resources declared in the compiled artifact, without touching any executable instruction, turning “performance regression” from an impression into a single identifiable variable.
  • Subtraction in the language layer and frontend: #3736 deleted 3384 lines of the legacy TVMScript parser fork (see the third item in Top Stories), with the risk of keeping it stated concretely—if anyone directly imports tilelang.language.parser.operation, it registers conflicting operators on tirx.PrimExpr and tirx.IterVar; #3760 introduced a bounded in-process transformation cache for nested T.macro, storing only context-independent transformation results and rebinding macro instances to the current globals and closure values, solving the problem where “the kernel hits the cache but the macro is still re-parsed and recompiled”; #3802 added T.annotate_host_mapped_buffers, writing the indices of annotated parameters into the PrimFunc’s tl.host_mapped_params attribute, with the runtime validating registration status via cuMemHostGetFlags and resolving the device-visible address via cuMemHostGetDevicePointer, while ordinary pageable memory is still rejected.
  • Debugging and observability: #3703 changed #line directives from “anchoring only the first line of each TIR statement” to tiered emission (0 off, 1 statement-level, 2 and above re-anchoring line by line), implemented in the C-family output stream shared by CUDA, HIP, CPU C, C host, Metal, and Ascend, with TILELANG_EMIT_LINE_DIRECTIVES added as the Python-side default; the author notes that the CUDA and HIP test cases were skipped locally and cuda-gdb’s actual behavior was not directly verified. The LSP repo followed up the same day, aligning CUDA debugging to the new line mapping; the VS Code extension repo followed up again on 11-04, updating docs and renaming the artifact to vsix.
  • Autotuning semantics: #3546 changed the supply closure of set_autotune_inputs from “parameters never passed at the call site” to get_current_device(); previously, on machines without CUDA, the fallback branch moved every captured tensor to CPU, and every tuning round failed due to a launcher device mismatch. On dual-card Ascend950, three Ascend autotuning regression tests all failed before the fix and all passed after. The related #3784 adds a device sync before and after the cross-device copy probe in AscendC CI, bringing asynchronous errors back within the scope of try ... except RuntimeError—previously they surfaced a step late, appearing as an unrelated torch.arange() reporting a 4-byte memcpy size mismatch.
  • Z3 solving resources go from hardcoded to configurable (#3752, merged 11-06, depends on tile-ai/tvm#91): rlimit still defaults to 100,000, and behavior is unchanged when unconfigured. This is the other end of the same direction as the concurrent Ascend-side tightening of the AutoSchedule period lower bound (#3776, Z3 solve time down 10.04%): one changes the search budget, the other changes the search starting point.

Correctness and Bug Fixes

  • ThreadSync and barriers: beyond the full revert in #3733 (see Top Stories) and the cross-domain reuse in #3761 (see Top Stories), #3765 in review explicitly accepts “partial barriers with a single thread-index bound guard” when the thread dimension is a whole number of warps; #3767 replaces the internal StructuralEqual assertion for multi-index fragment access with a ValueError carrying both index expressions; #3786 turns out-of-bounds T.copy destination regions into a compile-time error; #3788 fixes the fast path of ProveFragmentContains—it returned true directly when a large fragment was fully copied, skipping the thread-ownership check, so “small fragment owns threads 0 to 127, large fragment owns only 0 to 95” was misjudged as contained.
  • The silent-wrong-code family: beyond #3808, #3800, #3721, #3775, and #1991/#1992 (see the fourth item in Top Stories), this week also brought #3774 (a 16×16 shared-memory permutation performed only the row shift and dropped the column offset, getting 223 of 256 elements wrong, while the same code at 32×32 was correct), #3773 (three shared-memory copies plus a GEMM inside a uniform while loop: the serial control was 0 errors in 100 runs, while the while version failed 21 of 100 runs with 10 distinct outputs), and #3763 (nested integer floor division (x // -2) // 4 reporting Let cannot bind the same var to two different values at int16, 64 elements, 32 threads). These three, together with #3775, form a concentrated bug-report surface for the “consumer GPU + WSL + pip install” combination.
  • Root cause in dependency analysis and sync insertion: #3749 is the week’s most technically substantial bug report—two consecutive SIMD loads lost their dependency on an earlier GM-to-UB copy, with the root cause being that the logical region reconstructed by MemoryAccessDetector::LogicalPointerRegion missed part of the physical load range: in the minimal repro, work.shape is (2, 128), the generated read descriptor covers physical [64, 192), but the reconstructed region is [0, 128), so dependency analysis considered the input DMA unrelated to the load, AutoSchedule moved the input copy after the load, and InsertSync also emitted no MTE2-to-V set/wait. In the same window the main repo also gained 8 issues pointing at code generation and sync insertion in the Ascend AscendC and PTO backends (#3740 through #3749), 7 of them filed by the same reporter, all with reproducible prim_funcs and line-by-line generated-code comparisons.
  • Internal errors as soon as a boundary condition breaks: #3738 fixes “T.copy from a global region smaller than the padded shared tile (e.g. K=100 copied into BK=112) being judged a full-tile TMA copy and hard-failing”; #3766 fixes TMA byte counting for cta_group::2—the bytes of a CTA pair both land on the even CTA’s barrier, while tma_copy with a cluster barrier previously expected cluster_size × tile_bytes only on rank 0, which happened to be right for (2,1,1) and (1,2,1) but wrong for (4,1,1) and (2,2,1).
  • Operator and example boundaries: #3709 makes the CPU scalar GEMM clean only the destination region; #3712 makes LegalizeNegativeIndex skip unreachable branches; #3714 adds round-to-nearest-even for fp32-to-tf32 conversion (cvt.rn.tf32.f32; non-saturating requires SM90+, saturating requires SM100+); #3702 makes several attention examples derive tiles from shared_memory_per_block_optin, taking the CUDA 9 items on RTX PRO 6000 (sm_120) from “2 pass, 7 fail” to 9 of 9 passing, while H100 has a 227 KiB optional limit and still uses the original tile.
  • Two semantic deviations on the Ascend side: #3762 reports that T.simd.vcvt(src, "uint8", sat=False) silently ignores sat=False for int32 vectors and always saturates; the documented wraparound semantics (-1 becomes 255, -128 becomes 128, 300 becomes 44) were measured as truncation (-1 becomes 0, -128 becomes 0, 300 becomes 255), affecting paths like the f32-to-int8 tail of int8 quantization; #3789 reports that the PTO backend emits signed pto.si32/si64 for integer T.atomic_add while the PTO atomic interface accepts only unsigned carriers, with fixes in two directions, #3792 and #3794, filed the same day.

III. Multi-Backend Adaptation (Ascend / Hygon / Moore Threads / MetaX)

Ascend: From “Adaptation Target” to “First-Release Target”

  • On the first-release side: The FlashMLA kernel (#3678) targets Ascend 950 and is written directly into the mainline, using low-level capabilities such as T.Stage and T.annotate_buffer_addresses that are intended for explicit scheduling, rather than opening a separate repo for porting; the target shape has 64 heads, Dqk and Dv of 512, and topk as a multiple of 64. The accompanying bulk-copy capability was completed on 11-03: #3689 lets a [B, M, N] region be written as a single T.copy, with lowering generating bulk ND2NZ descriptors, preserving the source’s batch and row strides, routing transposed batches through DN2NZ, and skipping source batches that exceed the GM boundary; validation was on Ascend950DT with CANN 9.2, with 50 AscendC example tests and 1733 Ascend unit tests passing, of which 28 BF16/FP32 device cases cover batch and M/N tail blocks, whole-batch out-of-bounds, non-contiguous source strides, destination storage larger than source, and rectangular transpose.
  • Synchronization correctness: #1981 fixes the synchronization annotation of copy_pipe_to_l1 — A2/A3’s PTO cross-core V-to-C communication is relayed through GM, and the three-stage copy belongs to AIV’s MTE3, AIC’s MTE2, and AIC’s MTE1 respectively, yet it was labeled PIPE_MTE3 in operation_config.h, so automatic synchronization waited on the AIC core’s own MTE3, whose queue is empty and whose event is set immediately, allowing the MTE1 read of L1 to be released before the MTE2 write completes; there are two pieces of reproduction evidence: after changing PIPE_MTE3 to PIPE_MTE2 in the generated source, 10/10 runs recovered exactness, while locally on the latest mainline 10/10 runs were wrong, with up to 16384/16384 elements mismatched in a single run. The fix splits the name rather than replacing it outright: copy_pipe_to_l1 is relabeled PIPE_MTE2, and a new copy_pipe_to_l1_bind is labeled PIPE_MTE1 (the A5 direct-connect path), avoiding fixing A2/A3 while breaking A5.
  • Converging on the mainline: The RFC in #1971 lays out the replacement route for the synchronization implementation — port the mainline synchronization core and the standalone Z3 proof service, hook into the existing compilation pipeline through the old TIR adaptation layer, retain TVM, scheduling, memory planning, AscendC/PTO codegen, and the runtime ABI, and after acceptance switch the default implementation and delete the old algorithm; the accompanying #1965 migrates the mainline’s z3-based insert sync to the Ascend repo; #1975 is the migration of the repo-level skeleton. The three are three layers of the same path. In the same period, #3752 makes Z3’s rlimit configurable per kernel, and #3735 proposes proving the disjointness of modular indices within thread sync — precisely addressing the proof-budget problem left by last week’s rollback from both ends: “configuring solver resources” and “reducing the shapes that need proving.”

VI. Trend Observations

First, the TIRx migration has effectively become the top priority on the mainline, and it is starting to take the form of “subtraction.” Three pieces of evidence within one week: on 11-01, the entire set of ThreadSync correctness improvements was reverted for the migration (#3733, while also closing the optimization proposal #89 on the TVM fork side); on 11-03, 3,384 lines of the legacy parser fork were deleted (#3736); on 11-06, a complete migration draft was submitted (#3803, pinning two upstream commits and explicitly stating that no patches will be injected). In between, Z3 resources went from hardcoded to configurable (#3752 and tvm #91). What these actions share is that they pull complexity off the critical path: better to temporarily fall back to a conservative implementation than to let the internal structure of vendored dependencies keep growing inside the language layer. The cost is that during the migration window, the mainline will have a batch of paths that are “known not to be optimal”; the benefit is that once the foundation upgrade is complete, all downstream backends can adapt against a stable IR boundary.

Second, the form of correctness risk has changed: from “fails to compile” to “compiles, runs without errors, but produces wrong results,” and it lurks across versions. This week, at least seven issues belong to this family: #3808 is missing a __syncthreads() across four versions from 0.1.12 to 0.1.15; #3800 lets the MMA read an overwritten operand on Blackwell’s tensor memory path; #3721 silently drops the entire GEMM product on sm_100a; #3775 corrupts CUDA graph capture in production; #3749 makes the dependency analysis blind to the physical load range; #1991/#1992 make sin/cos compute 97.1% of elements incorrectly under Ascend with threads=2; #3774 and #3773 produce wrong results on consumer-grade cards. This family hurts users the most because it turns the premise of “whether the compiler is correct” into a hypothesis that needs verification. This week’s response direction is to turn correctness into a refutable proof—TileFoundry’s #283/#285 prove before emitting and reverse-verify after emitting; #3786 and #3787 change silently generating wrong code into compile-time errors or explicit rejection—rather than continuing to increase the number of unit tests.

Third, Ascend’s role has shifted from “adaptation target” to “first-launch target,” with engineering advancing on two legs simultaneously. One leg is architecture: FlashMLA, a core kernel on DeepSeek’s own decoding path, is written directly into the mainline targeting Ascend 950, and it uses low-level capabilities such as T.Stage and annotate_buffer_addresses that are prepared for explicit scheduling; together with three supporting changes on the compiler side, the Ascend path is moving from “it runs” to “it runs according to scheduling intent.” The other leg is the skeleton: #1975 moves the entire TileLang-Ascend into the mainline multi-backend framework with 2,887 files and 600,000 added lines, using arch="dav-2201" for backend dispatch; mlir-ascend’s #208 writes Codex’s three workflows—initialization, onboarding, and recovery—along with task handoff into .workspace/. Looking at “the migration repo” and “using agents to migrate the repo” in the same week, one can see that the bottleneck on the Ascend side has shifted from “missing operators” to “missing a consistent engineering skeleton and a reusable migration process.”

Fourth, the competitive focus of operator libraries has shifted entirely to selector quality and preparation cost; the remaining room for performance gaps is not in “how to compute.” Among the nine TileOPs merges on 11-05, only two actually change the GPU kernel: broadcast in-place read and native-width tiles; the reduction layout cache saves call preparation (4.33% to 6.17%, with generated source unchanged), and the three-stage tile factor changes a constant (regret drops from 0.89% to 0.06%). Combined with the roofline pricing rewrite and the change of L2 flush to read-only, a clear route emerges: first enumerate the legal configurations for each shape, then rank them with a unified cost model, and write the calibration process as a reproducible contract (#2826 has already written the three steps of “scan, fit, check” into the docstring). If this route is done right, performance no longer depends on manual tuning of individual kernels; if done wrong, it degenerates into “some shapes are especially fast, and other shapes rely on luck.”

Fifth, domestic backends are shifting from “merging one big backend at once” to “pushing the smallest reviewable units to the public repository.” Hygon has moved from its in-house branch to two formal PRs, #33 and #34; Moore Threads ends two months of silence with a single README; mlir-ascend uses three small PRs to move tl.sub/mul, tl.shl, and tl.shr one by one into the TLIR dialect; and Ascend fills in engineering details such as core ratios, exception dumps, and performance skills item by item. What the three lines share is that they no longer wait for one big merge: this is good for upstream, because reviewers can comment on the layout and lowering design early instead of seeing it only at the time of a 30,000-line one-shot merge; it also has a cost for downstream—the mainline will be in a state of “known to have several things not working” during such windows, requiring stronger pre-release regression discipline.

One final risk surface: the foundation switch will expose the cost of all glue layers at once. If #3803 moves forward, the vendored structure of 3rdparty/tvm will be replaced by two pinned upstream commits, at which point all backends that depend on TVM internal APIs (the glue layers of Ascend, Hygon, and mlir-ascend) will have to change accordingly. The draft explicitly states “no dependency patches will be injected,” which is both a commitment and a constraint—this week, mlir-ascend moving the BishengIR adapter into contrib and Ascend forking performance skills by chip generation can both be seen as replaceable surfaces reserved for this kind of foundation switch. To judge whether this line is healthy, look at whether, within two weeks after the migration draft is merged, these backends can complete adaptation without touching the core lowering.
