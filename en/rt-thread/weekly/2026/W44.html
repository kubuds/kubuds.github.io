I. Weekly Highlights

  • RT-Thread Smart lands in a single day after ten days in the queue, then is reverted from the default config seven and a half hours later (10-29): Proposal #11896, created on October 18, was merged at 09:29, enabling RT-Thread Smart for qemu-virt64-riscv and qemu-virt64-aarch64 — 14 files, +1263/-1075, 14 commits, with a new applications/mnt.c that mounts virtio-blk0 as the root directory and mounts ptyfs and procfs; at 14:48 and 14:50, the terminal compatibility proposal #11890 and the lwp syscall split proposal #11894 landed in succession; at 16:59, proposal #11942 restored the default configs of both BSPs entirely to the standard kernel (7 files, +51/-220), with Smart moved to running separately under utest’s config overlay. “Enable—verify—revert” all in one day is effectively the maintainers defining Smart’s place: keep the default config predictable, and run Smart through a separate overlay.

  • RT-Smart’s ioctl user-pointer boundary draws four reports in a row, extending from the terminal all the way to block devices, pipes, and mailbox (10-28 ~ 10-31): On 10-28, #11937 pointed out that the terminal subsystem port dropped FreeBSD’s copyin precondition, so an unprivileged process could read and write arbitrary kernel memory via open plus ioctl; on 10-29, #11952 repeated the same pattern on the block device side, with a 24-byte partition table written to a kernel address specified by the caller; on 10-30, #11958 landed on POSIX pipes, where FIONREAD and FIONWRITE write ring-buffer lengths directly into an unvalidated user pointer; on 10-31, #11960 escalated to the mailbox, where sys_mb_send() forwards a user-supplied pointer as a kernel object directly, enabling arbitrary kernel read/write. The common cause across all four is that RT-Smart’s ioctl adaptation layer assumes “the argument is already a kernel address,” while the user-space entry point performs no such conversion. The terminal-side fix proposal #11941 was submitted on the evening of 10-28 (6 files, +577/-52); the other three sites have no corresponding fix yet.

  • 21 commits and 16 proposals on mainline, with a rhythm of “stall first, then clear the backlog in bulk” (10-25 ~ 10-30): This week master took 21 commits and 16 merged proposals, distributed as one on 10-25, zero on 10-26, four on 10-27, seven on 10-28, seven on 10-29, and two on 10-30. A considerable share of the 10-27 to 10-29 merges were backlog proposals held for nine days or more — the four on 10-27 had author dates of 10-18, and the Smart group on 10-29 was also created on 10-18. This is the same pattern as last week’s “three days of silence, three hours of wrap-up”: development-side output lands all at once after review approval rather than advancing at a steady daily pace.

  • Automotive-grade TriCore (AURIX) enters the Env SDK via a “toolchain-first” approach (10-26): Two RT-Thread/sdk proposals were merged in succession: tricore-gcc v13.4.1 (GCC 13.4.1, binutils 2.40, newlib, GDB 14.0, covering the TC2xx/TC3xx/TC4xx three generations of chips) and qemu-tricore v1.0.0 (QEMU 10.2.94, supporting the TC277, TC397B, and TC4D7 machine models), released as toolchains-ci v1.12 and v1.13 respectively. It is important to distinguish that this is only a compile-and-simulate path: the main repo’s libcpu has no tricore among its existing 19 architectures, and there is no AURIX board-level directory under bsp; the batch of attempts around 2020 that relied on the TASKING commercial toolchain have all been closed. Compared with the old path, this step first solves “can compile, can run, can debug.”

  • Four test proposals held for over three months merged at once, a concentrated catch-up on test infrastructure (10-28): #11601, created on July 9, was merged on 10-28 at 14:44, bringing in a fix to utest summary statistics (a later unit passing no longer erases the failure count of earlier testcases), cutting the netdev test cases’ public-network dependency on 8.8.8.8 and Baidu, excluding signal 0 from installation under musl, and two clang-format cleanups. All three classes of problems carry the reverse risk of “the test ran wrong but the report says it’s fine”; only after the fix is CI’s verdict trustworthy. Complementing this, #11942 on 10-29 rewrote utest_auto_run’s config selection logic, changing “overwrite with a full git checkout” to appending a single disable switch line to .config.

  • The time subsystem spawns four reports overnight, two fixes move to mergeable status (10-23 ~ 10-26): The same researcher filed a series of reports on ctime.c: settimeofday()’s long type narrowing (under ILP32, legitimate timestamps such as 2040-01-01 are truncated to negative and rejected), gmtime_r() outputting illegal fields for negative timestamps (-1 yields 1970-01-01 00:00:-1), and timer_gettime()’s 32-bit truncation, plus a time baseline investigation ticket with six work items A through F. Fix proposals #11928 (+185/-135) and #11929 (+104/-9) both checked “mature version, mergeable” on 10-26 but were not merged this week. On 10-29, further investigation found that timer_settime() uses a 32-bit variable to hold a result on the order of 1e13, so a 10-second timer is actually armed for about 21 hours (#11938, +142/-5).

  • Version number raised to 5.4.0 development state; two institutionalization documents on version management and release process proposed the same day (10-29 ~ 10-30): On 10-29, #11942 raised RT_VER_NUM from 0x50301 to 0x50400, formally moving mainline into 5.4.0 development state; but releases and tags both remain at v5.3.0 from 09-10, with no pre-release tags or binaries. Two supporting documents appeared on 10-30: defect ticket #11956 proposes establishing version management CI and a release process (monthly reports on the Z line plus manual approval; the Y line every 3 to 6 months combined with hardware validation; split into work item groups B01–B05 and C01–C08), and proposal #11955 adds the mainline version and release process documentation. This is a rare “process-side” signal this week and warrants a check next issue on whether it lands in code.

  • Vendor side is still proposal-first: RA6W1 phase two lands, while the large-volume Allwinner and ESP32-S3 proposals keep soaking in the queue (10-26 ~ 10-30): Renesas RA6W1’s proposal #11934 was merged on 10-28 at 18:52, adding 24 Wi-Fi-side interrupt vectors and renaming two crypto entries in the ROM symbol table with the __rom_ prefix. Allwinner F101 YuzukiNeko and H616’s #11877 (172 files, +35428/-6) and ESP32-S3-DevKitM-1’s #11844 (cumulative +295371/-22825 after a 10-28 refresh) remain in the queue; the latter has been a full month since its 9-28 creation, with review attention falling on the wholesale import of the vendor SDK and license terms, not just whether the functionality runs.

Security and Defects

Date: 2026-10-23 ~ 2026-10-31　Source: Issue #11960

  • 16 new issues were opened this week, more than ten of them security-related. They fall into three families: time and libc semantics (#11922 settimeofday narrowing, #11923 gmtime_r negative timestamps, #11924 timer_gettime truncation, #11925 sys_poll count wraparound, #11927 time baseline investigation), RT-Smart boundaries (#11932 musl thread list use-after-free, #11933 signalfd global count out-of-bounds, #11937 terminal ioctl, #11952 block device ioctl, #11958 pipe ioctl, #11960 mailbox object pointer), and driver parameter validation (#11948 Bluetrum AB32, #11949 Bouffalo, #11951 HPM ADC channel out-of-bounds in all three, plus #11950 DM365 SPI divide-by-zero). There is also #11956, a feature issue for the version management process.
  • The RT-Smart boundary line is the most serious: The terminal, block device, pipe, and mailbox cases are completely identical in form—the adaptation layer treats user pointers as kernel addresses. The mailbox issue is the most severe: sys_mb_send() forwards without any validation, so an attacker can construct a fake object and the read/write range is no longer constrained by the value domain. All four issues trace back to the RT-Smart synchronization commit ecf2d821 of 2022-12-03; the first affected version is v5.0.0, and v5.3.0 and current master are all affected. They can be reproduced on QEMU.
  • Closed within the week: The DHCP raw option out-of-bounds read in #11913 was fixed by #11940, merged on 10-28 (+23/-5, 12 host-side ASAN regressions); the dlmodule uninitialized global variable in #11915 was merged on 10-30 at 10:54; the signalfd out-of-bounds in #11933 received fix proposal #11935 the day after it was opened (+41 lines, changing to a bounded slot lookup located by context, with listener unbinding added on the close path); the first half of #11925 was submitted as #11954 (+28 lines) on 10-30, with sys_poll() directly rejecting “byte count wraparound” counts on 32-bit.
  • Still in the queue or unclaimed: #11878 (lwip-dhcpd lease pool exhaustion infinite loop), #11905 (cromfs directory name out-of-bounds), #11917 (NFS out-of-bounds write), #11919 (NVMe heap leak, fix proposal #11920 not merged), #11902 and #11910 (tmpfs use-after-truncate, fix in #11910), #11918 (9PFS length convergence), #11901 (FDT depth limit), #11931 (serial rx_indicate erroneously cleared), #11932 (musl thread list), plus the four newly opened issues #11937, #11952, #11958, and #11960 (of which #11937 has proposal #11941, and #11938 fixes the timer truncation it introduced).

III. Ecosystem and Vendor Adaptation

Vendor BSPs and Drivers

Date: 2026-10-26 ~ 2026-10-30　Source: PR #11896

  • Infineon TriCore (AURIX): Two packages, tricore-gcc 13.4.1 and qemu-tricore 1.0.0, were added on the Env SDK side, covering TC2xx/TC3xx/TC4xx. The toolchain upstream is NoMore201/tricore-gcc-toolchain (a downstream of EEESlab 11.3.0); the Windows package adds the libwinpthread-1.dll that upstream omitted. The emulator only implements ASCLIN0 serial, STM, IR interrupt routing, SCU and clocks, the SFR bridge, and RAM/Flash mapping — no CAN, Ethernet, ADC, GPIO, or DMA. That is enough to run the kernel and a serial shell, but not enough to validate peripheral drivers.
  • Renesas: The rafw_gen vector table for RA6W1-EK gains 24 Wi-Fi-side interrupt entries (KDMA, TDES CBC, PSK SHA1, SYSPLL and FPLL lock, the full RTC interface group, MRM and D-cache MRM, CryptoCell-312, HSU, MAC timer/receive/transmit/protocol/general/beacon, radio controller), all declared as weak handlers and wired into the vector table. Two crypto entries in the ROM symbol table were also renamed with a __rom_ prefix to avoid name collisions with the RAM side.
  • Allwinner: The BSP proposals for F101 YuzukiNeko and H616 (#11877, 172 files, +35428/-6) are still queued. The F101 side adds the Sun252i device model driver set and the Sunxi DE0/TCON0 display backend (RGB, LVDS, MIPI-DSI); the H616 side is a separate driver set and adds AXP313A regulator support. The D1s QEMU boot script (#11903, 4 files, +145) is likewise under review, with reviewers suggesting it be moved into a unified target subdirectory.
  • Espressif: The ESP-IDF v6.1 adaptation wrapped up on the package side — the main-repo proposal #11926 was closed on 10-26 at 11:37, and the package-repo proposal #19 (fix(scons): prioritize RT-Thread FreeRTOS headers) was merged the same day at 09:15, resolving header priority at the SCons script layer. Board support for ESP32-S3-DevKitM-1 (#11844) is still awaiting review. Note the core distinction: ESP32-S3 is Xtensa, while C3/C6 are RISC-V.
  • STMicroelectronics: The OSPI and OctaFlash proposal (#11912, 32 files) adds OSPI bus, device, and message definitions at the component layer, integrates them with SFUD, and covers read, write, erase, and status query. It also adds a generic STM32 OctoSPI driver and stm32l4r9-st-eval board configuration, enabling SFUD access in both 1-1-1 and 8-8-8 modes. #11819 adds the full STM32 Ethernet ops callback set and SR8201F PHY support in one pass, and lowers the default ADC log level from spam to DBG_WARNING.
  • NXP: The RT1180 industrial Ethernet linker script proposal (#11959) adds Keil scatter-loading (.scf) and IAR configuration (.icf) files for CM33 and CM7 respectively, along with corresponding targets in Kconfig, rtconfig.py, and templates.

Toolchain and Development Environment

Date: 2026-10-26 ~ 2026-10-30　Source: vscode-rt-smart PR #38

  • vscode-rt-smart 0.4.14 (merged 10-26 22:35): 49 files, +883/-4826 — nearly five thousand lines removed in one pass. Media and Reviews

Date: 2026-10-24 ~ 2026-10-27　Source: EET-China: in-depth comparison of three major RTOSes

  • Comparison of three major RTOSes (10-27): Compares FreeRTOS, RT-Thread, and Zephyr across six dimensions: real-time performance, memory footprint, ecosystem, community, AI support, and migration cost. RT-Thread is positioned as an Apache 2.0 lightweight all-in-one package, with a kernel of 3KB Flash plus 1.2KB RAM and built-in POSIX, GUI, file system, and AI packages; its low-power components save 15% to 20% more power than the other two in Stop mode; the selection table assigns extreme low-cost projects under 64KB RAM, along with consumer electronics and wearables, to RT-Thread. The article also acknowledges that Zephyr is stronger in multi-protocol, multi-board, and long-term maintenance scenarios, concluding with a three-way split rather than a one-sided verdict.
  • RISC-V Summit side notes (10-24): The EET-China RT-Thread column publishes a system-software perspective report on the 6th RISC-V China Summit, which comprised 1 main forum, 7 parallel sub-forums, and a Member Day technical session. Two passages relate directly to RT-Thread: during the summit, views were exchanged with multiple RISC-V IP and chip vendors on kernel adaptation, toolchain integration, and joint solution deployment; the industry judges that RISC-V already meets the conditions for scale in embedded and IoT, with the bottleneck shifting from “cores” to system software, and OS participation filling in real-time performance, functional safety, and engineering delivery.
  • Arm China WTCC 2026 seminar (10-23 event / 10-27 report): Arm China held a small embodied-intelligence solutions seminar during the Shanghai West Bund International Tech Carnival, with Rockchip, Radxa Computer, and RT-Thread attending as Open Source AIOS Alliance partners. RT-Thread senior AI engineer Zheng Wei presented three types of agent solutions — robot dogs, HyperCar, and Codex orchestration — discussing how agents move from conversation to execution and explicitly naming embodied-intelligence projects as the focus going forward; Rockchip noted that LLM inference is shifting from compute-intensive to memory-intensive, with bandwidth becoming the edge bottleneck. Arm China also released two deployment results, JishuFriends and JishuBuddy, at the same event.

Community Projects and Competitions

Date: 2026-10-24 ~ 2026-10-30　Source: Community article API

  • 2026 Embedded Competition entries enter submission peak: At least seven entries were published in concentrated fashion within this week’s window — 10-24 to 10-25: edge-side Agent multi-sensor fusion monitoring, ART-Pi2 USB enumeration diagnostic tool, OpenMV protocol pedestrian tracking; 10-28: NOVOSENSE NS800RT7P65 industrial CAN mobile robot, XuanTie D121 multi-slave monitoring, NXP MCXA346 neural-network-enhanced FOC; 10-30: Infineon Edgi Talk “Little Watcher”. What they share is that all include open-source repositories or complete reproduction records, publishing engineering details together with code rather than just submitting a report.
  • GDR debugging tool (10-25): A rare community long-form post that treats debugging scaffolding as a formal engineering project. The tool solidifies RT-Thread kernel object inspection into GDB Python commands, offering three layers of capability: aggregate commands (threads/timers/system/semaphores/objects, with the thread table carrying peak watermarks based on stack fill patterns), convenience functions, and pretty-printers; its essential difference from FinSH is that it is completely non-intrusive, reading symbols and target memory only through GDB, making it suitable for Nano scenarios without a shell and for post-mortem analysis after a HardFault. CI uses QEMU to boot real firmware for pytest assertions, with a matrix covering Cortex-A9 (3.1.0 to 3.1.5, 4.x) and RV64 (4.0.4 to 4.1.1); the author notes it has not yet been verified on real boards.
  • Three long-form posts on the kernel and toolchain: The 10-26 “rtthread startup path analysis” splits Reset_Handler to main into three stages — assembly, C initialization, and scheduler startup — and points out the common misconception that SystemInit does not configure the main frequency to 72MHz; the 10-29 long-form post on kernel object distribution uses 4.0.3’s object.c and rtdef.h to explain how “manage by type, look up by name, traverse, and count” maps onto three things: rt_object_information, intrusive linked lists, and container_of; the two posts on Env v2.0.3 and the 24 implicit declaration warnings in Studio are toolchain pitfall records.
  • Livestream: 10-26 20:00 to 21:00, community developer Cui Junshuo shares a hand rehabilitation exoskeleton project for stroke survivors, post-hand-injury surgery patients, and people with peripheral nerve injury; highlights are the recognition pipeline from EMG signals to movement intention and how the two cores on a single development board divide the work.

Industrial Control and Industrial Connectivity

Date: 2026-10-28 ~ 2026-10-29　Source: Community article: Small autonomous mobile robot

  • Industrial CAN distributed mobile robot: The main controller is a NOVOSENSE NS800RT7P65 (Cortex-M7, 600MHz) running RT-Thread 4.x, with a single chip handling the FOC 2-axis gimbal, CAN master control, and 40 MSH online diagnostic commands; the chassis uses a five-node CAN architecture (main controller, gimbal FOC, sensor acquisition, chassis drive, vision AI), with each slave running its own local closed loop.   • DAMO XuanTie D121 master with Loongson LS2K0300 slave: The master runs RT-Thread plus an LVGL touchscreen and polls two slaves over RS485 with Modbus RTU — a Spark STM32F407 collecting temperature, humidity, and six-axis data, and a Loongson LS2K0300 reporting system metrics such as CPU, memory, and uptime. Collected data is written to a TF card with local playback support; communication interruptions, recoveries, and vibration limit violations go into a persistent queue, and after uploading to the cloud via ESP8266 the data can be viewed remotely through a WeChat mini program — stored locally during outages and backfilled after recovery. Putting both ARM and LoongArch slaves on the same Modbus bus offers a direct reference for integrating heterogeneous devices.
  • AI-based BLDC motor defect monitoring: On the Renesas Titan Board (RA8P1, 1GHz Cortex-M85 plus 250MHz Cortex-M33, with an Ethos-U55 NPU and 256 GOPS on a single chip), three-phase current, bus voltage, and inverter temperature are collected; the M85 core preprocesses the data and sends it to a lightweight CNN on the NPU for real-time defect recognition. On the software side, RT-Thread runs three threads for UI, communication, and inference, and the model is converted from TFLite to C code via RUHMI for integration. The approach emphasizes no additional sensors and a low-cost predictive maintenance path.

Motor Control and Edge AI

Date: 2026-10-27 ~ 2026-10-30　Source: Community article: Lightweight neural network enhanced brushless motor controller

  • Neural network enhanced FOC on NXP MCXA346: The hardware is an FRDM-MCXA346 with a 2804 motor, an AS5600 magnetic encoder, and a SimpleFOC Mini driver. On the control side, a feedforward-plus-PI speed loop and position loop are implemented first, then a dynamic compensation network with 3 inputs, 8 hidden units, and 1 output is superimposed on the output; the weights are exported as C code and computed directly on the MCU, the firmware introduces no inference framework, and the compensation can be toggled independently for same-parameter comparisons. 90 real-board tests (10 switching patterns within ±15 RPM, three rounds each) show the steady-state tail-segment 100 ms window RMSE dropping from 2.187 to 2.094 RPM (about 4.26%), and the full-segment RMSE from 3.327 to 3.291 (about 1.07%); after adding 25 ms smoothing to the P-term target, the mean full-segment speed error across 60 tests drops by about 5.83%.
  • K230 kitchen flame detection: Built on K230 and RT-Thread Smart with open-source code, including multi-sensor fusion, adaptive linkage strategies, reproduction records, and model evaluation; the repository also includes reference materials so third parties can rerun it per the records. The project describes itself as a research prototype rather than a product. This is one of the few public projects this week that puts Smart into a real perception closed loop.
  • CH32H417 handwritten symbol recognition: A WCH dual-core RISC-V platform with a stack of RT-Thread plus LVGL plus TFLM; the pipeline includes OV5640 capture, fixed-point image preprocessing, INT8 quantized inference, and UI display, with a separate toolchain on the training side. The project is newly created and has no traction yet; its value lies in laying out a complete lightweight vision pipeline on RISC-V clearly.
  • Infineon Edgi Talk “Little Watcher”: A single Chinese sentence specifies the appearance or disappearance of a visible target; when a change is detected it alerts and saves the before/after frames. The board’s M55 side handles voice recording and upload, camera capture and upload, and alert playback, while visual judgment runs on a ready-made model on the PC side; a single session is capped at 300 seconds and 60 image uploads, the web page keeps the latest 100 events, and restarts default to paused. The highlight is treating the “AI-assisted development” process itself as a deliverable — requirement breakdown, code integration, troubleshooting, test verification, and documentation all record how AI participated.

VI. Trend Observations

  • Smart’s positioning was clarified this week as a “test coverage layer” rather than a “default configuration”: On 10-29, Smart was first enabled and then reverted from the default configuration within a single day. This may look like flip-flopping, but in practice the maintainers were drawing a boundary: the default configuration must remain predictable so that regular regression runs without Smart stay stable; Smart runs separately through utest’s configuration overlay layer, with both paths sharing a single BSP. This achieves user-space test coverage on QEMU without disrupting the existing BSP build matrix. The follow-up observation points are #11892 and #11897: merge first what can be verified on QEMU, and merge later what depends on confirmation from physical boards. This scheduling pattern was corroborated twice this week.
  • The RT-Smart user-space/kernel-space boundary became the heaviest security theme this week, and has already become a family of issues: The terminal, block device, pipe, and mailbox cases are completely identical in form—the adaptation layer’s default parameters are already kernel addresses, and the user-space entry points lack copyin/copyout. The affected versions of all four tickets trace back to the same RT-Smart synchronization commit in 2022, indicating that this is not a newly introduced defect but a structural gap brought in wholesale during porting. The remediation path is also clear (#11941’s kernel-stack staging plus copy_from_user/copy_to_user plus lwp_in_user_space() boundary checks). The next thing to watch is whether this approach can be abstracted into a common layer, rather than being fixed separately in each subsystem.
  • The mainline cadence was dominated by “batch clearing of the proposal backlog” rather than same-day new development: Among the merges on 10-27 (four) and 10-29 (seven), most proposals had author timestamps more than a week earlier than their merge times; the 10-29 batch was created on 10-18 and queued for eleven days. This pattern affects judgments of project health: looking only at commit counts overestimates the week’s development intensity, while looking only at zero-commit days underestimates review activity. The truly stable metric is “proposal queue turnaround time”—this week it narrowed from ten days to one (#11942 followed on the same day after #11896 merged), which is a positive change.
  • Test credibility was treated as infrastructure to fix, not as tests to fix: The three things fixed this week were not “adding more cases” but making the verdicts themselves trustworthy—subsequent unit passes no longer erase earlier testcase failures (#11601), CI no longer depends on the public network or invalid IP strings (#11601), and under musl it is no longer assumed that signal 0 can be installed (#11601). In addition, #11942 changed CI’s configuration override method from “checking out the whole thing” to “appending a line to turn off the switch.” This belongs to the same line as last week’s pkgs-test concurrency-lock fix: first make the premise of “tests pass” solid, then talk about coverage.
  • Version releases shifted from “waiting” to “institutionalized,” but the release action has still not been unlocked: The v5.4.0 version number was bumped on 10-17, raised again to 0x50400 on 10-29, while releases and tags remain stuck at v5.3.0 from 09-10, already seven weeks. Two materials that appeared on 10-30 (#11956’s version management CI and release process, #11955’s version and release process documentation) show that the community itself is addressing this gap—the Z line has monthly reports plus manual approval, the Y line combines hardware verification every 3 to 6 months, and “QEMU conclusions are not expanded into physical-machine conclusions” is written into the non-goals. The next issue should verify whether the release gate has actually opened based on the release page and ChangeLog, rather than based on the version macro.
  • The shape of vendor adaptation is changing: on one side, toolchains and emulation come first; on the other, a large category of hardware enters in batches: TriCore entered the Env SDK rather than the main repository, first using open-source GCC plus QEMU to solve “can compile, can run, can debug,” with BSP and drivers left for later; meanwhile, three large proposals—RTL8125, RK3588, and RT1180—are under review at the same time, indicating that the focus of adaptation is shifting from “lighting up a single development board” to “entire classes of platforms and peripheral stacks.” The review costs of the two paths differ greatly: the former is a low-risk increment at the package-index level, while the latter often runs to thousands of lines and requires board-side confirmation—which is also why the BSP queue always has proposals on the order of 172 files and 290,000 lines pending.
