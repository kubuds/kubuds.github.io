I. Weekly Highlights

  • Next RISC-V major refactor merged into mainline, kernel version bumped to v5.4.0 (10-17): The RISC-V architecture refactor proposal (#10320, author GuEe-GUI) tracked for nearly five months was merged into master by maintainer Rbb666 on 10-17 at 09:44 — 4 commits, 165 files, +15134/-2617. One minute before the merge (09:43), another commit (#11875) had already bumped the kernel version to v5.4.0 (version macro 5.4.0). The official Release and tag have not yet landed; the releases page still shows v5.3.0 from 09-10, and the latest ChangeLog chapter also remains v5.3.0. This item occurred after the Friday daily cutoff (10-16 08:00) and is a release-day verification addendum.

  • 15 merges this week, 8 landed within 64 minutes on the morning of 10-14 (10-12 ~ 10-16): 2 on Monday (#11863, #11787), zero on Tuesday (master silent for about 46 hours), 8 on Wednesday (09:13 to 10:17), 4 on Thursday, 1 on Friday (#11846, after the daily cutoff). Merge topics cluster into three groups — network stack robustness, memory and bounds safety, and vendor BSPs — reflecting a batch-closure pattern by maintainers.

  • Two defects exposed in the 9PFS filesystem, one rated CVSS 3.1 7.5 (10-15): New defect ticket #11868 points out that the 64-bit load macro on the receive path dereferences the buffer via a plain pointer (struct size 292 and protocol constant 42 make rx_buffer+42 always 6 mod 8); under ARMv7-A, GCC-generated LDRD requires 8-byte alignment, so any valid Tstat reply triggers a data abort in dfs_9pfs_open and dfs_9pfs_stat. The report traces back to the commit that introduced 9PFS in 2025-12, gives a remote denial-of-service rating with a full reproduction chain on QEMU vexpress-a9, and includes disassembly and addr2line evidence. The other ticket, #11289 (out-of-bounds read on the getdents path), opened in March, was confirmed by the community reproducing it on master (bsp/simulator with ASan). Both remain open.

  • Network stack robustness closed out: two ping cases and lwip macro control settled in succession (10-14 ~ 10-15): #11853 fixes a shell crash caused by division by zero in ping statistics when the target is unreachable; defect ticket #11852 was closed the same day. #11866 corrects the last-packet delay logic (the original loop condition was always false, so a delay was still added after the final packet, and the delay interface was called with a 0 argument); #11865 was closed the same day. An earlier proposal in the same direction, #11862, was closed without merging. The lwip section content macro control in #11815 was merged on 10-15 at 13:28 after several rounds of expansion — 6 files, +188/-28.

  • Memory and bounds hardening became a parallel mainline (10-12 ~ 10-15): Kernel calloc integer overflow fix (#11848), legacy USB host interface count upper bound and enumeration cleanup (#11822, defect ticket #11288 closed), two out-of-bounds fixes in the device tree ofw phandle hash table (#11845), and DHCP server option bounds checking (#11858, carried over from the start of the period) were merged in succession; the heap memory AddressSanitizer proposal (#11772) added range checks for rt_memcpy, rt_memset, and rt_memmove plus boundary test cases, with the latest CI reported all passing.

  • High-density interaction in the review queue, new contributors take on multiple threads (10-13 ~ 10-16): The ESP32-C6 SPI proposal (#11846) received three line-level comments from the maintainer (single-transfer 64-byte limit after disabling DMA requires segmentation, C3 and C6 sharing IDF causes build overwrites, bit-width validation needs to be moved earlier); the author addressed each and it was merged on 10-16 at 11:31. The efm32 menuconfig newcomer task (#7530) was claimed by a contributor who opened a proposal (#11867, 2 files, +408) one minute later. A disambiguation proposal (#11869) was opened for the DFS layer where F_GETFL and F_SETFL collide with device control command codes. The aarch64 GIC acknowledge-path discussion provided hundreds of thousands of inference-level measured data points.

  • Ecosystem and toolchain: hpm_sdk v1.13.0 completes the “tag, index, BSP” loop (10-13 ~ 10-17): The upstream package repository released the v1.13.0 tag on 10-13 (adding the HPM5100 series); the packages index proposal #2045 was merged on 10-14 at 18:41, the first merge in the index repository in half a month; on the main repository BSP side, 10-17 upgraded to v1.13.0 and added hpm5100evk support (#11871). In the same period, the Env tool merged PR #302 on 10-17 (built-in documentation navigation in WebUI) and bumped the version to v2.0.3; the releases page still shows v2.0.2 from 09-16 as the latest.

  • Community content holds at two posts per day, with dual tracks of competition and teaching output (10-12 ~ 10-15): 5 posts this week on 2026 embedded competition projects (portable debugger, Muyu Xinsheng, GD32H759IM USB secondary display, environmental monitoring logger, Shuheng Zhiyang physiotherapy robotic arm), all using GD32H7 series MCUs; the CIIF AI car Workshop retrospective (10-15) and the RK3588 virtualization hybrid deployment teaching platform (10-13) were published in succession; the official news feed has remained silent through its fifty-seventh day.

Review Queue Observations

Date: 2026-10-13 ~ 2026-10-16　Source: PR #10320

  • RISC-V is the most active area: #10320 went through multiple rounds of iteration all week — on 10-13 a test report on five QEMU configurations was submitted (the S-Mode device-model-disabled combination failed to link due to missing common startup and multicore scheduling symbols); maintainers set the adaptation rule of “choose either device model or no device model”; that evening the contributor verified two companion patches on ESP32-C3 hardware, with both C3 and C6 compiling cleanly with zero warnings; on 10-15 at 11:51 a libcpu-level refactoring commit (6bf3686) was pushed, and tracking ticket #11246 was refreshed in sync.
  • Technical scope of the refactoring commit: introduces an optional unified RISC-V common layer (RV32 and RV64), using ARCH_RISCV and ARCH_CPU_64BIT for architecture and bit-width selection, unifying context, trap, MMU, cache, timer, SBI, SMP, and ISA handling, adding PLIC, CLIC, ACLINT, APLIC, and IMSIC support, establishing a mapping table from physical HART ID to logical CPU ID while retaining T-Head vendor override branches; qemu-virt64-riscv becomes the first BSP to adopt the new common layer (the same directory supports both RV32 and RV64 S-mode), while existing BSPs that do not select the RT_USING_RISCV_NEW_COMMON macro continue to use the legacy_common and common64 paths.
  • Networking and memory: #11815 expanded from macro control to four submission groups — DNS cleanup, DHCP startup, buffer options, and WLAN lifecycle — before merging; #11772’s ASan proposal adds libc interface wrappers and boundary test cases on top of existing heap instrumentation and QEMU CI coverage.
  • Board-level and peripherals: #11844 (ESP32-S3 board support) completed automated review remediation (mutex macro expansion, UART0 interrupt allocation level); the remaining build failure was judged to be a pre-existing upstream issue, and manual re-review has been requested; #11860 (aarch64 GIC) saw in-depth discussion on removing the redundant GICD_ICPENDR write before EOI, with the author providing measurements showing that keeping it causes a completion interrupt loss in roughly 19 to 33 inferences, while removing it yields zero failures across 480,000 inferences.
  • Tooling and conventions: the author of #11743 (automatic Keil and IAR target selection) responded to two re-review comments; #11859 (clang-format rule consolidation) added two more commits and demonstrated the actual effect using thread.c; #11812 was reopened by a maintainer and then merged; #11869 (DFS code collision disambiguation) was newly opened, distinguishing commands by parameter form and synchronously reworking both dfs_v1 and dfs_v2 implementations.
  • Beginner tasks activated: the menuconfig task in #7530 was claimed by a contributor for the efm32 subtask (adding Kconfig and .config), and #11867 was opened just 1 minute after the claim.

V. Industry Applications and Business Trends

Device-Edge Collaboration and Elderly-Care Robotics

Dates: 2026-10-12, 10-15　Source: Device-Edge Collaborative Dexterous Robotic Arm Tech Talk

  • Device-Edge Collaborative Dexterous Robotic Arm (10-12): Open-sourced project that won an RT-Thread AIoT Competition award — using Titan Board (Renesas RA8P1, Cortex-M85 plus Ethos-U55 NPU) as the sole control core, a 17-class gesture recognition model is quantized to INT8 and compiled via RUHMI and Vela into a single all-NPU subgraph (all 107 operators mapped to Ethos-U55), with a single inference taking about 77 to 78 ms. The author shares engineering lessons on staged startup of D-cache and OSPI clocks, merging WiFi and NPU projects, and low-bandwidth streaming with TinyJPEG.

The cloud side runs SpringBoot services with intelligent consultation, and safety mechanisms include angle limits, parameter validation, state machine management and emergency stop.

VI. Trend Observations

  • RISC-V completes the leap from proposal to mainline: #10320, under review for five months, was merged as an optional common layer, with qemu-virt64-riscv onboarded first and existing BSPs retaining the legacy path — migration will proceed platform by platform, with no risk of a full switch in the short term, but the RISC-V architecture stack now enters a period of parallel old and new tracks. Version numbers landed ahead of releases (both v5.4.0 and Env v2.0.3 were only bumped, not released); the next issue should verify official releases against the release page and ChangeLog.
  • Merges arrive in batch windows: 8 of this week’s 15 merges landed within 64 minutes on 10-14, following roughly 46 hours of silence — maintainers are draining the review queue in windows, so single-day merge counts are no longer a suitable sole indicator of activity; interaction density on pending proposals should also be considered.
  • Security audit focus shifts to new components: 9PFS, introduced in 2025-12, exposed a remote denial of service and an out-of-bounds read for the first time, alongside the ASan proposal and boundary fixes in calloc, USB host, ofw and DHCP — memory and protocol-parsing security became the week’s most concentrated technical theme.
  • Contributor pipeline expands and response chains shorten: the ping divide-by-zero defect took about 9 days from report to merge, and a second case was claimed by a new contributor the same day; a menuconfig newcomer task was claimed and a proposal produced within 1 minute after months of inactivity; the 9PFS audit was completed independently by a community researcher with disassembly evidence — review burden is shifting from a single maintainer to community division of labor.
  • Domestic MCU ecosystem density continues to rise: all 5 competition projects this week are based on GD32H7; N32 timer completion and FEMC fixes, three XHSC boards entering Studio, and hpm_sdk v1.13.0’s index and BSP landing in both directions — domestic platforms have moved from “it runs” to competition over toolchain completeness.
  • Official channels silent for the 57th day; community channels are the only external window: the news API has been dormant since 06-17, with external information carried by the forum, competition projects and industry media; monthly reports and CIIF recaps still use third-party media as their publishing vehicle, and the absence of official first-party channels continues to lengthen.
