RuyiAI Weekly Report (2026-09-30 ~ 2026-10-07)

2026-10-07 Kubuds

Reporting period: September 30, 2026 (Wednesday) to October 7, 2026 (Wednesday), 8 days total; special National Day holiday summary period (October 1–7 is the holiday, daily reports paused) Sources: This publication’s daily RuyiAI activity reports (one issue on 2026-09-30, window 09-29 04:30 ~ 09-30 04:30; no daily report material during the 10-01 ~ 10-07 holiday); also includes a 216-hour window audit of organizational repositories, dual-source community verification, and a full-period scan of the technical intelligence repository (coverage through the morning of 10-08) Editorial note: This period spans the National Day holiday—code merges entered a low-output mode during the holiday, while the review queue, intelligence repository, and community channels kept operating; all technical facts come from hands-on verification of code repositories, releases, and community forums

1. Weekly Highlights

  • openRuyi 2026.09 officially released: RVA23 refinements, expanded cloud and storage software stack, repo GPG verification enabled (09-30): The release announcement went live on the first day of this cycle (not yet visible within the 09-30 daily window; a newly discovered item in this cycle’s review). Key points: GCC 16 backports a BCLR codegen fix—eliminating the DPDK rte_softrss() hang and Mesa ACO scheduler miscompilation; DPDK 25.07 enables RVV lookup paths on RVA23 (lpm / fib / acl / node); OpenJDK merges 15 RISC-V patches (Zbs / Zfa instruction generation, RVV vset optimization, CRC32C intrinsic); on the container side, after allowing riscv_hwprobe, testing on SpacemiT V100 shows OpenSSL enabling Zvkned / Zvknhb vector crypto extensions and zlib-ng enabling Zbc-accelerated crc32; kernels 7.2.6 / 6.18.54; newly added Cloudpods 4.0.3 multi-cloud platform, SPDK 25.09 (io_uring / RBD bdev / SMA), and Ceph 21.3.0 snapshot; the installation pipeline enables GPG signature verification by default; security updates land in batch (expat / jq / vim / pcre2 / runc, etc.); four editions offered—Server / Container / Desktop / Zero—plus an experimental RVA20 edition. Source: openRuyi 2026.09 release

  • “PyTorch RISC-V CI in Practice” blog post published: Ruyi community, together with XuanTie and RISE, discloses a real-machine CI system (live in the early hours of 10-08): The post reveals the full picture of PyTorch RISC-V continuous integration—co-built by RuyiAI (Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences), the XuanTie team, and the RISE AI/ML working group: it adopts a Debian Trixie / Python 3.13 / GCC 14 / OpenBLAS 0.3.33 baseline, “build once, test on multiple machines”; Jenkins parallel sharding (core tests about 3 hours, full suite about 5 hours); dynamic sharding based on historical durations; a unified blocklist managed through manual classification; failure logs centrally archived (community-ci.openruyi.cn); SyncBots continuously syncs upstream main into the RISC-V working branch and automatically triggers core tests—the RISC-V core test suite currently passes consistently and stably. The work originated from the XuanTie team’s RFC (pytorch#171659) and tracking issue (#180975); the Institute of Software is a RISE member, and a RuyiAI member serves as co-chair of the RISE AI/ML WG. Source: blog post (Chinese-English bilingual), RFC #171659, tracking #180975

  • PyTorch fork ends its silence: riscv branch resumes daily sync and completes a full rebuild in the early hours of 10-08 (09-30 ~ 10-08): The riscv branch had been silent since 09-21, and maintainer pushes resumed on the morning of 09-30—thereafter roughly once per day (7 times from 09-30 to 10-07); in the early hours of 10-08 (Beijing time) a full rebuild was completed—the local patch stack was replayed in order (23 patches starting from #1 RISC-V Blocklist, including #44 bytes_to_scalar fix, #50 inductor blocklist, #42 ULP tolerance, etc.), and the SyncBots integration commit advanced the upstream sync point to e188cec; the baselines of 8 open PRs (#23 / #35 / #36 / #38 / #43 / #46 / #48 / #51) were uniformly refreshed—the two RVV PRs (#46 ATen RVV support, #48 RVV CPU capability) and the CI local image (#51) are among them. Source: riscv branch commits, PR list

  • llvm-project riscv branch: three SpacemiT A100-series patches merged (10-03); nightly rebuild cadence continues through the holiday (10-01 ~ 10-07): #30 lowers IME intrinsics to A100 dot-product instructions, #32 adds A100 pack/unpack intrinsic lowering and scheduling, and #31 adds A100 HP/SP and pack/unpack lowering and calibrates the scheduling model—the three were merged in succession on 10-03; the branch maintains nightly rebuilds (in the early hours of Beijing time), with the last rebuild of the window headed at 03e4e494. The standard AME pre-approved backend PR #22 (opened in July, 7 discussions) was closed on 10-02 (not merged); the two FPGA PRs (#25 / #28) remain under review and received baseline refreshes. Source: PR #30, PR #31, PR #32, PR #22

  • Two riscv-gcc P-extension PRs merged (10-02); Ztt draft toolchain workstream opens (from 09-30): #20 (abs / pabs series changed to header-file operator lowering with test fixes) and #21 (removing an erroneous same-channel PAS/PSA combine pattern) were merged on 10-02 (author TelGome); during the same period, a new ztt-dev branch was opened in riscv-gcc and riscv-binutils to add register operands, instruction encoding, and CSR support for the Ztt draft extension, with continuous commits through the holiday (including codegen optimizations, latest through the morning of 10-08); riscv-gnu-toolchain added an ame-experimental branch. Source: PR #20, PR #21, ztt-dev branch

  • Matrix extension workstream in place: AME-operator repo open-sourced, official AME spec repo fork lands (09-30): The ruyisdk organization created the AME-operator repo—RISC-V AME/Ztt GEMM and GEMV kernels, an independent numerical reference, and a DejaGNU/Spike verification suite (covering INT8 / UINT8 / INT32 / FP32 / BF16, T=4~128; smoke tests ran 16 ELFs and 32 executions, all passing); on the same day it forked the official RISC-V attached-matrix-extension (the AME task group’s management repo). With this, the matrix direction forms a three-line layout: “LLVM (BOSC AME / A100) - GCC/Binutils (Ztt) - operators and verification (AME-operator)”. Source: AME-operator repo, attached-matrix-extension fork

Source: ruyiai-daily, forum #2885

  • triton-riscv: Nightly 2026-10-04 build released; softmax and matmul optimization PR opened (10-05, 10-08): Nightly builds maintain a weekly cadence—Nightly 2026-10-04-12c2f6a was released on 10-05 (x86_64 and riscv64 wheels covering CPython 3.12 to 3.14); on the morning of 10-08, PR #85 “softmax and matmul optimization” was newly opened (community contributor). Source: Nightly 2026-10-04, PR #85

VI. Trend Observations

  • Divergence in holiday mode: “low merge output, but queues and intelligence never stop”: Across the three organizations, main-repo merges over the 8 days were few and far between (two for riscv-gcc, three for llvm-project, zero for openRuyi), yet PR reviews, branch refreshes, intelligence repos, and community channels ran throughout; in the early hours of the first post-holiday day (10-08), a full PyTorch rebuild and a new triton PR appeared — a recovery speed worth noting.
  • Systematic build-out of matrix extensions: LLVM-side A100 series merges, GCC/Binutils-side Ztt draft branch, operator-side AME-operator open-sourcing, and a fork of the official AME repo on the spec side — the matrix direction has shifted from scattered patches to parallel progress across “compiler–operator–spec–verification,” the most technically significant structural change this issue.
  • PyTorch RISC-V moving from “can compile” to “continuously verified”: the CI blog post, fork rebuilds, and SyncBots automated syncing together form a closed loop of “upstream tracking, on-hardware regression, issue feedback”; next, watch the review of the two RVV PRs (#46 / #48) and the onboarding of more hardware backends.
  • openRuyi’s “enterprise-grade software stack” narrative: the 2026.09 release brings the Cloudpods cloud platform, Ceph / SPDK storage, and GPG supply-chain signing into the mainline, layered with RVA23 fixes and container performance paths — the distribution’s target scenario is shifting from “it runs” to “production-ready.”
  • Continued instruction-level correctness fixes on the compiler side: BCLR codegen fix (backported to openRuyi GCC 16), P extension lowering, A100 and Ztt — the toolchain is gaining on both correctness of existing extensions and support for new ones.
  • Gaps and watch items: publication of Biweekly Report No. 078; the direction of the standard AME route after llvm-project #25 / #28 (FPGA) and the closure of #22; recovery of openRuyi main-repo merges and the 2026.10 release cadence; post-holiday continuity of ruyiai-daily; review of triton-riscv #85; merge cadence of support-matrix #409 and board-docs #50.
