RuyiAI Weekly Report (2026-11-02 ~ 2026-11-06)

2026-11-06 Kubuds

Reporting period: Monday, November 2, 2026 through Friday, November 6, 2026 — 5 working days.

Sources: 5 issues of this publication’s daily RuyiAI update reports (2026-11-02 through 2026-11-06; each issue’s window runs from 04:30 the previous day to 04:30 of the issue date, with Monday covering the preceding 3 days including the weekend); plus a 168-hour repository audit of three organizations — RuyiAI-Stack, ruyisdk, and buddy-compiler — a full paginated commit-by-commit review of the main openRuyi-Project repository, dual-source verification against the RuyiSDK developer community and the openRuyi distribution news page, and three-page snapshots of the official RuyiAI homepage (verification coverage extends to the afternoon of 11-07).

Editorial note: This week saw the most concentrated structural changes of the quarter, with three main threads converging at once. First, the last mile of the distribution pipeline was opened up — openRuyi #1350, stalled for 43 days, was merged on 11-03, bringing the buddy-mlir, triton, and tilelang trio into the distribution as RPM specs for the first time. Second, triton-riscv spent a single day transforming itself from “maintaining a patched Triton fork” into “a plugin for upstream Triton,” netting over two thousand lines deleted and the patch directory disappearing entirely. Third, the AME/BOSCAME matrix backend landed two commits within the week: on the LLVM side, register classification and tile scheduling were added; on the buddy-mlir side, a single commit of 209 files and over forty thousand lines rolled out the Kernel Contract, SPM/DMA planner, runtime, and FPGA tooling together. The release side tells the other half of the story: ruyi shipped a beta after a 39-day gap, while the stable release and the openRuyi 2026.10 monthly release had still not landed by the end of this week. Beyond the daily-report material, this weekly adds five new verification findings (mostly after the daily-report windows or at period boundaries), listed in the appendix.

llvm-project riscv branch: one BOSCAME commit after two replays (11-01 to 11-04)

Date: 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-04　Sources: riscv branch commit list, commit f346011, PR #35

On the eve of the period start (11-01 23:00) and the night of the first day (11-02 23:00), the branch underwent a full replay each time: the former rewrote the commit timestamps of twenty-six branch-owned commits to a uniform 23:00:22 to 23:00:26, with head commit afdd0982 (content: the 10-29 “cancel preview, add contribution guide”); the latter replayed again, swapping the head commit to 375bf3e4 with unchanged content. At 11-03 11:57, bef335e2 “[workflows] Use workflow-scoped token for PR rebases” landed, switching the rebase workflow token from repository-scoped to workflow-scoped—automation maintenance.

The 11-04 15:40 f346011 is the week’s only genuine compiler change (8 files, +412/−1, author caihanyi111). It has two parts. First, register classification: loop-carried accumulator PHIs take their default register class from the type table, but nxv32i32 was previously registered only as TileReg, while the accumulator operands and results of mlce32.m, mqma.*, and msce32.m are restricted to AccReg—so every loop edge had to generate a copy between the tile and accumulator register files, and copyPhysReg cannot expand such cross-file copies into a single AME instruction. The fix registers nxv32i32 as AccReg as well, expands same-file whole-register copies using mmve64.t.t and mmve64.a.a, and keeps cross-file copies as a hard error. Second, scheduling: each tile configuration and its consumers are held in an ordered, side-effecting pseudo-instruction until just before emit, matrix registers become allocatable, MMA accumulators are tied, and zero-accumulator definitions no longer read old values. The commit also adds three tests covering configurable load/MMA/store ordering, same-file and cross-file copies, and both RVV and non-RVV selection.

One inconsistency remains unexplained: the author date of the branch’s most recent [SyncBots] Integrate LLVM at b3b5e33 is stuck at 09-29, but comparing the current head commit against the previous period’s head commit, the diff set includes the 10-30 upstream commit—that is, the replay advanced the upstream baseline while rewriting history, yet the sync bot’s record was not updated accordingly.

triton-riscv: from “patched branch” to “upstream plugin” in one day (11-05)

Date: 2026-11-05　Sources: PR #98, commit list, PR #92

The overhaul has three parts. First, backend form: Triton’s TRITON_PLUGIN_DIRS mechanism installs backend/ as a triton_shared backend plugin, registers the entry point, links the C++ plugin into libtriton.so, and copies triton-shared-opt into the package; the standalone distribution package disappears accordingly, imports in the code are unified to go through triton.backends.triton_shared, and the backend itself now locates buddy-opt, buddy-llc, mir-translate, and triton-shared-opt via paths.py, with the CPU driver declaring itself active only when no GPU backend is present.

Second, the patch set is zeroed out. The patches/ directory and the scripts that applied patches are all deleted. The patch set previously existed for two reasons: to let Triton build in environments without NVIDIA/AMD toolchains, and to cushion frequent LLVM API changes. Now that buddy-mlir’s LLVM distribution ships NVPTX/AMDGPU targets and lld (#1027), and Triton’s GPU dependency path points to a stub directory, neither reason holds.

Third, the packaging paths are merged. release.sh no longer assembles a second wheel—it just strips tools and runs bdist_wheel directly; Makefile becomes the unified entry point for setup, build, test, lit, and clean. A Python 3.14 pitfall was also fixed: in setup.py, shutil.which("lit") preferentially picks up the pip-installed lit console script, and the one bundled with the LLVM distribution calls main() without an if __name__ == "__main__" guard, so the forkserver multiprocessing re-runs it when importing the main module, causing the lit target to fail at 80-way concurrency.

The cost is written in the same PR’s description: Triton 3.9’s frontend reads fields such as backend_name from the backend, and without it every tl.atomic_* call throws AttributeError, failing 383 FlagGems cases on riscv64; only after adding the three fields backend_name, default_dot_input_precision, and deprecated_fp8_dot_operand_dtypes did the failure surface close. The examples side also made concessions: upstream rejected the frontend patches for asin/acos/atan/atan2, so the examples switched to libdevice, which lowers to the same __nv_* extern_elementwise and is then converted by triton-shared into MLIR math operators.

Three triton-riscv companions: nightly and weekend releases, 16-process pytest, cp312-abi3 wheel (11-05 to 11-06)

Date: 2026-11-05 to 2026-11-06　Sources: PR #99, PR #100, PR #101, Release list

#99 aligns the release cadence with buddy-mlir: the nightly job publishes nightly/<date>-<sha> prereleases, and the weekend job publishes release/<date>-<sha> marked as latest; runs are skipped when there are no new commits, releases stay in draft until all wheels are uploaded, and workflow_dispatch offers an out-of-band dev/patch choice. Two nightly builds were produced that day under this cadence.

On the test side, the pytest suite was switched to 16 processes, with measured justification: on a 207-case slice, 1 process took 360 s, 8 processes 89 s, 16 processes 67 s, and 24 processes 67 s, while 32 and 64 were actually slower—a single case is a serial chain of “compile, triton-shared-opt, llc, execute, compare,” and beyond a point more processes contend for the shared compilation cache. Previously, running serially, the FlagGems suite took 1 hour 14 minutes.

#100 builds a stable ABI wheel following upstream practice: setting TRITON_STABLE_ABI=ON, compiling nanobind against the limited API (macro Py_LIMITED_API set to 0x030C0000), tagging the wheel cp312-abi3, so one wheel covers 3.12 through 3.15 and the release matrix shrinks from three Pythons to one. The same branch also fixes release.sh’s manylinux toolchain detection: the script hardcoded /opt/rh/gcc-toolset-14/enable and checked for it before dnf install clang, while the image is a rolling :latest that by then shipped gcc-toolset-15, so the enable script never ran and clang could not find crtbeginS.o, -lstdc++, -lgcc (riscv64), or libstdc++’s <version> header (x64), failing builds on both architectures; now clang is installed first, then all toolchains are enabled by existence with the newest last. #101 merged at 11-06 12:52 (2 files, +17/−2), handling release workflow failures during the checkout cleanup phase.

ruyi 0.54.0-beta.20261102: only four of the thirteen-item changelog are new, ABI moves from scanning packages to judging hosts (11-02)

Date: 2026-11-02　Source: Release 0.54.0-beta.20261102, PR #507, PR #496, PR #509

Timeline: tagged at 15:16, single-file binaries and mirror directory published at 18:37, PyPI synced 0.54.0b20261102; the stable channel remains 0.53.0 from 09-24. This is the first new tag in the 39 days since 0.53.0 — the previous pre-release, 0.54.0-alpha.20260918, was six days earlier than 0.53.0, and no alpha or beta was released throughout October. The alpha-to-beta gap was 45 days, whereas the alpha→beta intervals for the 0.50 and 0.51 lines were both 28 days and for the 0.53 line 20 days.

The divergence reported by the comparison interface is worth noting: beta is diverged relative to 0.53.0, ahead by seventy commits and behind by three; the three behind are the version-number bumps for 0.53.0-beta and 0.53.0, plus a fix for “cross-repository detection of installed packages” — the same fix exists on beta under a different SHA, so no functionality is missing; it is just that the bookkeeping on the 0.53 release branch was not merged back into mainline.

The layers of ABI capability need to be distinguished: the ELF scanner from #496 was merged as early as 09-16 and shipped with 0.53.0; it can scan directories and tar/zip/deb archives, produce deterministic .abi.toml sidecars, and provides rescan-package-abi. What is new in this release is the consumer side, #507, which uses the sidecars to compare against the current host and answer “can this package run on this machine.” In the same version, #509 fixes failure reporting when fetching packages: non-2xx responses are no longer treated as “download successful” and left as an error page; resumable partial downloads are preserved; and HTTP status, redirects, connection failures, and stalled transfers each get their own message — this one is more directly useful to actual users than the ABI check.

packages-index: eleven ABI sidecars for three packages, coverage still single digits out of a three-digit denominator (11-02)

Date: 2026-11-02　Source: packages-index commits, gnu-ruyisdk riscv64 sidecar

The three commits landed within eighteen minutes before #507 was merged: at 13:09, 0.20260921.0 was added to toolchain/gnu-ruyisdk (with three sidecars); at 13:18, six sidecars across two versions were added to board-util/wlink; at 13:26, two amd64 sidecars were added to emulator/qemu-system-riscv-upstream.

Taking the gnu-ruyisdk riscv64 sidecar as an example, the scanning granularity is visible: of the archive’s 2178 files, 43 ELFs were identified and 5478 excluded; e_machines=[243], with dependencies on five sonames; version requirement ceilings are GLIBC 2.38, GLIBCXX 3.4.21, and CXXABI 1.3.9; ISA attributes are recorded at version-numbered granularity (rv64i2p1, m2p0, a2p1, f2p2, d2p2, c2p0, plus zicsr, zifencei, zmmul), with stack alignment 16. This is enough to support a judgment of “can the host run this toolchain.”

But coverage is a hard constraint: the index contains 327 package version description files in total, yet only 3 packages and 11 files have sidecars, and qemu has only two amd64 sidecars and no riscv64 one. A RISC-V user checking the vast majority of packages today will get “undetermined.” For sidecar coverage to catch up, the build pipeline will have to rescan package by package.

Missing library functions on one end and missing visibility of feature flags on the other are two sides of the same problem.

This Week’s Three VIR Commits: set_vl Exact/Arbitrary VLEN Split, vir.shuffle Segmented Lowering, Kernel Lowering by llc Options (11-04 to 11-05)

Date: 2026-11-04 to 2026-11-05　Source: PR #1028, PR #1029, PR #1030, PR #1031

The tightening of set_vl semantics is covered in “This Week’s Highlights.” One implementation detail to add: #1029 requires that builds without rvv-vlen produce code valid for any VLEN, with strip-mined regions stepping at runtime by N times vscale and vectors sized to the minimum 128 bits; a set_vl with a result is rejected outright with “requires rvv-vlen, i.e., exact VLEN”—previously this pass only reported an error, with semantics relying on “masks happening to be all-true” as a fallback.

The two lowering changes on 11-05 target vir.shuffle and kernel lowering. Broadcast first goes through vector<[2]xT> and then takes the first half (vector.scalable.extract) to obtain vector<[1]xT>, because when vector.broadcast is applied directly to vector<[1]xT>, LLVM generates a shufflevector with mask [0], which createOrFold folds away as an identity operation, leaving the remaining vscale-1 lanes all poison; llc turns the insertelement into a vfmv.s.f that writes only lane 0, and once this value is stored to a register rather than fused into a .vf instruction, the result is wrong. Going through [2]xT lets llc use vfmv.v.f to complete the broadcast at the LMUL of [1]xT, with no increase in instruction count and one fewer instruction in the loop. The other commit lowers kernels according to llc options such as -mattr, with the test vir-broadcast-scalable-one.mlir covering both the strip-mined and set_vl-with-result paths.

VII. Trend Observations

The distribution pipeline is connected, but “packaging complete” does not equal “capability synchronized”

The significance of #1350 lies not in the version number, but in the fact that the distribution path has been written into distro configuration for the first time: previously, to use this stack, you had to pull triton-riscv, tilelang-riscv, and buddy-mlir yourself and set variables like BUDDY_MLIR_BINARY_DIR per the documentation; now openRuyi’s packaging repo contains these three components, lowering the installation barrier from “can build a compiler” to “can install a package.”

But the code anchored by all three specs predates this period’s upstream: triton is stuck at 09-13, tilelang at 10-28, buddy at 0.1.2, while all three repos saw new activity this week. The most glaring case is tilelang—PR #5, merged on 11-03, was the first feature after eighteen days of silence, yet it is not in the RPM merged that same day. The version string 3.8.0+git20261103 reinforces misreading, mistaking the packaging date for the code date. To close this gap, either establish a regular bump process or state the snapshot date clearly in the package description; otherwise, with every feature merged, the gap between package and repo widens a little more.

Plug-in architecture trades patch cost for version binding; 383 failing test cases are the down payment

The benefit of triton-riscv is straightforward: following upstream version bumps no longer requires replaying patches, packaging yields a single wheel, and the installation surface drops from two distribution packages to one. The cost is equally clear, and it surfaced on the same day: Triton 3.9’s frontend reads the backend_name field from the backend, and its absence causes 383 FlagGems test cases to fail. Parasitizing upstream extension points means upstream frontend implicit contracts become mandatory interfaces—and these contracts are often undocumented, discoverable only by hitting them in tests.

Whether this refactor succeeds is not judged by how many lines were deleted today, but by the replay cost at the next upstream version bump, and whether downstream suites like FlagGems still need dedicated version-watching. By the end of this week, the path to following upstream is clear, but the accompanying acceptance pipeline is still not in place: #56 “Migrate additional FlagGems operators” remains open, and the question “can this batch of operators run on riscv64” can still only be answered inside PRs.

Backend becomes the week’s main axis; frontend and runtime stand still

Nearly all verifiable increments this week landed in “compilation and lowering”: LLVM’s AME backend added register classification and tile scheduling, buddy-mlir’s BOSCAME connected contracts to FPGA, VIR tightened the VLEN semantics of set_vl, and tilelang added a lowering path for streaming load. Meanwhile, pytorch’s RISC-V branch was replayed three times with zero feature increments, and none of the hundred-plus upstream syncs on the main side touched RISC-V.

This distribution shows that the focus at this stage is making existing operators lower correctly and run stably on RISC-V, rather than expanding frontend coverage. For downstream users, perceptible short-term changes will appear in compilation artifacts and JDK behavior, not in the list of newly supported frameworks. The corresponding weakness is in the same place: the only triton PR explicitly aimed at optimization, #85, is still stuck at 10-18, with no movement for half a month.

Release cadence: beta is out, but stable and monthly releases remain outside the window

Two monthly commitments both fell through at the end of October, and the first week of November brought half an answer for each: ruyi shipped 0.54.0-beta on 11-02, the first jump after a 39-day gap, with the 0.54 stable release set for around November 11; openRuyi’s 2026.10 monthly announcement still had not appeared as of 11-07, making it the first full month without a release.

Neither line is stalled on the code side—openRuyi’s main repo saw 156 commits this week, and ruyi landed ABI detection and package-fetch fixes—so the bottleneck lies in version freeze, announcement writing, image building, and publishing. This is consistent with last week’s judgment: in this ecosystem, “building things” and “shipping things” are two separate capabilities; the former advances across multiple repos simultaneously, while the latter has not completed on schedule even once in nearly two months.

Long-term benchmark gap makes “is the optimization effective” unanswerable

VecIntrinBench has been dormant for nearly nine months, IntrinTrans for four months, and FBGEMM for three months—all three repos saw zero commits all week. In the same week, glibc and newlib added eighteen vector math functions, LLVM merged six P-ext and Ztt v0.6 patches, and buddy-mlir rolled out a matrix backend across 209 files—capabilities are expanding, but measurement is not keeping up.

The consequences are not abstract: without an updated intrinsic benchmark, one cannot answer “how much has the compiler’s actual supported set grown”; without a standing performance baseline, one cannot answer “how much faster is the BOSCAME path than the old path.” Every performance-related statement this week is at the level of “no obvious regression observed,” and such statements cannot support technology selection.

Desktop packages and AI stack proceed in parallel, on different cadences

openRuyi saw 156 commits in a week, of which 74 were firecracker’s Rust dependency closure and 37 were the LibreOffice dependency chain—this is desktop office suite packaging advanced component by component. On the same day (11-05), the RuyiAI stack was entirely silent except for triton-riscv: pytorch’s RISC-V branch had zero commits, tilelang-riscv zero commits, and llvm-project’s riscv branch zero commits.

The two lines are driven by different people, different repos, and different cadences. For readers, mistaking packaging-side activity for AI-stack progress is a misreading; for the project, this division of labor is fine in itself, but at the weekly-report level they must be recorded separately—otherwise the “commit count” metric will keep giving a false sense of prosperity.
