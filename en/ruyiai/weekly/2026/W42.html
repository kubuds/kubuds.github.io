External developments: RISE’s SpeexDSP RVV optimization and the XuanTie C950 matrix extension narrative

  • RISE SpeexDSP RVV optimization (10-14): Introduced a Meson build, Checkasm tests, and hand-written RVV kernels for SpeexDSP, validated on K1 (VLEN 256) and K230 (VLEN 128) — K1 floating-point resampling reached roughly 3.71x / 3.59x the Clang 18 / GCC 13 baselines respectively. This work pushes RVV optimization into foundational multimedia libraries such as VoIP and audio resampling, and is directly relevant to the SpacemiT platform. Source: RISE blog post
  • DAMO XuanTie C950 interview (10-15): The RISC-V International website published an interview with XuanTie chief scientist Jianyi Meng — the team ran Qwen3.8-27B locally on the C950’s 64-core target configuration, achieving throughput above 30 tokens/s without relying on a discrete GPU or NPU; reaching that throughput requires roughly 400 GB/s-class memory movement, depending on high-bandwidth memory and the team’s self-developed matrix extension validated in silicon; the team subsequently joined with Qualcomm, IBM, VRULL, ISCAS, BOSC, and others to launch the AME (Attached Matrix Extension) task group to drive standardization. Key points: prefill is dominated by dense matrix computation, while decode shifts to a bandwidth bottleneck; separate matrix registers are used (INT4 / INT8 to INT32, FP4 / FP8 / FP16 / BF16 to FP32 mixed-precision accumulation) to avoid competing with RVV registers; the specification still needs to cover data layout, accumulators, rounding and saturation, context save/restore, exceptions and virtualization, and compiler and ABI mapping. Source: RISC-V International interview

VII. Trend Observations

  • K3 became the week’s only systematic main thread: firmware (three versions in three days), kernel (the first 31 commits of ruyi/7.3.y), and images (kiwi-image automation) all moved within the same week, and the firmware has progressed from “link bring-up” to “selectable CPU modes + simulatable drivers.” The watch point is whether these capabilities can form a demonstrable complete-device form during the 10-18 summit week, and the measured data for the three CPU modes.
  • Matrix expansion shifted from “single-backend advancement” to parallel “dual-backend + specification + operator validation”: the three GCC ztt-dev batches (36 → 51 → 20 commits) have moved from feature rollout to correctness in coordination with other extensions (state preservation across RVV / Zfa / atomics); the LLVM-side ztt-dev first push fills in the other half, with documentation V0.6 and the AME-operator tutorial following up; meanwhile the upstream AME task group is advancing standardization under XuanTie’s leadership—the in-repo draft and the specification line are starting to stay in sync.
  • The cadence divergence between the two fork maintenance lines is widening: the PyTorch riscv branch maintains a 23-commit rebuild every morning with 8 PRs refreshed in sync; the LLVM riscv branch has not replayed for three consecutive nights since 10-12. The former indicates a high degree of automation in the upstream-following mechanism; the latter may mean the LLVM-side focus has shifted to the ruyisdk/llvm-project maintenance line (the XTHeadVector fix is a result of that line).
  • Buddy Compiler has become one of RuyiAI’s de facto upstreams: buddy-mlir completed the 0.1.0 release and riscv64 cross-compilation / CI / manylinux release-chain fixes within a week, while triton-riscv’s dependency upgrade and tilelang-riscv’s new CI both anchor on Buddy’s versioned artifacts—the chain “MLIR compilation framework → operator frontend → nightly artifacts” closed the loop on riscv64 to a releasable wheel for the first time this week.
  • Distro maintenance has entered a dual cadence of “batch processing + security batches”: openRuyi’s single-day 90 Go modules, 39 KDE packages, the full GnuPG stack, and five security packages in quick succession show that dependency following has been engineered; the implicit constraint is the coupling between full-rebuild cost and image release cadence, and the timing of 0.54.0 and the 2026.10 images is worth watching.
  • Watch list: 0.54.0 release at month-end and ABI compatibility checks; when ruyi/7.3.y enters the image channel; triton-riscv #85 review; the two LLVM FPGA PRs (#25 / #28); PyTorch #46 / #48 (RVV) and #51 (local image already merged into the branch); attached-matrix-extension documentation PR #1; whether any versions or results are released during the summit (10-18 ~ 10-20).
