1.1 llvm-project: riscv branch rebased for the third time on 10-25, baseline advanced by 474 commits (10-25)

Date: 2026-10-25　Sources: riscv branch commit list, new head 29edf281, head-to-head comparison of the two rounds

The riscv branch completed its third full-branch rewrite at 23:00 on 10-25, with the head moving from 58d62a47 to 29edf281; the head title remains “[RISCV] Add A100 HP/SP and pack/unpack lowering with calibrated scheduling (#31)”. Comparison against the previous head yields diverged: 474 commits ahead, 26 behind, 300 files differing. The 26 behind exactly equals the number of local patches; a patch-by-patch comparison confirms the titles and order of all 26 patches are unchanged — from #31’s A100 HP/SP and pack/unpack scheduling, through BOSC AME’s #14, #16, #18, #24, #29, the Buckyball vendor extensions #20 and #23, IME builtins to A100 dot #30, down to the CI and MLIR-JIT fixes in #11, #6, #4, #3, #1. The new baseline brings in two pieces of engineering infrastructure directly related to RISC-V: a new .github/workflows/riscv64-ci.yml (+121 lines) adding a native riscv64 CI job to the image repo; and on the BOLT side, a new RISCVLongJmpPass (+174 lines) plus a series of split-functions tests, handling branches and long jumps across fragments of split functions. The rebase span also returns from the previous round’s 275 commits to 474, close to the routine catch-up magnitude of the 1769-commit rebase on 10-21.

Assessment: the rebase cadence has stabilized from “as scheduled” to once every two or three days, but the zero change in the patch set shows that output during this period is concentrated on the upstream baseline side. Tracking this branch should rely on the set of patch titles, not on SHAs or commit counts.

1.2 llvm-project upstream: seven dense P-extension merges, spacemit-a100 tuning lands in mainline (10-23)

Date: 2026-10-23 to 10-25　Sources: Packed Widening MAC intrinsics a94e3ab3, mul/macc isel patterns bbbc1cb9, Packed Slide b0e82457, mulu isel matching 99e46680, spacemit-a100 scheduling preference fbdefa77

Of the 474 upstream commits brought in by the rebase, 14 are RISC-V related, 7 of which belong to the P (packed SIMD) extension — a higher density than in the previous several windows.

Intrinsics layer: Packed Widening Multiply Accumulate intrinsics merged at 00:47 on 10-24 (#226505); scalar mqwacc and mqrwacc intrinsics merged at 14:55 on 10-23 (#234560); packed byte horizontal multiply-accumulate intrinsics merged at 09:59 on 10-23 (#234484). Instruction selection layer: isel patterns added for mul.h01, mul.h11 and macc.h01, macc.h11 at 11:02 on 10-24 (#234976); isel matching added for mulu.h01, mulu.h11 at 21:34 on 10-24 (#235226); Packed Slide supported at 13:17 on 10-24 (#234629). Validation layer: immediate range checks added for sati and usati intrinsics at 23:32 on 10-23 (#234721).

The remaining RISC-V changes lean toward backend quality and machine model: hasMultipleConditionRegisters made to return true for vector registers at 10:58 on 10-25 (#234876); multiplication judged high-cost when the M extension is absent at 07:30 on 10-25 (#235363); jump table range checks preserved under cf-protection-branch at 00:11 on 10-25 (#234733); TunePreferAscendingLoadStore enabled for spacemit-a100 at 21:31 on 10-23 (#234532); BOLT support for cross-fragment branches of split functions at 11:03 on 10-23 (#223958).

Assessment: the P extension had previously been stuck at “compiles” — instructions and intrinsics merged piecemeal; this batch fills in isel patterns and immediate validation, pushing usability toward “compiles well and doesn’t miscompile”. The spacemit-a100 scheduling preference shows that domestic commercial cores have entered the machine-model tuning stage in LLVM, not just instruction addition.

III. Vector Extensions, Architecture Testing, and Operator Optimization

3.1 riscv-arch-test Migrates to the riscv Organization: 30 Commits, Suite Consolidation and H Extension Completion (10-25)

Date: 2026-10-23 to 10-25　Source: Commit list, PMP suite merge #2996, Sv suite merge #2981, hypervisor CSR and instruction suite #2982, Svinval H and SmH groups #2997

During this period’s review, we found that the original address riscv-arch-test/riscv-arch-test now returns 404, and the repository has moved to riscv/riscv-arch-test (latest push 10-25 18:56 UTC). The previous issue’s conclusion that “the latest commit stopped at 09-08” is therefore void—the migrated repository has 30 commits within the window, making it one of the most active upstreams in the entire window. The RuyiSDK support-matrix and the compliance judgments for various chips are all based on this test suite, so this signal deserves a separate note.

The content splits into two lines. The first is suite consolidation to reduce maintenance cost: merging the scattered PMP suites into one (#2996), merging the F, S, Sm, U, Zaamo, Zalrsc, Zc, and Zicbo groups of Exceptions into a unified Exceptions suite, merging Sv, SvSm, SvZicbo, and ExceptionsSv into a unified Sv suite (#2981), with the CI side simultaneously changed to one exclude_extensions entry per line (#3001). The second is filling gaps in privilege levels and virtualization: the H extension CSR and instruction suite (#2982), merging hypervisor M-mode tests into the Sm group (#2983), VS, VU, and VS-level interrupts in S and H modes (#2911), the H and SmH groups of Svinval (#2997), counter enable and htimedelta tests for H_Zicntr and Sm_Zicntr_H (#2998), six S-state constraints including Shcounterenw (#2987), four basic constraints Ziccif, Ziccrse, Ziccamoa, and Ziccamoc (#2939), plus a fix for the xepc[1] read mask when misa.C is 0 (#2994). The main authors are davidharrishmc and jordancarlin.

Assessment: Doing consolidation and completion at the same time shows that the goal of this reorganization is to first straighten out the structure, then fill content into that structure. The direction is consistent with the openRuyi side pushing RVA23 forward—both are filling in the server and virtualization line.

VI. Trend Watch

6.1 Two rebase lines pause together: one keeps its base, the other rebases without adding anything

The clearest signal this period is that “rebase” itself is carrying less information. The pytorch riscv branch has had zero commits for three days; the llvm riscv branch rebased on 10-25, but its 26 local patches are unchanged one by one—only the upstream baseline moved forward by 474 commits.

Together this shows that the maintenance mode of these two fork branches is “keep the baseline close to upstream,” not “keep adding features.” Real new features come from upstream merges (see 6.2); the local patch set has long stayed on the same set of vendor extensions.

The practical implication for readers: to track these two branches, look at the set of patch titles, not the SHA or the commit count. A rebase rewrites committer dates uniformly and inflates the commit count with upstream, but the patch content may not change for a week—citing any downstream by SHA will be stale the same day.

6.2 Upstream P extension moves from “compiles” to “compiles well”

In three days there were 14 upstream RISC-V-related commits, 7 of them for the P extension: three add builtins (multiply-add wide, scalar accumulate, byte horizontal multiply-add), three add instruction selection patterns (high/low nibble combinations of mul, macc, mulu and packed slide), and one adds immediate range checking for sati and usati.

These three layers correspond exactly to the sequence by which an extension goes from “has instructions” to “safe to use”: first builtins so the compiler can express it, then isel so code can be generated, and finally validation so bad input is rejected. Last issue’s judgment that the P extension “lacks application-side benchmarks” is unchanged this period, but the foundation has been filled in enough to start performance comparisons. The spacemit-a100 LoadStore scheduling preference and “treat multiplication as high-cost when there is no M extension” are machine-model calibrations in the same direction.

6.3 Test suites trade merging for maintenance cost

After riscv-arch-test migrated to the riscv org, there were 30 commits in three days, a substantial share of them merging scattered suites into one (PMP, Exceptions, Sv). Such changes do not increase test coverage; they reduce the maintenance surface: with fewer suites, CI configuration, exclusion lists, and report aggregation all get simpler.

The H extension tests added at the same time are a different matter—hypervisor-related CSR and interrupt semantics were previously thinly covered, and this period filled in Svinval, Shcounterenw, H_Zicntr, and others in one go. Doing merging and filling in at the same time shows that the goal of this reorganization is to straighten out the structure first and then fill content into it, rather than simply slimming down.

6.4 Distributions enter a pre-release quiet period

All 124 openRuyi commits are concentrated in the dozen or so hours of 10-23, followed by two days of zero commits; openRuyi 2026.10 has not been released, and the news page still shows the 2026.09 announcement from 9-30. On the ruyi side, 0.54.0 has only the 9-18 alpha tag, and the main branch has had no commits since 9-25.

Following the pattern of previous months, there is a “cleanup and freeze” quiet period before a monthly release: push metadata, dependencies, and security updates all at once, then cut the version. This window’s 102 metadata corrections and 16 Go dependency packages fall exactly in the cleanup phase of this pattern. What to watch next period is which comes out first, 2026.10 or 0.54.0.
