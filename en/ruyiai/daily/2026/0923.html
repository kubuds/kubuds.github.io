2.1 Compiler Tuning Tool RuyiTuner Implements Project-Scope Search: Aggregated-Fitness Genetic Algorithm Lands in v1.x Branch (09-22)

Date: 2026-09-22　Source: v1.x commit d2367ec0, CHANGELOG

At 15:59 Beijing time on 09-22, RuyiTuner, the automatic compiler tuning tool under the ruyisdk organization, merged a medium-sized commit into the v1.x development branch (+274/-154 lines, covering 6 files including CHANGELOG, Readme, RunCSiBE docs, and scripts/utils/GA.py): Implement project-scope search with aggregated-fitness GA and update docs, formally implementing project-scope search mode.

Per the CHANGELOG, this constitutes a 1.8 release change. There are three key points: First, utils/GA.py extracts a generic genetic algorithm search core _ga_search and adds a new project-level function LeverageSyner_GA_codesize_project—the fitness function changes from “single-file instruction count reduction ratio” to “file-size-weighted overall reduction rate (Σbaseline−Σoptimized)/Σbaseline,” searching for a single common optimal pass sequence for the entire project. Second, the single-file version function is correspondingly renamed to LeverageSyner_GA_codesize_file, matching the project version, with run.py calls updated accordingly (the --search_scope parameter was previously a placeholder marked “not yet implemented,” now completed in this cycle). Third, on engineering details, when a sequence fails to opt on individual files, it falls back to the original IR count, and a new “(file, sequence) → size” cache is added to reduce redundant opt runs. The CLI is indirectly connected as a result: ruyituner.py removes the placeholder notice, and project mode can now complete the full training-plus-optimization workflow.

RuyiTuner positions itself as an “LLVM tuning tool based on optimization synergy analysis”: it is not bound to a specific LLVM version or target architecture (determined by the target triple of the input IR, naturally covering both x86 and RISC-V), and through empirical measurement it mines synergy pairs where “the combined effect exceeds the sum of individual passes,” then uses a genetic algorithm to search within the space formed by synergy pairs for the optimization sequence with the best code size. The accompanying RunCSiBE.md documents the measured average optimization rates of nineteen CSiBE v2.1.1 benchmark projects on LLVM 22.1.0-rc2 x86 and RISC-V toolchains, such as 53.94% on the RISC-V side for the compiler project; this document now also adds usage examples for --search_scope. The repository is continuously maintained by community developers (established in late August, Apache-2.0), and this update is its first inclusion in the daily briefing within the ruyisdk organization.

V. Trend Observations

5.1 Compiler tuning moves from single-file to project-level: the roadmap signal from RuyiTuner v1.8

The project-level search (2.1) added to RuyiTuner in this issue is worth reading together with the previous two reports: 09-21 was the GCC side filling in -mcpu/-mtune for SpacemiT X100/A100 (the toolchain “recognizing the core”), 09-17 was the measured RVV kernel numbers for ONNX Runtime (operators “running faster”), and this issue hands “how to choose the optimization sequence” to a genetic algorithm for project-level automatic tuning (with gains “landing on whole-package size”). Together the three sketch out a complete chain on the compiler side of RuyiSDK: core recognition → operator optimization → sequence tuning. The project-level mode elevates the evaluation metric from single-file to whole-package weighted, directly matching the real demand for “whole-package size” in software distribution; the next thing worth tracking is whether it gets incorporated into the official RuyiSDK toolchain distribution, along with the expansion of the RISC-V dataset side (datasets/ll_files/riscv) and the pace of adaptation to new LLVM versions.

5.2 Releases and events enter a dense week: Yunqi Conference (through 09-24) overlapping with Biweekly Report 077 (09-24)

This window opens two outward-facing timelines at once: 09-22 saw the Yunqi Conference kick off, with the Ruyi community booth open through 09-24; the same evening, the 077th biweekly report completed integration, with release planned for 09-24. Stacked on top of the previous week’s content loop for the K3/CoM260 platform (report, examples, course) and the newly added P550 board report, RuyiSDK’s “external visibility” reaches a recent high over the three days from 09-22 to 09-24: offline there is a conference booth, online there are the biweekly report and support matrix updates. Attention naturally falls on whether the official 09-24 biweekly report announces a new ruyi version number (the state after the 0.54.0-alpha pre-release line) and on the follow-up outcomes of discussions at the Yunqi Conference.

5.3 Both ends of board validation: application-level P550 and MCU-level CH32V307 advance in parallel

The two reports in this issue’s support matrix cover the two endpoints respectively: SiFive Premier P550 (application-level, capable of running Fedora 44 Server) and CH32V307 (MCU-level, RT-Thread BSP 5.3.1). Combined with the multi-OS reports for K3/CoM260 and the synchronized completion of P550 documentation and examples, RuyiSDK’s board support matrix is rolling forward across the entire span from “application processor to MCU,” and the completeness of the supporting assets for each newly added board (system report, board documentation, example code, course entry point) is improving. For teams that use development boards as their delivery vehicle, this means reproducible materials beyond the toolchain are thickening.

5.4 Gaps and points of attention in this window

Gaps that must be recorded honestly: the PyTorch fork saw no functional-side progress whatsoever (both the riscv branch and the 8 open PRs went silent, and the two RVV patches continue to wait); triton-riscv, tilelang-riscv, and the vector-operator dedicated target repositories were all silent; ruyi and packages-index had no commits or releases; the openRuyi organization entered a quiet window; on the news side, multiple Google News queries in Chinese and English for RuyiAI, RuyiSDK, the Ruyi community, and openRuyi returned zero hits in a row, and there were no new arXiv entries within the window. Points of attention: first, follow-up developments at the Yunqi Conference booth on 09-23 to 09-24; second, the official release of Biweekly Progress No. 077 on 09-24 and whether it brings a ruyi version milestone; third, updates to the actual benchmark numbers for RuyiTuner’s project-level search and the possibility of its inclusion in the official toolchain; fourth, the openRuyi 2026.09 monthly release announcement.
