1.2 FlagGems examples land in three stacked PRs: 63 + 43 + 40 operators pending merge (11-19)

Source: PR #126. PR #127. PR #130.

FlagGems examples stacked into three branches within a single day. #126 covers 63 aten operators, #127 adds 43 torch.special operators, and #130 adds another 40 activation, dropout, and loss operators.

The three are stacked serially. #127 sits on top of #126, #126 on top of #120, and #130 on top of #127. Each PR description notes that while predecessors are unmerged, the diff counts predecessor commits as well.

Per their respective descriptions, the sizes relative to main are 239, 325, and 405 files. #130’s own portion is 4 commits and 81 files; the rest belongs to preceding PRs.

Test counts rise layer by layer. #126 reports 6416 passing, #127 reports 6782, and #130 reports 7091. Newly added cases are 309 and 366 respectively.

#127 covers special functions. The Bessel family, four kinds of Chebyshev plus shifted variants, two kinds of Hermite, Laguerre, and Legendre are all included, along with log_softmax and logsumexp.

Intrinsics that the toolchain cannot lower are replaced with closed forms. erfcx uses a three-segment hybrid, with an fp32 segment, an fp64 segment, and an asymptotic series each handling one range. The tail of log_ndtr is computed as −t² + log(erfcx(t)/2), avoiding ndtr underflow.

#130 leans toward neural-network operators. Backward formulas for GELU and GLU, fused conv/addmm/prelu, dropout and randomized-ReLU variants, and a loss group including CTC forward and backward.

CTC’s lattice dynamic programming uses an fp64 Triton kernel. The backward pass keeps the beta scan on the host side while sanitizing the incoming alpha. The author notes that aten’s CPU implementation leaves unreachable lattice cells uninitialized.

The cudnn epilogue of convolution is routed through aten. Triton keeps only the bias, Z addition, and ReLU. Triplet margin follows pairwise_distance semantics, with eps broadcast onto the difference vector.

All three PRs limit their scope to python/examples/flaggems. The descriptions state that the backend, CI, and other PRs’ files are untouched.

III. P Extension, Vector, and Runtime Optimization

3.1 GCC Delivers 3 P Extension Examples, 13 Commits Land in Mainline (11-19)

Source: Biweekly Report No. 081. GCC mainline commit fb66e0c.

The three P extension examples run directly on RV32 and RV64. The scenarios are image matching, Q15 fixed-point dot product, and Q15 digital filtering; the repository includes a prebuilt toolchain and two interactive tutorials.

In the image example, each packed instruction compares 4 pixels on RV32 and 8 on RV64. The program iterates over 289 candidate positions and recovers a displacement of 5 pixels right and 3 pixels up.

Dynamic instruction reductions are measured with QEMU at -O3 with unaligned memory access enabled. Image matching drops 75.7% on RV32 and 80.0% on RV64. Q15 dot product drops 70.7% and 74.9% respectively, and Q15 filtering drops 69.5% and 73.2%.

On the GCC side, 13 commits land in mainline. Eight improve bit manipulation and rotation, four support vector dot product, and one fixes a general loop optimization.

Gains on K1 hardware are concentrated in scalar bit manipulation. Setting, clearing, and flipping 32-bit masks all cut execution cycles by about 27%. The RV64 single-bit set function shrinks from 3 instructions to 2.

Other microbenchmarks range from positive to flat. Shift-add followed by rotate left or right saves about 19.8% of cycles; when followed by division or modulo, dynamic instructions drop 8.30% with cycles unchanged. Concatenating two 32-bit words on gem5 saves 32.77% of cycles, while SQLite varint decoding regresses by 0.63%.

The Zvdota and Zvbdota vector dot products have landed in mainline. C/C++ can call integer, BF16, and FP8 dot products plus batched FP32 dot product.

On the Binutils side, Shlcofideleg v2 was submitted. RISE’s pre-commit CI ran a manual dry run for the first time, with three configurations that automatically compare patch test results against the production baseline.

3.2 LLVM: 14 P Extension PRs, 13 Merged (11-19)

Source: Biweekly Report No. 081. LLVM PR #243813.

Fourteen P extension PRs were submitted this period, 13 of which are merged upstream. The remaining one is still in review.

The merged set has broad coverage. Legalization of widening shifts is restricted to left shifts, fixing the issue where right shifts were mis-selected as left-shift instructions.

New intrinsics include scalar saturating shift-add, scalar comparison, saturating and rounding shifts, high/low halfword multiply-accumulate, scalar zip/unzip, and narrowing clip.

There is also a batch of fixes. zext_invec gains a v8i8 to v2i32 match pattern, packed shifts return the original operand directly on zero, and RV32 i64 shift-amount legalization no longer fails an assertion.

Crashes in WADDA and WSUBA with a zero accumulator are also fixed. The cause was the zero accumulator being selected as X0_Pair; register pairs are now built with buildGPRPair.

On the GlobalISel side, KCFI type information is preserved. Indirect calls can therefore generate checks, matching SelectionDAG behavior.

Still in review is #243813. It folds constant shifts exceeding the element bit width per the P extension’s signed shift semantics, producing zero or an equivalent fill plus most-significant-bit extraction.

3.3 V8 on K3: JetStream 3 Total Score +3.5% (11-19)

Source: Biweekly Report No. 081. CL 8698757.

This period’s V8 gains come from a batch of performance patches. The biweekly report states that these patches were located with tools such as riscvlint, drafted with AI assistance and benchmark-validated, then merged after manual review by ISCAS maintainers.

The largest single item is short jumps after the trampoline pool. Previously 8- to 12-byte auipc plus jr, these now emit 4-byte jal, with a new far-jump pool ensuring targets are reachable.

On K3 hardware, JetStream 3 total score improves 3.5%. Of 75 items, 46 exceed noise; gaussian-blur alone improves 27%, and wasm items improve 17% to 22%. Generated code shrinks by 1.7 MB, about 2.2%.

Zfa’s fli also lands in TurboFan and Liftoff. Each floating-point constant goes from 3 instructions to a single fli.d, eliminating 248 occurrences in JetStream 3.

There are two more cleanups. PushAll and PopAll with empty register lists return early, removing about 90,000 useless instructions from Maglev. Int32 comparisons compare registers directly, cutting instruction count by a further 0.47%.

The built-in simulator also benefits. After IsShortInstruction is inlined into the header, richards drops from 28.9 s to 19.0 s, a 34% reduction; ML drops from 40.2 s to 27.3 s, a 32% reduction.

Caching of debug checks saves another tenth. Six checks are skipped when debugging is off, cutting richards by 27% and ML by 28%. After optimizing the decode path, richards drops a further 20% and ML a further 22%.

Thirteen patches were merged and 15 reviewed this period. Topics include heap number deduplication, sign extension of Liftoff stack arguments, modeling of fence and AMO memory ordering, and CAS address computation order.

IV. Buddy Compiler Updates

4.1 AME dialect replaced with ZTT v0.6, 138 instruction ops merged (11-19)

Source: buddy-mlir PR #1066.

#1066 was merged at 19:21, touching 30 files, +8349 −1277. It replaces the retired AME dialect wholesale with ZTT v0.6, keeping the ame namespace.

The new dialect defines 138 typed instruction ops. Each op comes with custom assembly syntax, an intrinsic bridge, and matrix/accumulator target extension types.

Lowering goes uniformly through lower-ame, landing on llvm.riscv.ztt.* calls. The intrinsic bridge retains a path that translates directly to LLVM IR.

Verification is two-layered. One set covers lowering, diagnostics, all intrinsics, mixed dialects, side effects, and backend selection. The other is two freestanding payloads run on AME_gem5 in atomic syscall emulation mode.

The 4×4 signed i32 payload passes bit-for-bit comparison against an independent scalar reference. The PR description states that the old ISA contract is replaced, with no promise of numerical behavior compatibility with the old implementation.

Documentation is not in this PR. README and standalone docs are left to a follow-up PR, and the BOSCAME and XTAME implementations are unchanged.

4.2 LLVM bumped to 163d6a7, XSAI’s ZAMES and ZMASYNC land in the backend (11-19)

Source: buddy-mlir PR #1067. llvm-project PR #33.

#1067 was merged at 19:20, changing only the LLVM submodule pointer, +1 −1. The old pointer was 542365c9cd38, the new one 163d6a753b58.

The new pointer corresponds to the merge commit of #33 in the llvm-project fork. That merge adds XSAI’s ZAMES matrix instructions and ZMASYNC synchronization instructions to the RISC-V backend.

Coverage includes matrix and sync intrinsics, sizeless target extension types, assembly and disassembly, and RV64 SelectionDAG lowering.

The boundaries are equally clear. Matrix copy, spill and reload, and cross-function ABI support are not yet supported.

4.3 EmbeddingGemma-300M speeds up 23–38× on K3 (11-20)

Source: buddy-mlir PR #1070.

#1070 was opened at 01:56 on 11-20, touching 19 files, +3072. It builds on #835, fixing model import, RAX packing, and tokenizer compatibility.

The optimizations are IME and RVV. A single forward pass on K3 is 23.24–38.24× faster than the F32 RAX baseline and 7.11–19.03× faster than native Hugging Face F32.

Memory usage drops in step. Peak process RSS is down 71.6%–93.5% versus the RAX baseline. All nine payloads and 81 formal runs pass the embedding accuracy threshold.

The import-side fix switches to a whole-graph approach. The bidirectional additive mask is constructed in the model wrapper layer and imported in one pass through pooling, two dense layers, and L2 normalization, replacing the old version’s textual rewriting of generated MLIR.

Batch shapes and sliding attention are also fixed. Import uses eager attention, passing the full mask and sliding mask separately, no longer assuming B1.

4.4 GPT-2 decode fix opened, polynomial-approximation math pass under review (11-19)

Source: buddy-mlir PR #1069. PR #1068. PR #1065.

#1069 was opened at 23:57, fixing a numerical error in GPT-2 decoding. The problem was that batched matmul with transposed B was treated by decode vectorization as a non-transposed layout.

The linalg layer has no separate transposed-B op. Both share linalg.batch_matmul and are distinguished only by indexing_maps. In the decode pipeline two passes run back to back, and the first vectorizes all batch_matmul ops under the default layout.

The fix adds a guard. Only default indexing_maps enter vectorization; transposed B is left to the dedicated pass that follows, and such ops are marked dynamically legal. 2 files changed, +127.

#1068 is the math approximation itself and is still under review. The new pass replaces vector math such as exp, tanh, and log with polynomials, keeping computation in vector registers instead of calling libm per lane.

Upstream MLIR’s approximations have edge-case problems on six ops. log and log2 clamp subnormal inputs to FLT_MIN, sin and cos single-constant reduction distorts on large arguments, and atan2’s quadrant determination is also off. This pass writes separate patterns for them.

sin and cos use three-stage Cody-Waite reduction, with arguments beyond 1e5 going through 192-bit Payne-Hanek. The branch is only taken when such lanes appear in the vector.

The loop tail is also changed. The avl loop runs the remainder in a single active-length iteration instead of splitting in half and adding a scalar loop.

#1065 was closed at 11:10 the same day without merging. It implemented cache-friendly tiling for linalg.transpose and was created on 11-18.

VI. Trend Observations

6.1 Matrix extensions connect from simulator to backend in one line

Four layers of activity appeared in the same window. The software sources picked up QEMU’s AME package, the LLVM backend merged XSAI’s ZAMES, Buddy switched to the ZTT v0.6 dialect, and the triton side opened RISC-V lowering tuning.

This is the first time in a single window that the simulator, compiler, dialect, and operator backend have all moved together. AME and XSAI are still two separate naming schemes, and downstream users will have to pick a side based on their scenario.

6.2 Software sources are filling out both the P and matrix extension chains

QEMU’s RVP package fills in the simulator link. GCC’s three examples and LLVM’s 13 PRs fill in the compiler link, and the examples repo fills in the hands-on path.

Both chains are still at draft v0.6. The toolchain and simulator will change as the draft is updated, so current results can only be treated as interim records.

6.3 The distro side is migrating to Go and OpenSSL 4

Of openRuyi’s 84 commits in one day, 61 are Go dependencies, and another group wires grpc to system OpenSSL 4. The opensc update is likewise a build fix for OpenSSL 4.

These two types of work do not appear in feature lists, but they determine whether upper-layer software can compile on the new baseline. The distro side’s focus is shifting from adding packages to swapping out the foundation.

Limitations

This issue’s window is 24 hours (11-19 04:30 to 11-20 04:30). The GitHub anonymous API used roughly 42 calls during collection; pytorch main’s commit count was sampled at the 100-commit cap and not classified commit by commit.

The biweekly report’s figures were measured and reported by the team itself and were not re-tested on riscv64 hardware for this issue. Reductions such as V8’s 3.5% and 27%, and GCC’s cycle savings, are recorded as stated in the biweekly report.

triton-riscv #129 depends on buddy-mlir #1068, which has not yet been merged. The 1–7 ULP accuracy figures in the text are recorded per the PR description; actual behavior awaits re-verification after #1068 is merged.

The QEMU AME and RVP packages correspond to draft v0.6. Instruction encodings and behavior may change with the draft; the 20261016 and 20261027 strings in package version numbers are build dates and do not indicate that the extensions are finalized.

openRuyi’s 84 commits are counted per the API response; the Go dependency packaging batch may extend into the next window. On the external news side, five Google News queries returned zero hits, though Chinese-language media may carry reports that are not indexed.
